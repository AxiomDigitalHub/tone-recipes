> **Start Here: The Tone in Six Blocks:**
> 1. Compressor: **Deluxe Comp**, always on, light
> 2. Boost: **Minotaur** (Klon-style), always on, low gain
> 3. Amp: **Essex A30**, Drive set just past clean
> 4. Cab: **2x12 Blue Bell**
> 5. Delay: **Adriatic Delay**, tempo 68 BPM, 331 ms (1/16 dotted) or 662 ms (1/8 dotted)
> 6. Reverb: **Plateaux**, long decay

*Updated 27 November 2026. An earlier version of this post gave the tempo as 136 BPM and described the chain as Nigel Hendroff's documented rig for the song. The tempo is 68 BPM, and no source we could find ties a rig to this recording. Both are corrected below.*

## Key, Tempo and Versions

| Recording | Key | BPM | Length | 1/8 dotted | 1/16 dotted |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hillsong Worship, *Let There Be Light* (2016) | D | 68 | 5:35 | 662 ms | 331 ms |
| Hillsong Worship, Radio Edit | D | 68 | 4:03 | 662 ms | 331 ms |
| Hillsong Worship, Orchestral Selah | D | 65 | 3:37 | 692 ms | 346 ms |
| Hillsong UNITED, *The People Tour: Live from Madison Square Garden* | C | 69 | 4:08 | 652 ms | 326 ms |

Keys, tempos and lengths are from MultiTracks; all four are in 4/4. WorshipTogether also gives D at 68, and Wikipedia describes the song as a slow ballad at 68 beats per minute in D major. Ben Fielding and Brooke Ligertwood wrote it (CCLI 7068424). PraiseCharts lists the original key as D with a "Med Slow" tempo, and a Gospel arrangement in Eb.

## 331 ms Is Right. It Is a Dotted Sixteenth.

The usual advice for this song is "dotted eighth, 331 ms, 136 BPM." We repeated it. The arithmetic is fine: 60,000 divided by 136, times 0.75, is 331 ms. The tempo is the problem. Every chart source lists the song at 68, and at 68 BPM a dotted eighth is 662 ms. The 331 ms figure is a dotted sixteenth.

This matters on a Helix for a practical reason. If your click, your tracks or your [MIDI clock](/blog/helix-midi-clock-playback-click-worship) are at 68 and the delay is set to a dotted-eighth note value, you get 662 ms, twice as long as the setting you meant to copy. If you tap 136 into a preset, every other tempo-synced block follows it at double speed.

Set the preset to 68 and pick the delay by what you are playing:

- **331 ms (1/16 dotted)** under steady eighth-note picking. Eighth notes at 68 BPM are 441 ms apart, so each repeat lands three-quarters of the way to your next note and makes the quick, galloping pattern people associate with the song.
- **662 ms (1/8 dotted)** under swells and held notes. Each repeat lands a dotted eighth later, between beats, and fills the space without adding a fast pattern.

Typing 331 ms as a fixed time works too, as long as nothing else in the preset depends on the tempo. The [tempo grid post](/blog/one-tempo-grid-predelay-dotted-eighth-delay) covers keeping delay and reverb on one grid.

## What Is and Isn't Documented About the Rig

The credits for *Let There Be Light* list electric guitar at album level: Michael Guy Chislett, Dylan Thomas, Jad Gillies, Jarryd Scully, Joel Hingston and Nigel Hendroff, who was also music director. There is no per-song credit, and the album was recorded live at Hillsong Conference in July 2016.

Hendroff's own gear is well covered in general terms, with dates that matter here:

- His signature Gretsch Penguin, with Broad'Tron BT-65 pickups, was announced in August 2022.
- The Jackson Audio Prism boost was announced in December 2016, five months after the recording. Hendroff has written that the Prism sits at the start of his pedalboard, without a date.
- In the same text he credits his English-made AC15 as the main contributor to his guitar tone on every Hillsong Worship album he has worked on since he got it in 2004.

None of those sources mentions this song. We found nothing for or against the "Klon" claim that our earlier version argued with, so that argument is gone. What follows is a chain that suits the song, built on the general shape of that rig: a bright guitar, a boost left on, a Vox-style amp and a long reverb.

## The Full Helix Chain

Key of D, 68 BPM.

| Position | Block | On/off | Key settings |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Volume Pedal (EXP 1) | On | Heel back for swelled entrances |
| 2 | Deluxe Comp | On | Threshold −30, Ratio 3, Mix 70% |
| 3 | Minotaur (Klon-style) | **On all song** | Gain 2.5, Tone 6, Level 6.5 |
| 4 | Essex A30 (amp) | On | Drive 5.5, Bass 4.0, Treble 7.0, Cut 4.5, Sag 5.5, Ch Vol to taste |
| 5 | 2x12 Blue Bell (cab) | On | Low cut 90 Hz, High cut 8.5 kHz |
| 6 | Adriatic Delay | On | Time 1/16 dotted (331 ms), Feedback 38%, Mix 30% |
| 7 | Plateaux (reverb) | On | Decay 7 s, Mix 35% |

The amp knobs are on the Helix 0 to 10 scale. The Essex A30 is the Helix model of a top-boost AC30; it has a Cut control in place of Presence and no Mid knob. Cut works in reverse, so raise it to darken the top end. An earlier version of this table named the amp "Essex A30 Fawn Brt," which is two different Helix models run together (Essex A30 and A30 Fawn Brt).

Drive at 5.5 is higher than in the cleaner worship presets on this site. The idea is an amp that is already close to breaking up, so that picking harder in the bridge adds drive without a pedal.

Given Hendroff's AC15 comment, the **Essex A15** is a reasonable swap. Start with the same Drive and adjust by ear; we have not verified its control set against the model list.

## The Boost Stays On

The Minotaur here is not a drive you switch on for a solo. Gain is at 2.5 and Level at 6.5, so it adds level into the amp and very little dirt of its own. It stays on in every snapshot. The amp's Drive setting assumes that push is there; with the boost bypassed, the amp sees a quieter signal and sits further from breakup, so the bridge will not lift the way the snapshots below expect.

Dynamics come from the guitar's volume knob and from how hard you pick.

## The Cab and the Guitar

This preset shares its cab with [Way Maker](/blog/way-maker-guitar-tone-helix) and sits opposite [Goodness of God](/blog/goodness-of-god-guitar-tone-helix): the **2x12 Blue Bell**, Helix's model of an AC30's Alnico Blue speakers, with the high cut left at 8.5 kHz. [Alnico Blue vs. Greenback in an AC30](/blog/alnico-blue-vs-greenback-ac30-worship) covers the published differences between the two speakers.

A bright guitar helps: a Telecaster, a semi-hollow with Filter'Tron-style pickups, or a Strat in the neck-and-middle position. With a hot bridge humbucker, move the cab's high cut up toward 9 kHz and bring the amp's Bass down to 3.5.

## Four Snapshots From the Song Map

MultiTracks maps the *Let There Be Light* recording as Verse 1, Chorus, Verse 2, Chorus, Tag, Instrumental (three times), Bridge (four times), Chorus, Bridge (twice), Chorus, Tag (twice), Ending. There is no pre-chorus. The middle of the song is nine consecutive passes of instrumental and bridge, all over the same chords (G, A, Bm, F#m, then G, A, Bm, A in WorshipTogether's chart), and that is where the build happens.

| Snapshot | Where | Delay | Reverb mix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1: Bed | Verses, first chorus, ending | 662 ms (1/8 dotted), Mix 30%, Feedback 38% | 35% |
| 2: Chorus | Second chorus, first tag, final tags | 331 ms (1/16 dotted), Mix 28%, Feedback 35% | 32% |
| 3: Build | Three instrumentals, bridge passes 1 and 2 | 331 ms, Mix 30%, Feedback 40% | 40% |
| 4: Top | Bridge passes 3 to 6 and the choruses between and after them | 331 ms, Mix 32%, Feedback 44%, output +1 dB | 44% |

- **Snapshot 1.** Volume pedal back, swelled entrances, one note or two-note shape per chord. The longer delay suits how little you are playing.
- **Snapshot 2.** Fuller picking, still light. The delay moves to 331 ms as the eighth notes start.
- **Snapshot 3.** Start the instrumental quietly and add a little on each of the three passes, so there is still room when the bridge begins.
- **Snapshot 4.** Pick harder and let the amp break up. If you play a lead line over the later bridge passes, this is the snapshot for it.

Turn **trails on** so tails carry across snapshot changes. The Radio Edit's map is different (four verses, an interlude in place of the instrumentals, four bridge passes), but the same four snapshots cover it. The [four-snapshot Sunday morning system](/blog/helix-worship-snapshots-sunday-morning) generalizes this layout for a whole service.

## Other Platforms

- **HX Stomp:** Deluxe Comp, Minotaur, Essex A30, 2x12 Blue Bell, Adriatic Delay and Plateaux make six blocks. Use three snapshots (Bed, Build, Top) and cover the chorus by easing your guitar's volume up from the Bed snapshot.
- **Quad Cortex:** an AC30 or AC15 capture set just past clean, a Klon-type boost in front at low gain, an analog-voiced delay and a long plate or hall. Set the tempo to 68 and use the same two note values across four scenes.
- **Pedalboard:** leave a clean boost on into a Vox-style amp or amp pedal. Tap quarter notes at 68. If your delay offers only a dotted-eighth subdivision, tap at 136 to get 331 ms and at 68 to get 662 ms. The [worship pedalboard guide](/blog/worship-pedalboard-guide) covers the rest of the chain.
