> 1. Amp: **Essex A30 TB** (Vox AC30 Top Boost), low gain, pushed by the master
> 2. Drive: **Minotaur** (Klon-style), always on at low drive for shimmer and sustain
> 3. Delay 1: **Transistor Tape**, dotted eighth at low mix for rhythmic texture

## Why the Helix Works for Worship Guitar

Worship guitar demands three things simultaneously: pristine cleans that sparkle, smooth drive that sustains without harshness, and ambient effects deep enough to fill the room without drowning the vocal. Doing this with pedals requires a large board, careful gain staging, and prayer that the tap tempo syncs at the right moment.

The [Helix](/platforms/helix) handles all of this in a single preset with snapshots. One footswitch press takes you from a shimmering clean arpeggio to a driven lead with different delay and reverb settings. No tap dancing, no volume drops, no gaps. Trails carry across snapshots, so your reverb tail from the clean section bleeds naturally into the next dynamic.

This walkthrough builds a complete worship preset from an empty patch. Every block name and setting is exact; you can replicate this on a Helix Floor, Helix LT, HX Stomp, or HX Stomp XL. The HX Stomp's 8-block limit means you'll need to make some tradeoffs (covered at the end).

## The Signal Chain

Here's the full chain in order, left to right on Path 1:

**Input → Compressor → Drive → Amp+Cab → Delay 1 → Delay 2 → Reverb → Output**

Seven blocks total. That fits comfortably on any Helix product, including the HX Stomp with one block to spare.

---

## Block 1: Compressor (LA Studio Comp)

Worship playing involves a lot of dynamic range: soft arpeggios that need to be heard, swells that build gradually, and lead lines that should sustain. A compressor at the front of the chain evens this out without squashing your expression.

| Parameter | Setting | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Block | LA Studio Comp | Smooth, optical-style compression |
| Peak Reduction | 3 | Gentle, not peak-limiting |
| Gain | Unity (adjust by ear) | Match compressed level to uncompressed |
| Mix | 70% | Parallel blend; keeps transient attack natural |

The Mix control is key. At 100%, the compressor handles everything. At 70%, your pick attack comes through naturally while the sustain tail gets lifted. This is the difference between worship compression, where you only notice it when it is switched off, and country compression, where the leveling is part of the sound.

---

## Block 2: Drive (Minotaur)

The Minotaur is Helix's Klon Centaur model. In worship, it stays on most of the time as a light always-on drive, adding harmonic shimmer and sustain without pushing into obvious distortion.

| Parameter | Setting | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Gain | 2.5 | Barely clipping; the amp does the heavy lifting |
| Treble | 5.5 | Slight sparkle without harshness |
| Level | 6.5 | Pushing the amp just past its clean threshold |

For the lead snapshot, bump the Gain to 4.5 and the Level to 7.5. This pushes the AC30 model into a singing, sustained drive without getting fuzzy. The Klon circuit's midrange character helps leads cut through the mix. That's the same reason it's been a worship board staple for years. The [Klon settings guide](/blog/klon-centaur-settings-guide) covers the three standard use cases in detail.

## Block 3: Amp+Cab (Essex A30 TB)

The Vox AC30 Top Boost is one of the most common amps in modern worship guitar. Its chimey high end, busy midrange, and the way it compresses when the master is pushed make it a forgiving foundation for clean-to-edge-of-breakup parts.

On the Helix, the model is called **Essex A30 TB**.

| Parameter | Setting | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Drive | 3.5 | Clean with harmonic complexity, not sterile |
| Bass | 4 | Controlled low end; too much gets muddy with delays |
| Mid | 6 | Presence and body |
| Treble | 6 | The AC30 chime |
| Master | 7 | Push the power section for natural compression |
| Presence | 5 | Top-end air |

### Cabinet Choice

Use the **2x12 Blue Bell** cab, Line 6's model of a 2x12 AC30 cabinet with Celestion Blue speakers, added in firmware 3.50. On older firmware, the **1x12 US Deluxe** or a third-party Celestion Blue IR is the substitute. The Blue cab is the matched pairing for the Essex A30 TB, and its upper-mid chime is the part of the sound that lets a quiet arpeggio cut through a full band without being turned up.

If the top end feels harsh through your FRFR or in-ears, apply a high cut on the cab block at **8kHz**. This removes fizzy frequencies that real speakers naturally roll off. More on this in the [fizzy modeler fix guide](/blog/why-modeler-tone-sounds-fizzy).

## Block 4: Delay 1, Transistor Tape (Dotted Eighth)

The dotted eighth delay is the rhythmic backbone of modern worship guitar. It turns simple eighth-note strumming or arpeggios into a flowing, rhythmic pattern that fills the sonic space between vocal phrases.

The **Transistor Tape** is Helix's Maestro Echoplex model. Its repeats come back slightly saturated and darker than the dry note, which helps them sit behind the dry signal rather than competing with it.

| Parameter | Setting | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Time | Dotted 8th (sync to tap tempo) | The defining rhythmic pattern |
| Feedback | 30% | 3-4 repeats; enough pattern, not runaway |
| Mix | 25% | Present but not dominant |
| Wow/Flutter | 2 | Subtle tape warble; adds organic movement |
| Bass | 4 | Roll off low end on repeats to prevent mud |
| Treble | 5 | Repeats darker than the dry note, so they don't compete with it |

Store each song's tempo in its preset rather than tapping it in before the song. If your worship leader uses a click, sync the Helix to MIDI clock instead; our [MIDI clock guide](/blog/helix-midi-clock-playback-click-worship) covers Playback and Ableton. If tempo does change while repeats are ringing, the [Tap Tempo Pitch setting](/blog/helix-tap-tempo-pitch-accurate-vs-transparent) decides whether those repeats bend.

### Is the Song in 6/8? Change the Note Value

The dotted eighth is a 4/4 setting. A lot of the worship catalogue is in 6/8, and there the dotted eighth is one and a half eighth notes, so the repeat lands halfway between two notes and sounds like the guitar is dragging. For a 6/8 song, use **1/8** (the song's eighth note) under strummed parts and **1/4 dotted** (the beat the room sways to) under swells, with the chart's BPM stored in the preset. MultiTracks counts 6/8 tempos in pairs of eighth notes, so those two note values come out right without any conversion.

Here are the songs we've built so far, with the delay that lands for each:

| Song | Key | BPM | Meter | Main delay | Build |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| *Praise* (Elevation Worship) | A | 127 | 4/4 | 1/8 dotted, 354 ms | [Praise on Helix](/blog/praise-elevation-worship-guitar-tone-helix) |
| *Gratitude* (Brandon Lake) | B | 78 | 6/8 | 1/8, 385 ms | [Gratitude on Helix](/blog/gratitude-brandon-lake-guitar-tone-helix) |
| *Trust in God* (Elevation Worship) | C | 74 | 6/8 | 1/8, 405 ms | [Trust in God on Helix](/blog/trust-in-god-elevation-worship-guitar-tone-helix) |
| *Firm Foundation (He Won't)* (Cody Carnes) | Bb | 75 | 6/8 | 1/8, 400 ms | [Firm Foundation on Helix](/blog/firm-foundation-cody-carnes-guitar-tone-helix) |
| *Great Are You Lord* (All Sons & Daughters) | A | 72 | 6/8 | 1/8, 417 ms | [Great Are You Lord on Helix](/blog/great-are-you-lord-all-sons-daughters-guitar-tone-helix) |

Keys and tempos are the MultiTracks listings for the original recordings; live and medley versions often differ, so check the version your team is learning. Four of five songs on that list would be wrong with this preset's default dotted eighth, which is the strongest argument for setting delays per song instead of once per preset.

For deeper exploration of delay types and the dotted-eighth technique, see [The Edge's delay settings guide](/blog/the-edge-delay-settings). The same rhythmic approach applied differently.

## Block 5: Delay 2, Elephant Man (Ambient Swells)

The second delay handles ambient textures: long, modulated trails for swells, pad-like backgrounds, and transitions between song sections.

The **Elephant Man** is Helix's Electro-Harmonix Deluxe Memory Man model. Its analog-voiced modulation adds chorus-like movement to the repeats.

| Parameter | Setting | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Time | 600-800ms | Long enough to create space, not rhythmic |
| Feedback | 40% | Extended trail that decays gradually |
| Mix | 15% (clean), 30% (ambient snapshot) | Low in the mix normally, raised for transitions |
| Depth | 4 | Chorus-like modulation on repeats |
| Speed | 2 | Slow modulation; shimmer, not seasick |

This delay is the "secret weapon" block. During verses, keep the mix low so it adds depth without being obvious. During transitions and ambient sections, raise the mix via snapshot to 30-35% and the space opens up dramatically.

## Block 6: Reverb (Searchlights)

The **Searchlights** reverb is a Line 6 original ambient reverb with modulation built in; Line 6 forum users compare it to the Cloud setting on a Strymon BigSky. It creates a large, diffuse reverb field that's the foundation of the ambient worship sound.

| Parameter | Setting | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Decay | 4.5s | Long tail; fills the room |
| Pre-delay | 60ms | Keeps the attack clear; reverb blooms after the note |
| Mix | 25% (normal), 40% (ambient snapshot) | Adjust per dynamic section |
| Low Cut | 120Hz | Prevents low-end reverb mud |
| High Cut | 6kHz | Softens the reverb tail; no harshness |

The low cut and high cut on the reverb block are critical. Without them, the reverb accumulates bass energy and high-frequency sizzle that makes the mix soupy. Cut below 120Hz and above 6kHz and the reverb sits in its own frequency pocket.

For a deeper understanding of reverb types and when to use each, the [reverb types guide](/blog/reverb-types-guide) covers hall, plate, spring, and shimmer algorithms. To stack a pitched-octave pad on top of this Searchlights foundation — the rising, choir-like texture under a lot of worship choruses — see the [shimmer reverb settings guide](/blog/shimmer-reverb-guitar-settings-helix). Keep the shimmer mix well under this reverb's, or the two tails will fight.

## Snapshot Assignments

Here's where the Helix earns its place on the worship board. Set up four snapshots for the four dynamics of a typical worship set:

| Snapshot | Compressor | Drive Gain | Amp Drive | Delay 1 Mix | Delay 2 Mix | Reverb Mix | Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1: Sunday Clean | On | 2.5 | 3.5 | 25% | 15% | 25% | Verses, soft arpeggios |
| 2: Building | On | 3 | 4 | 25% | 20% | 30% | Pre-chorus, building sections |
| 3: Full Worship | On | 4.5 | 4 | 30% | 25% | 35% | Choruses, full band |
| 4: Ambient Swell | On | 2.5 | 3 | 20% | 35% | 45% | Transitions, prayer moments |

Trails stay on across snapshots, meaning the reverb and delay tails from your Clean snapshot carry naturally into the Building snapshot. No gaps, no cuts, no unnatural silence.

To set up snapshots: press the Snapshot button, select Snapshot 1, set all your blocks as shown, then move to Snapshot 2 and adjust. The Helix saves per-snapshot parameter values automatically.

## Adapting for HX Stomp (8-Block Limit)

The HX Stomp's 8-block limit means you have one block to spare with this preset. If you need to cut a block, drop the compressor first; you can manage dynamics with your picking hand. If you need the compressor, consider using the Searchlights reverb's built-in modulation instead of the second delay, consolidating two blocks into one.

The HX Stomp supports 3 snapshots instead of 8, so use: Clean, Full, and Ambient as your three.
