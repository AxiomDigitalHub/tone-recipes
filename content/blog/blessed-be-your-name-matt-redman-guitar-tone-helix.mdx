*Blessed Be Your Name* moves faster than most of the slow builds a worship set leans on. MultiTracks lists its recordings between 114 and 120 BPM, and PraiseCharts files the 2002 original under "Rock" and "Med Fast." That changes the electric's job. Instead of long swells under a held chord, the guitar carries rhythm: a driving strum in the choruses, a repeating figure through the vamps and interludes, and a bridge that builds over three passes. The tone needs some grit, a delay short enough to stay out of the strumming's way, and a capo plan, because the key moves more between versions than the tempo does.

| Recording | Key | BPM | Length | Dotted 1/8 | 1/4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Matt Redman, *Where Angels Fear to Tread* (2002) | B (PraiseCharts) | not published | 5:06 | — | — |
| Tree63, *The Answer to the Question* | Db (PraiseCharts) | not published | — | — | — |
| The Worship Initiative, *Vol. 3* (2014) | B | 114 | 4:23 | 395 ms | 526 ms |
| Matt Redman, *Let There Be Wonder (Acoustic)* (2020) | A | 116 | 3:53 | 388 ms | 517 ms |
| Matt Redman, *Coming Back to the Heart* (live, 2024) | Bb | 116 | 4:48 | 388 ms | 517 ms |
| Maranatha! Music, *Top 25 Praise Songs 2012* | B | 120 | 5:12 | 375 ms | 500 ms |

Keys and BPMs for the last four rows are from MultiTracks; all are in 4/4. Matt and Beth Redman wrote the song (CCLI 3798438, Thankyou Music). WorshipTogether lists its original key as A at 120 BPM, which likely reflects the 2004 live recording rather than the B-major studio original, so if your chart says A, that's why. Tree63's 2003 single took the song to No. 2 on Billboard's Hot Christian Songs, and plenty of teams still learned it from that cover.

## One Set of Shapes, Five Capo Positions

The key spread looks like a problem until you notice the chords are the same four in every version. Play them as open A-shape voicings and move the capo:

| Key | Capo | Used by |
|---|---|---|
| A | none | Redman acoustic (2020), WorshipTogether chart |
| Bb | 1 | Redman live (2024) |
| B | 2 | 2002 original, Worship Initiative, Maranatha |
| C | 3 | MultiTracks *He Reigns* recording |
| Db | 4 | Tree63 |

PraiseCharts charts the 2024 live interlude in Bb as Bbmaj7, F4, Gm7 and Ebsus2+4. With capo 1 and A shapes, that's:

- **Amaj7:** x02120
- **E4 (Esus4):** 022200
- **F#m7:** 2x2220
- **Dsus2+4:** x00030

The open high E string rings through all four, and the open A string through the first and last, which is what gives the interlude its shimmer without a pad: one note holds still while the chords move under it, so the figure sounds bigger than four chords should. In B with capo 2, the same shapes give Bmaj7, F#4, G#m7 and Esus2+4.

## Where the Electric Actually Spends Its Time

I expected a verse-chorus rock song with a short bridge, and for the 2002 original, PraiseCharts' section list (verse, pre-chorus, chorus, verse 2, bridge) fits that. The 2024 live map is different. MultiTracks lays it out as Verse 1, Pre, Chorus, Verse 2, Pre, Chorus, Vamp, Interlude ×2, Bridge ×3, Vamp, Chorus ×2, Vamp, Interlude ×2, Chorus, Ending. Count the instances: two verses against three vamps, four interludes and three bridge passes. In under five minutes, the electric plays the interlude figure more often than it plays verse rhythm.

That's a reason to build the preset around the interlude rather than the verse. The verse can be simple. The interlude sound is the one people will hear most and remember.

## The Helix Preset

Set the preset tempo to your version (116 for either Redman recording) or let the click drive it over [MIDI clock](/blog/helix-midi-clock-playback-click-worship). Delay times are note values, so the same preset covers 114 to 120 BPM.

| Block | Settings | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Deluxe Comp** | Threshold −22 dB, Ratio 3:1, Mix 50% | Evens out the interlude's picked figure; light enough not to squash the strum |
| **Kinky Boost** | Gain low, Level about +3 dB over bypass; on from chorus 1 | A clean push into the amp for the choruses |
| **Scream 808** | Gain 3, Tone 5.5, Level 6; off until the bridge's second pass | The extra grit for the build |
| **Matchstick Ch1** | Drive 4, Bass 4.5, Mid 6, Treble 5.5, Ch Vol to taste | Edge of breakup with clear note separation, so open strings stay distinct when the drive is on |
| **2x12 Match H30** | Low Cut 90 Hz, High Cut 7 kHz | Same family as the amp; the high cut keeps the strum from fizzing in in-ears |
| **Transistor Tape** | Time 1/8 dotted, Feedback 25%, Mix 18% | Moves under the strum without filling the gaps between strokes |
| **Plateaux** | Decay 1.8 s, Mix 18%, Low Cut 200 Hz | Short enough that eighth-note strums at 116 BPM stay separate |

At 116 BPM an eighth note lasts 259 ms. Long repeats or a 4-second hall turn a strummed chorus into one gray block, so the delay and reverb on this song sit lower than on a slow ballad. More wash belongs in the bridge, and only for the passes where the band drops back.

## Four Snapshots for the 2024 Live Arrangement

| Snapshot | Where | Changes |
|---|---|---|
| 1: Verse | Verses, pre-choruses | Kinky Boost off, Scream 808 off, delay 15%, Plateaux 15% |
| 2: Chorus | All choruses | Kinky Boost on, delay 18%, Plateaux 18% |
| 3: Vamp / Interlude | Vamps and interludes | Kinky Boost on, delay 25% with Feedback 35%, Plateaux 25% |
| 4: Bridge build | Bridge passes 2 and 3 | Kinky Boost and Scream 808 on, delay 22%, Plateaux 22% |

Play the bridge's first pass on snapshot 1 with the guitar volume rolled back to about 7, then step to snapshot 4 for the second pass and roll the volume back up for the third. Three passes need three levels, and two of them can come from your hands. For the 2002 original or a shorter four-verse chart (Tree63 and The Worship Initiative both label four verses), snapshots 1, 2 and 4 cover the song and 3 is optional.

## Other Platforms

- **HX Stomp:** drop the Kinky Boost and use the amp's Ch Vol plus a snapshot for the chorus lift. Comp, Scream 808, amp, cab, Transistor Tape and Plateaux are six blocks.
- **Quad Cortex:** an edge-of-breakup Matchless or AC-style capture with a tube-screamer block in front, a tempo-synced tape delay at a dotted eighth and a short plate. If you play the Redman live version one week and Tree63's the next, save a preset per tempo, or set the tempo to Global and change it from the click.
- **Pedalboard:** a low-gain boost always on from the chorus, a mid-hump overdrive for the bridge, and a delay tapped at the song's quarter note with the dotted-eighth division. Any delay covers this: 395 ms is the longest dotted eighth in the table.
