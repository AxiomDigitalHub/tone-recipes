*Great Are You Lord* is one of the easiest songs in the 6/8 worship catalogue to play and one of the easiest to get slightly wrong on a modeler, because the version on your team's setlist probably isn't the one the chart was written from. MultiTracks lists the All Sons & Daughters original, from *All Sons & Daughters Live* (2013, Integrity Music), in **A at 72 BPM in 6/8**, 4:55 long, written by David Leonard, Leslie Jordan and Jason Ingram. The recordings most teams actually learn it from run between 74 and 78.

> **Start Here: Great Are You Lord on Helix**
> 1. Tempo: **store the number for your version** (72 for the original; see the table below). Set every delay as a note value.
> 2. Eighth-note delay: **Simple Delay, 1/8** (417 ms at 72), Feedback 25%, Mix 12%.
> 3. Swell delay: **Transistor Tape, 1/4 dotted** (1,250 ms at 72), Feedback 35%, Mix 20%.
> 4. Reverb: **Glitz**, Decay about 4 seconds, Mix 25%, Low Cut 150 Hz.
> 5. Snapshots: one per bridge pass, three in a row.

## What Tempo Is Great Are You Lord?

The number depends on which recording you mean. All four are 6/8, and MultiTracks counts 6/8 tempos in quarter notes, meaning pairs of eighth notes, so the eighth note is 30,000 ÷ BPM milliseconds and the beat you sway to is three eighths.

| Recording | Key | BPM (MultiTracks) | Length | Eighth note (1/8) | Felt beat (1/4 dotted) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| All Sons & Daughters, *All Sons & Daughters Live* (2013) | A | 72 | 4:55 | 417 ms | 1,250 ms |
| Passion, *Whole Heart* (2018) | A | 74 | 8:26 | 405 ms | 1,216 ms |
| Kari Jobe & Cody Carnes, medley with *Firm Foundation*, *Live From Europe* (2024) | A | 75 | 8:24 | 400 ms | 1,200 ms |
| Gateway Worship, *Gratitude / Great Are You Lord* medley, *Church Moments* | B | 78 | 5:19 | 385 ms | 1,154 ms |

Integrity Music's own listing gives **144 BPM** in A and 6/8. That's the original counted in eighth notes (72 × 2), not a different tempo; our [Firm Foundation build](/blog/firm-foundation-cody-carnes-guitar-tone-helix) explains how to read the two conventions.

I assumed the medley versions would sit at the original's tempo, since the song's feel is so tied to that slow sway. They don't. Every later recording in the table is faster, and the Gateway medley is 8 percent faster and a whole step higher, because it runs at *Gratitude*'s tempo and key.

## What Happens If the Preset Is Stored at 72?

If your delays are note values, nothing bad: change the stored tempo and every delay follows. The problem is a delay typed in milliseconds, or a preset built for the original and used unchanged for a medley. Each repeat is late by the difference, and the error adds up with every repeat:

| Playing this version with a 72 BPM delay in ms | Per repeat (eighth, 417 ms) | By repeat 3 | Per repeat (quarter, 833 ms) | By repeat 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Passion (74) | 11 ms late | 34 ms | 23 ms | 68 ms |
| Kari Jobe & Cody Carnes medley (75) | 17 ms late | 50 ms | 33 ms | 100 ms |
| Gateway medley (78) | 32 ms late | 96 ms | 64 ms | 192 ms |

At 75 BPM a sixteenth note in 6/8 is 200 ms, so by the third quarter-note repeat in the Kari Jobe medley the echo is half a sixteenth behind the band. It won't sound like a delay effect. It'll sound like the guitarist is dragging. Our post on [why delays drift from the tap light](/blog/delay-drifts-from-tap-light-long-repeats) works through how per-repeat errors accumulate.

If you play the Kari Jobe medley, the good news is that *Firm Foundation* and *Great Are You Lord* share 75 BPM, so one stored tempo covers both songs and nothing changes at the transition. The Gateway medley is the same: one tempo, 78, for both halves, and our [Gratitude build](/blog/gratitude-brandon-lake-guitar-tone-helix) has the delay values for it.

## The Helix Preset

The All Sons & Daughters recording leaves a lot of room around the vocal, and the electric's job is to widen the room without filling it. One path, seven blocks:

**Input → LA Studio Comp → Minotaur → US Deluxe Nrm → 1x12 US Deluxe → Simple Delay → Transistor Tape → Glitz → Output**

| Block | Settings | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **LA Studio Comp** | Peak Reduction 3, Mix 60% | Lifts quiet arpeggios in the verses without flattening swells |
| **Minotaur** | Gain 2, Treble 5, Level 6.5; off in verses | A small push for the second and third bridge passes and the last choruses |
| **US Deluxe Nrm** | Drive 4, Bass 4, Mid 6, Treble 5.5, Ch Vol to taste | Clean with a little edge when the Minotaur is on |
| **1x12 US Deluxe** | Low Cut 90 Hz, High Cut 7 kHz | Keeps the bottom clear of the bass and the top out of the in-ears |
| **Simple Delay** | Time 1/8, Feedback 25%, Mix 12% | Doubles the strummed eighths quietly, the pulse of the song |
| **Transistor Tape** | Time 1/4 dotted, Feedback 35%, Mix 20% | One echo per sway under swells and held chords |
| **Glitz** | Decay about 4 s, Pre-delay 40 ms, Mix 25%, Low Cut 150 Hz | About three felt beats of tail at 72, so a held chord fades before the next phrase arrives |

If you'd rather use the AC30-style build from our [worship Helix walkthrough](/blog/worship-guitar-tone-helix), swap the amp and cab for **Essex A30 TB** and **2x12 Blue Bell**; the delay and snapshot settings below don't change.

## Snapshots for Three Bridges in a Row

The original song map on MultiTracks reads: Intro, Verse 1, Pre Chorus, Chorus, Interlude, Verse 2, Pre Chorus, Chorus, Chorus, Breakdown, **Bridge, Bridge, Bridge**, Chorus, Chorus, Outro, Outro, Ending. The three bridge passes are the song's build, and if all three sound alike on guitar, the drummer is doing the building alone.

| Snapshot | Where | Changes |
|---|---|---|
| 1: Verse | Intro, verses, pre-choruses | Minotaur off, Simple Delay mix 10%, Transistor Tape 15%, Glitz 25% |
| 2: Chorus | Choruses, interlude | Simple Delay 12%, Transistor Tape 20% |
| 3: Breakdown / Bridge 1 | Breakdown and first bridge | Volume swells, Simple Delay off, Transistor Tape 30%, Glitz 35% |
| 4: Bridge 2 | Second bridge | Simple Delay back on at 12%, Minotaur on |
| 5: Bridge 3 and final choruses | Third bridge to the end | Minotaur Gain 3.5, Output +1.5 dB, Glitz back to 25% so the drive stays clear |

On an HX Stomp, which has three snapshots, merge 1 and 2 into one, make 3 the breakdown and first bridge, and put the drive change on a footswitch for bridges two and three.

The Passion version moves the sections around: one bridge, a chorus, a pre-chorus, then a second bridge into the ending. The same snapshots work; you'll just use Bridge 1 and Bridge 3 and skip Bridge 2.

## Capo and Key

Three recordings are in A. Open A shapes leave the high E string ringing over the A chord, which suits swells. Capo 2 with G shapes is the other common choice and works just as well. For the Gateway medley in B, capo 2 with A shapes keeps exactly the same fingerings as the original, so a team that plays both versions only moves the capo.

## Other Platforms

**HX Stomp:** the preset fits as written, seven blocks. See the snapshot note above.

**Quad Cortex:** the same chain with a compressor, a Klon-style drive, a Deluxe-style clean amp, two delays synced to tempo, and a hall or plate reverb. Use Scenes for the five snapshots. Set the QC to Preset tempo mode so each song keeps its own BPM.

**Pedalboard:** most digital delays let you type a time directly. For the original, store 417 ms and 1,250 ms rather than tapping; for a medley, store that version's numbers from the first table. Most tap-tempo pedals read each tap as a quarter note, so tapping the sway makes every subdivision wrong by half again in 6/8. A low-gain Klon-style drive into a clean amp with a little hair covers the bridges.

## Before Sunday

Ask which recording the team is learning from, then set the stored tempo to that number. Everything else in this build follows from it.
