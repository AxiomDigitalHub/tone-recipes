Pull the back panel off three blackface Deluxe Reverbs built within a couple of years of each other and you may find three different speakers looking back at you — an Oxford with a 465 stamped on the frame, a Jensen C12N, a Jensen P12Q — every one of them original, every one of them installed at Fullerton, and not one of them a mistake. Fender, in those years, bought speakers the way a busy kitchen buys onions, from whoever had them that week at a decent price, and the Deluxe Reverb is the model where that habit matters most, because a 1x12 combo has nowhere to hide its speaker.

That is worth knowing before you spend money chasing "the Deluxe sound," because the phrase quietly assumes there was one.

## What the Record Actually Shows

The difficulty with this subject is that Fender left no document — none that has surfaced, anyway — saying which speakers went into which amps and why. What we have instead is the amps themselves, as they pass through the hands of dealers who photograph the codes. That is a reasonable record, so long as you remember it is a record of survivors.

Here is what is documented, graded honestly:

| Amp | Speakers documented in original blackface examples | Strength of the record |
|---|---|---|
| Deluxe Reverb (1x12) | Oxford 12K5 and 12K5-6; Jensen C12N; Jensen P12Q | Dealer listings with frame codes — good |
| Twin Reverb (2x12) | Jensen C12N; Oxford 12T6; JBL D120F as an extra-cost option | Dealer listings, and JBL's own corporate history of its Fender F-series |
| Princeton Reverb (1x10) | Oxford 10J4; Jensen C10R | One dealer listing for the Oxford; the rest is reference-site tier |
| Super Reverb (4x10) | Jensen C10R; CTS; Oxford 10L6; JBL D110F as an option | Reference-site tier, plus JBL's history for the D110F |

A 1967 Deluxe Reverb sold by Retrofret, for instance, carries what the listing calls an original gold-label Jensen C12N stamped 220-651. A 1965 Deluxe sold by another established dealer carries an Oxford 12K5. They are the same model, two years apart, and both are what the factory installed.

The JBL rows deserve one aside, because they are the only speakers in the table that were ever a *choice*. HARMAN's own history of JBL and Fender describes the F-series — the D130F, D120F and D110F — as developed with Fender, and a 1968 Twin Reverb listing — silverface by then, but the same option — shows the JBL upgrade priced at $150 on an amp that otherwise listed at $499.50. Everything else in the table was simply what came in the crate.

## The Rule People Repeat, and Why It Does Not Hold

A tidy version of this story circulates: early blackface amps got Jensens, later ones got Oxfords, and you can tell the year by the brand. It is the kind of rule that sounds like it came from somebody's notebook, and it would be convenient if it were true.

The dated speakers do not cooperate. Oxford 12K5s turn up in Deluxe Reverbs dated 1964 and 1965; Jensen C12Ns turn up in 1966 and 1967 examples. Both brands span the whole blackface run of the model. If you are dating an amp, the speaker brand tells you nothing on its own — which brings us to the code.

## How to Read the Code on Your Speaker

Every speaker of the period carries an EIA source-and-date code stamped on the frame, and it reads the same way across brands.

**The first three digits are the maker.** The EIA's own source-code book and the Blue Book of Guitar Values agree on the ones that matter here:

| Source code | Maker |
|---|---|
| 220 | Jensen |
| 465 | Oxford |
| 137 | CTS |
| 328 | Utah (listed by the EIA as a division of Oxford) |

**The next digit is the last digit of the year, and the two after it are the week.** So 220-651, the code on that 1967 Deluxe, reads Jensen, a year ending in 6, the fifty-first week — late December of 1966, a few weeks before the amp was likely built. A code like 465-437 would be Oxford, a year ending in 4, week thirty-seven.

Two cautions, and both of them bite.

First, **the year digit repeats every decade.** 220-412 is the twelfth week of 1954 or 1964, and the code alone cannot tell you which. Use the pots, the transformers and the tube chart to choose the decade.

Second, **a speaker date is not the amp's date.** The Blue Book warns that speakers sat on the shelf for months, sometimes longer, before they were installed. A speaker dated a few months — even a year — before the chassis is perfectly normal. A speaker dated *after* the amp was built is a replacement, whatever the seller says about it.

**The model number tells you the rest.** Jensen's Vintage-era naming puts the magnet in the first letter — C for ceramic, P for alnico — then the size, then the model letter. So a C12N is a ceramic 12-inch and a P12Q is an alnico one, and finding both in Deluxe Reverbs of the same era means the magnet type varied too. Oxford's numbers run size, then a letter, then a digit — 12K5, 12T6, 10J4 — and the Blue Book says the letter indicated power handling, higher letters for higher ratings. That fits the pattern in the table: the Twin's 12T6 sits further up the alphabet than the Deluxe's 12K5, and the Twin is the louder amp.

## What Nobody Published

I expected, going in, to find an Oxford spec sheet somewhere — a catalog page, a distributor's price list, a magnet weight in an old service bulletin. There is none to be found. No published power rating, no sensitivity, no resonance figure for the 12K5, the 12K5-6, the 12T6 or the 10J4 has turned up in any source that could be checked. The Blue Book's note about the letter is as close as the record comes, and it gives no numbers.

So any wattage you see quoted for an original Oxford, and any claim that it had more or less low end than the Jensen beside it, is unsourced. That includes the claim — common in listings — that the Oxford is the "softer" or "sweeter" speaker. It may be. There is no published figure to say so, and listening reports from fifty-year-old speakers are reports on fifty-year-old speakers, with whatever recones, dried suspensions and moisture they have collected since.

## The Bigger Lottery Is Inside the Jensen Name

Here is the part that changes how to spend money. The Oxford-or-Jensen question assumes "Jensen" names a sound. Jensen's own current Vintage Ceramic line says otherwise. Three 12-inch models, all of them speakers you will find recommended for a Deluxe Reverb, from Jensen's product pages:

| Model | Rated power | Sensitivity (1 W, 1 m) | Resonance (Fs) | Magnet |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C12Q | 35 W | 93.2 dB | 87.5 Hz | 426 g ceramic |
| C12N | 50 W | 97.7 dB | 113 Hz | 810 g ceramic |
| C12K | 100 W | 99 dB | 115 Hz | 1,450 g ceramic |

These are modern speakers, not measurements of 1960s units, and that distinction matters. But the spread is the point. Between the C12Q and the C12K there is 5.8 dB of sensitivity — the C12K needs about a quarter of the amplifier power for the same level — and 27.5 Hz of resonance. The C12K, which is the speaker Fender fits to the '65 Deluxe Reverb reissue, resonates at 115 Hz, above the 110 Hz fundamental of your open A string. Jensen itself says the C12K became one of the speakers of choice for Fender.

That is a far larger documented difference than anything anyone has documented between an Oxford and a Jensen. Two Deluxe Reverbs, both "with a Jensen," can be further apart than a Jensen amp and an Oxford one.

## What to Do With This

**If you own a blackface amp,** read the code before you believe the listing, the previous owner or the sticker. Source code first, then the date against the chassis, then the model number. Write it down; it is part of the amp's history whether or not it is original.

**If you are matching a sound from a record,** you cannot match the speaker by spec, because the Oxford's specs do not exist and the Jensen's varied by model. Match by the published figures of what you can buy instead. For less level and a lower resonance, the C12Q is the documented choice; for more headroom and more level, the C12N or C12K. Our [Deluxe Reverb settings guide](/blog/fender-deluxe-reverb-settings) has starting points for each amp, and [alnico or ceramic, same speaker](/blog/alnico-vs-ceramic-speaker-same-cone-published-specs) covers what the C-or-P letter does and does not change.

**If you are on a modeler,** Line 6's 1x12 US Deluxe cab in the Helix is captured with an Oxford, according to Line 6's own cab list — our [Helix cab and IR pairings](/blog/helix-cab-ir-pairings) table has it. On other platforms, check whether the Deluxe cab names its speaker before assuming it is a Jensen.

**If you are buying a reissue or a Tone Master,** neither ships with what a 1960s original carried. The reissue's C12K and the Tone Master's neodymium N-12K are compared in [Deluxe Reverb vs. Tone Master](/blog/fender-deluxe-reverb-vs-tonemaster).

The lottery was real, and it is still running — it just moved from the Fullerton loading dock to the speaker you choose.
