Put a Vox AC30 on a church stage and the speaker decides more about how it sits than any knob on the top panel. The two Celestions most AC30s carry, the Alnico Blue and the G12M Greenback, publish nearly the same resonance and differ in two places that matter on a Sunday: how loud they are for the same input, and how much power they can take.

## The Published Figures

Here is what Celestion prints for each speaker, set beside the two current Vox models that use them.

| | Celestion Alnico Blue | Celestion G12M Greenback |
|---|---|---|
| Magnet | Alnico | Ceramic |
| Power rating | 15 W | 25 W |
| Sensitivity | 100 dB | 98 dB |
| Resonance (Fs) | 75 Hz | 75 Hz |
| Two in a 2x12, total rating | 30 W | 50 W |
| Current Vox AC30 that ships with it | AC30C2X | AC30C2 |

*Figures from Celestion's product sheets. One retailer lists the Greenback at 97 dB; Celestion's page says 98.*

Vox sells the AC30C2 and AC30C2X as the same amp with different speakers. So the question "which speaker is the AC30 sound" has two factory answers. The Blue is the older one; it is what the early-sixties AC30s were built around, and it gave the amp its reputation for a bell-like top. The Greenback is what most people get when they buy a new AC30 off the shelf.

## The Resonance Is the Same. The Margin Is Not.

I expected the two sheets to split at the low end. The common description of a Blue is that it does not thud, and the obvious place to look for that is a higher resonance. Celestion publishes 75 Hz for both. Whatever separates them in a palm-muted low E is not in that number, and the magnet type alone does not settle it either. Our [alnico or ceramic comparison](/blog/alnico-vs-ceramic-speaker-same-cone-published-specs) found that, in Jensen's figures, the moving parts predicted the sensitivity gaps better than the magnet did.

What the sheets do show is power. An AC30 makes about 30 watts. Two Blues are rated 15 watts each, which adds up to exactly what the amp can deliver. Run the amp near full output for a long set and the Blues are working at their limit with nothing in reserve. Two Greenbacks are rated 50 watts between them. That is the practical reason the Greenback version is the one that survives being cranked night after night. It is a rating, not a sound.

The current Blue is still a 15-watt speaker. Anyone who runs an AC30 hard should know they are buying a speaker built for a lower ceiling, and that a blown alnico speaker is an expensive repair; our [recone or replace piece](/blog/recone-vs-replace-vintage-guitar-speaker) covers what that repair keeps and what it swaps.

## Two Decibels Matters More Than It Sounds

The Blue's 100 dB against the Greenback's 98 dB means the Blue produces more level from the same amp settings. Two decibels is not subtle. On a quiet stage it shows up as the amp seeming to wake up a notch earlier on the volume knob, and it puts more guitar into the room before the PA does anything.

In a worship setting that cuts both ways. If you play quiet stages with in-ears and the amp is miked, the sound tech will set the mic gain to taste and the level difference disappears into the console. What stays is the character of the top end, which is the part Celestion's own copy sells as the Blue's bell-like edge, and which the AC30's Top Boost circuit already leans into. In a hard-walled room with a bright PA, that is the region that tips over first. Our [harsh-through-the-PA quick fix](/blog/worship-guitar-harsh-through-pa-quick-fix) covers what a sound tech can and cannot fix from the console, and most of it is cutting exactly that range.

If the amp is unmiked and carries the room on its own, the Blue's extra 2 dB is free volume, and it arrives with the chime that the [What a Beautiful Name](/blog/what-a-beautiful-name-guitar-tone-helix) and [Way Maker](/blog/way-maker-guitar-tone-helix) recipes are built around.

## How to Pick

| Choose the Alnico Blue if... | Choose the Greenback if... |
|---|---|
| The part is chime: arpeggios, swells, octave lines up top | The part is rhythm under a dense band and vocals |
| You play single coils, or a guitar with a soft top end | You play bright humbuckers or a Telecaster bridge pickup |
| The room is controlled and the mix engineer knows the amp | The room is hard and bright, or the PA is not yours |
| You keep the amp below full output | You run the AC30 near flat out |
| You want the most volume from the least amp | You want power margin and longer speaker life |

For a volunteer who plays different rooms with different engineers, the Greenback is the safer default. It gives the console less top end to tame and the speakers 20 watts of margin. The Blue rewards a good room and a player who knows where the amp's ceiling is.

If you are setting up the amp itself, the [AC30 settings guide](/blog/vox-ac30-settings-guide) and the [Cut knob explainer](/blog/vox-ac30-cut-knob-explained) cover the controls that interact most with this choice. The Cut control trims the same upper range where the two speakers differ, so a Blue-loaded AC30 usually wants more of it.

## On a Helix, It Is a Cab Block, With One Catch

On the [Helix](/platforms/helix), the **2x12 Blue Bell** is the AC30-with-Blues cab. The Greenback options are the **4x12 Greenback 25** and **4x12 Greenback 20**, both Marshall-style 4x12s. So when you A/B them on the same amp block, you are changing the cabinet size and construction as well as the speaker. Part of the extra weight you hear from the Greenback block is a closed-back 4x12, not the speaker alone.

As starting points, set the high cut near 8.5 kHz on the Blue Bell and near 7 kHz on the Greenback 25. The [Goodness of God recipe](/blog/goodness-of-god-guitar-tone-helix) uses the Greenback 25 that way on purpose. The [Helix cab models breakdown](/blog/helix-cab-models-decoded) lists the source cabinets for both, and the [speaker power-handling guide](/blog/speaker-power-handling-watts-cab) covers the ratings side in more depth for players building a real cab.

The Blue and the Greenback start from the same 75 Hz. What separates them on a stage is 2 dB of sensitivity, 10 watts of rating, and which of those your room can afford.
