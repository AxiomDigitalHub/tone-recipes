On June 2, [TONE3000 and Steve Atkinson released Neural Amp Modeler Architecture 2](/news/tone3000-nam-architecture-2-a2-open-source-modeling) as free, open-source software. By the first week of November it was inside a $130 headphone amp, a ¥47,800 Zoom floor unit, three HeadRush boards, an audio interface, and the Fractal Axe-Fx III. That is the story of the year, and most of what follows either caused it, reacted to it, or carried on beside it.

This is a review of what we reported in 2026, grouped by theme, with each item linked to its dated post or to the original source. Two housekeeping notes. Our news desk started in March, so January and February are covered from outside sources. And this site is written by AI: nothing below is a claim that anyone here played the gear. It is a reading of release notes, announcements and prices.

## NAM A2 turned the capture into a shared file format

Before this year, a capture belonged to the box that made it. A Kemper profile lived on a Kemper, a Neural Capture on a Quad Cortex, a Tone Model in TONEX. NAM files were the exception, and they mostly lived on laptops.

A2 changed the hardware half of that. It came in two versions: A2-Full for desktops and flagship DSP, and A2-Lite, which TONE3000 says runs at 50% CPU on a $3 ARM Cortex-M7 chip. TONE3000 also published a blind MUSHRA listening test with more than 1,000 participants in which A2 scored above Neural Capture V2, IK TONEX and Line 6 Proxy. That test was run by the people releasing the product, so treat it as their claim. What is not a claim is the licensing: the architecture, training code and inference engine are free to ship in commercial products, per [TONE3000's announcement](https://www.tone3000.com/blog/introducing-neural-amp-modeler-nam-architecture-2-a2).

Free matters more than better here. Here is the rollout as we logged it:

The earlier, pre-A2 wave was already under way at the bottom of the market: [Valeton's GP-150](/news/valeton-gp-150-gp-180-nam-support) put NAM import in a $169.99 floorboard in April, and [Mooer's GE100 Pro](/news/mooer-ge100-pro-ai-modeler-under-100) did it for $96 in June.

Then Fractal joined. Fractal built its name on modeling circuits component by component, so a NAM Player block on the Axe-Fx III is the least expected entry on the list. We [covered the announcement on November 2](/news/fractal-axe-fx-iii-firmware-33-nam-a2-player-tone3000-axe-edit). The firmware is out: [Fractal's downloads page](https://www.fractalaudio.com/axe-fx-iii-downloads/) lists firmware 33.01, dated October 31, 2026.

One practical consequence outlasts all the spec sheets. A NAM file is the only amp sound in your rig that survives a change of platform. A Helix preset does not open on a Quad Cortex. A `.nam` file opens on everything in that table.

## Static captures started to look old

A capture is a photograph of an amp at one setting. Turn the "gain" knob on a static capture and you mostly change the level going into a frozen circuit. Three 2026 releases went after that limit from different directions.

Kemper went first. [OS 14 left beta on March 31](/news/kemper-os-14-stable-profiling-2-live) with Profiling 2.0, which Kemper says analyzes more than 100,000 frequency points per profile, and it let the Profiler Player create profiles for the first time. In June, [Two Notes Genome 2.0](/news/two-notes-genome-2-paradex-parametric-capture) added PARADEX, a capture engine that sweeps the amp across its control range so the result re-voices when you turn the gain down. In October, [Zoom's Neurig](/news/zoom-neurig-nam-a2-lite-dna-engine-300-modeler) paired NAM A2 Lite with a separate "DNA Engine" whose models track knob changes.

For anyone who sets one slightly driven sound and rides the guitar's volume knob through a song, this is the capture news that matters. A static capture mostly gets quieter when you roll back. A parametric one, or a modeled amp block, cleans up. Helix amp blocks have always been the second kind, which is worth remembering before assuming a capture is automatically the more realistic option.

## Line 6: three Stadium updates, a free plugin, and a NAM-shaped gap

Line 6 had the busiest release calendar of the big four, and almost all of it landed on the newer platform.

**Helix Stadium 1.3 (March 24).** Line 6 called it the Proxy Update. Per the [press release as carried by MusicPlayers](https://musicplayers.com/2026/03/line-6-launches-helix-stadium-1-3-firmware-update/), it rolled out phase one of Proxy cloning (amp and cab, amp, preamp, and distortion pedal) and added five Agoura channels from two amps, the Matchless DC30 based Matchstick 30 and the Orange Rockerverb based Mandarin Rock 30. Our [March post on this update](/news/helix-stadium-firmware-1-10-new-amps) originally gave the wrong version number and amp list. It has been corrected.

**Monkey and License Manager retired (April 10).** [The servers went dark](/news/line6-monkey-license-manager-retired). It changed nothing for Helix and HX owners, who have updated through HX Edit and Line 6 Central for years, and it mattered a great deal to anyone still holding POD HD model packs.

**Helix Stadium Native (August 4).** [The plugin shipped with a permanently free Intro tier](/news/line6-helix-stadium-native-intro-free-plugin), a first for Line 6. Intro has five amps, all on the Agoura engine, and no HX models, so existing Helix presets do not open in it. One of the five is an AC30 Top Boost, which is the amp most worship tones are built on.

**Helix Stadium 1.4 (October 13).** [The Eras Update](/news/helix-stadium-1-4-eras-update-brit-sl100-four-plexis-stems-matrix-midi) added a Marshall Super Lead modeled in four years ('67, '68, '70, '71), an Omega Granophyre, six effects, cloud stem separation in Showcase, and MIDI control of the Matrix Mixer. [Line 6's release notes](https://kb.line6.com/helix-stadium-1-4-release-notes) put the Agoura total at 67 channels.

**The NAM warning (November 16).** Unofficial firmware that lets the HX Stomp, Helix Floor, Helix LT and POD Go load NAM A2 captures had been circulating for months. [Line 6 asked owners not to install it](/news/line6-warns-against-hacked-hx-nam-a2-firmware). Guitar World's [report](https://www.guitarworld.com/gear/amp-modeler-pedals/line-6-hx-nam-a2-hack-warning) says full support is on its way and that no timeline has been stated.

Put those five together and the pattern is plain. New amp models went to Stadium. HX hardware, in the news we covered, got a server shutdown and a warning.

## What 2026 meant if you play a Helix or HX Stomp on Sunday

Most of this site's readers run an HX Stomp, Helix LT or Helix Floor into in-ears and a PA. Here is the year filtered for that rig.

**Your preset library is safe, and it is not growing.** Nothing we covered this year added an amp model to legacy HX hardware. That is fine for worship work. The AC30, Matchless and Deluxe models that [modern worship tone on Helix](/blog/worship-guitar-tone-helix) depends on are the same ones they were in January, and so is every snapshot you built on them.

**Do not flash the NAM hack onto a Sunday rig.** A NAM block that exists only in unofficial firmware has no ID that official firmware recognizes. Any preset built around it is likely to break the day Line 6 ships its own version, and the snapshot and footswitch assignments go with it. If you want a capture in your chain before then, put a NAM-capable box in a Send/Return block and leave the firmware alone.

**The best worship feature of the year is on Stadium, and it is a MIDI map.** Stadium 1.4 gives each Matrix Mixer layer its own MIDI channel, with separate Mute CCs per source. In practice that means one footswitch can mute the click on the XLR feed to the house while it keeps running in your phones. If you are weighing a Stadium against the hardware you own, that feature is a stronger argument than any amp model. It builds on the same thinking as our [Helix worship snapshots guide](/blog/helix-worship-snapshots-sunday-morning) and the [click and playback sync piece](/blog/helix-midi-clock-playback-click-worship).

**There is now a free way to audition the Agoura AC30.** Stadium Native Intro costs nothing and has no expiry. It will not open your presets, but it will let you hear how the newer engine's AC30 behaves before you spend money on new hardware.

**The eight-block budget got cheaper to work around.** [Boss's EX-4](/news/boss-ex-4-effects-expander-four-blocks-299) runs four effects at once for $299.99, [Valeton's VDS pedals](/news/valeton-vds-series-dual-engine-stereo-pitch-modulation-delay-reverb-119) are €119 each, and [Behringer teased 200 to 300 pedals at $10 to $15](/news/behringer-teases-200-300-pedals-10-15-dollars-hx-stomp-block-budget), with no models, specs or date so far. For an HX Stomp owner the useful question is which jobs to move off the DSP, and a tuner, a boost or an always-on compressor are the obvious candidates.

**Stereo pedals do not change a mono mix.** [Walrus's Lore and Fable MKII](/news/walrus-lore-fable-mkii-stereo-midi-127-presets-349) and [Strymon's TimeLine MX](/news/strymon-timeline-mx-dual-engine-delay) both sell width. Most church PAs sum the guitar to mono, so check any ambient pedal summed before you buy it for its stereo image.

**Acoustic players got one item worth watching.** [Gibson and L.R. Baggs announced the HiFi DNA](/news/gibson-baggs-hifi-dna-dual-sensor-class-a-acoustic-299), a $299 dual-sensor pickup. A different pickup means a different starting EQ, so the settings in our [acoustic through a modeler guide](/blog/acoustic-guitar-through-modeler-worship-di) are a starting point for it, not a preset.

## Quad Cortex, Fractal, Kemper, Fender and IK

**Neural DSP** opened the year with the [Quad Cortex mini](/news/neural-dsp-quad-cortex-mini-shipping), shown at NAMM at €1,299. In April it [announced CorOS 4.1.0 and a US price rise](/news/neural-dsp-coros-4-1-pcom-darkglass-ultimate) on the full-size unit, from $1,699 to $1,799 on May 7, citing tariffs. [CorOS 4.1.0 shipped on August 26](/news/neural-dsp-coros-4-1-0-ships-device-presets-virtual-devices), four months after the announcement, with five Archetype X plugins, Device Presets and eight new virtual devices. Neural called it the biggest CorOS update it has released.

**Fractal** spent the first half of the year on modeling accuracy. [Axe-Fx III 32.04](/news/axe-fx-iii-32-04-power-amp-modeling) reworked the power amp model in April, [32.05](/news/axe-fx-iii-32-05-pvh-6160-block-clean) and [32.06](/news/axe-fx-iii-32-06-brit-jvm-crunch) added amps, and [FM9 12.00 and FM3 13.0](/news/fm9-12-00-final-fm3-13-beta-32-06-amps) carried the same work to the smaller units in July. The $699 [AM4 reached open stock](/news/fractal-am4-v2-firmware-now-shipping) in the spring. Then came firmware 33 and NAM.

**Kemper** shipped Profiling 2.0, covered above. It was the company's one large release of the year in our coverage.

**Fender** moved the Tone Master Pro toward high gain with [three EVH 5150 III models in June](/news/fender-tone-master-pro-firmware-1-8-45-evh-5150), and in November [rebuilt its Virtual Capo](/news/fender-tone-master-pro-november-2026-virtual-capo-rebuilt-greer-lightspeed) on a new polyphonic pitch algorithm, released as a public beta. It also put Joe Bonamassa's name on a digital amp, the [$1,999.99 Tone Master '59 Twin](/news/fender-bonamassa-tone-master-59-twin-jb-5f8a-quad-cortex-helix).

**IK Multimedia** filled in the TONEX line from both ends: [preset sharing on ToneNET](/news/ik-multimedia-tonex-1-11-tonenet-preset-sharing) in April, the [TONEX ONE+](/news/ik-multimedia-tonex-one-plus-wireless-midi) with Bluetooth and MIDI in May at an unchanged $249.99, and the [$999.99 TONEX Board](/news/ik-multimedia-tonex-board-four-footswitches-computer-capture) in September. Capture on the Board still happens on a computer.

## The floor of the market kept dropping

A battery and a sub-$300 price became ordinary this year. [Mooer's GE100 Pro Li](/news/mooer-ge100-pro-ai-modeler-under-100) is $116 with a battery. [Positive Grid's Spark PEDAL](/news/positive-grid-spark-pedal-199-33-amps-six-presets) is $199, with the catch that it stores six presets. [Hotone's Pulze Jr.](/news/hotone-pulze-jr-ai-presets-nam-battery-modeling-amp) is a $299 battery amp. The [NUX MG-50Li](/news/nux-mg-50li-ships-april-20) is $499 with three DSPs. [Boss's Katana-25](/news/boss-katana-25-practice-amp-8-inch-open-back-half-watt-199) is $199.99 with a half-watt mode, and [Harley Benton's FLY-STOMP](/news/harley-benton-fly-stomp-battery-multi-effects-guitar-bass) is a $54 battery multi-effects.

Is the modeler replacing the amp, then? The sales data says not yet. In a [Guitar World interview published last December](https://www.guitarworld.com/gear/trends-behind-the-used-guitar-gear-market), Reverb's Joel Handley said tube and traditional solid-state amps still outpace modelers on the marketplace, even though the Quad Cortex earned more revenue there in 2024 than any other single piece of gear. A [March post of ours](/news/modelers-outsell-tube-amps-2025-reverb-data) originally stated the opposite. It was wrong and has been corrected.

## Text-prompt tone showed up in hardware

[Positive Grid's REACTOR](/news/positive-grid-reactor-amp-intelligence-tone-from-prompt), announced June 2 at $349 and $449, builds a signal chain from a typed description, a recording or a photo. A week later [Mooer put a text-prompt assistant and a stem separator in the $96 GE100 Pro](/news/mooer-ge100-pro-ai-modeler-under-100). Hotone's Pulze Jr. generates presets, and Melofty's Axom Max has a prompt assistant too.

Stem separation was the quieter and more useful arrival. Mooer has it on the device, and Helix Stadium 1.4 does it in the cloud through Yamaha's Music Connect service. Soloing the guitar stem of a recording is a faster way to learn a part than any preset generator.

No maker has published how often a prompt lands on a usable tone. Until one does, text-to-tone is a faster way to reach a starting preset, and the tone still has to be set by ear.

## Pedals: big delays, small boxes, old circuits

The expensive end was all delay and pitch. [Chase Bliss shipped the Big Time](/news/chase-bliss-big-time-stereo-delay) at $999, [Strymon's TimeLine MX](/news/strymon-timeline-mx-dual-engine-delay) runs two delay engines at once for $679, and [Eventide's H9 Gen 2](/news/eventide-h9-harmonizer-gen-2-full-h90-library) put the H90's algorithm library in the smaller box for $599.

The middle was reissues and signature drives: [MXR's EVH Modern High Gain](/news/mxr-evh-modern-high-gain-5150iii-third-channel-pedal), the [JHS Wanderer](/news/jhs-wanderer-notaklon-morning-glory-order-switch-299), the [Fulltone OCD-Lux](/news/fulltone-ocd-lux-dual-drive-raw-voicing-hp-lp-stack-299), [Seymour Duncan's 805+](/news/seymour-duncan-805-plus-overdrive-forza-mode-189), and Catalinbread's [revival of the DOD Punkifier](/news/catalinbread-cold-one-dod-punkifier-glitch-fuzz-199). Real tubes came back in pedal form with the [Keeley Tube Drive](/news/keeley-tube-drive-twin-triode-real-tube-into-modeler) and the [Tone King Royce](/news/tone-king-royce-ac30-tube-preamp-pedal-600).

Two smaller releases were aimed straight at modeler owners. [Walrus's Canvas Expression](/news/walrus-audio-canvas-expression-pedal-selectable-resistance) has a selectable-resistance output, so one expression pedal works across brands. [SolidGoldFX's Fuzzbox](/news/solidgoldfx-fuzzbox-vintage-modern-switch-modeler-buffer) has a Vintage/Modern toggle that SolidGoldFX says makes it work with buffers, and a buffer upstream is the usual reason a fuzz sounds wrong on a modern board.

## Amps and guitars: signatures at both ends of the price list

Signature amps had a strong year. Marshall built [Billie Joe Armstrong's 1959BJA](/news/marshall-1959bja-billie-joe-armstrong-dookie-plexi), its first artist signature amp in 14 years, and a [20-watt Hendrix Plexi](/news/marshall-jmh-studio-hendrix-60th-plexi-20-watt). [Dumble returned](/news/dumble-is-back-overdrive-special-25000-which-dumble-does-your-modeler-have) with three amps between $7,000 and $25,000, all listed as sold out within days. Jimmy Page's [Sundragon Dragonette](/news/sundragon-dragonette-jimmy-page-6-watt-led-zeppelin-i-combo) put the Led Zeppelin I amp in a 6-watt box, and Friedman's [Jose 20](/news/friedman-jose-20-el84-arredondo-tribute-head-combo) named the modder behind a generation of hot-rodded Marshalls.

Guitars split the same way. At the top, Gibson made [60 Gustavo Cerati ES-335s](/news/gibson-custom-gustavo-cerati-1965-es-335-60-made-miami) and a [$19,999 Pearly Gates replica](/news/gibson-billy-gibbons-pearly-gates-les-paul-murphy-lab-standard-3199). At the bottom, Harley Benton sold [relic'd nitro guitars from $238](/news/harley-benton-aged-series-relic-nitro-238) and Gretsch a [$278 solidbody](/news/gretsch-streamliner-cvt-solidbody-ceramic-humbuckers-modeler). In between, Fender [refreshed the American Professional line](/news/fender-american-professional-2026-v-mod-iii-radial-saddles-hotter-pickups-modeler-input) with hotter V-Mod III pickups, PRS launched the [Tekton family](/news/prs-tekton-four-model-shred-family-stylus-pickups-26-5-svn), and Gibson began selling its [Alnico 8 Elev8 humbuckers](/news/gibson-elev8-pickups-sold-separately-alnico-8-139) separately.

For a modeler player the common thread is pickup output. Hotter stock pickups, baritone scales and mismatched pickup pairs all change what reaches the amp block, and a preset dialed on a vintage-output Strat will not behave the same with a 14.5k ceramic humbucker in front of it.

## What to watch in 2027

Everything in this section is expectation. None of it has been announced beyond what is linked.

- **Official NAM on Line 6 hardware.** Line 6 has said support is coming. It has not said when, on which units, or how much DSP it will cost. The legacy HX processors are older than anything else now running A2, so a reduced implementation on HX and a fuller one on Stadium would not be surprising.
- **NAM on the FM9 and FM3.** Fractal's pattern in 2026 was Axe-Fx III first, FM9 weeks later, FM3 after that. If firmware 33 follows it, the smaller units get a NAM Player in the first half of the year. Fractal has not confirmed this.
- **Capture on the cheap boxes.** Zoom has promised cloud-built NAM captures from gear plugged into the Neurig's loop in December, and has not announced a US price. If that ships and works, making a capture stops being a premium feature.
- **Parametric capture in the open format.** Kemper, Two Notes and Zoom all moved past the static snapshot. An open capture format that also tracks the knobs is the obvious next step, and nobody has announced one.
- **Behringer's pedals.** The teaser had no models and no date. Whether any of the 200 to 300 arrive in 2027, and what they clone, will say a lot about the budget pedal market.
- **Prices.** Neural DSP attributed its May increase to tariffs. Other US prices held through the autumn in what we covered, and that may not last.

If you run a Helix or HX Stomp and you are deciding whether to buy anything, the cheapest useful move right now is free: back up your setlists, leave the firmware official, and wait for the NAM release note.
