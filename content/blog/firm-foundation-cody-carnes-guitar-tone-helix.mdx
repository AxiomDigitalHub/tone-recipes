> **Start Here: Firm Foundation in Five Moves**
> 1. Tempo: **75 BPM in 6/8, key of Bb** (MultiTracks, original single). Eighth note = **400 ms**. Felt beat = **1,200 ms**.
> 2. If your chart says **148**, it's counting eighth notes. Store **75** (or 150 with the note values below), not 148.
> 3. Delay: **1/8** under strumming, **1/4 dotted** under swells. No dotted eighth.
> 4. Amp: **US Deluxe Nrm** into a **1x12 US Deluxe**, with a **Minotaur** for the later choruses.
> 5. Snapshots: four, built as a ladder, because the song is almost all chorus.

| What you want | Tempo stored at 75 | Tempo stored at 150 | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Eighth note, under strumming | 1/8 | 1/4 | 400 ms |
| One felt beat, under swells | 1/4 dotted | 1/2 dotted | 1,200 ms |
| Avoid: lands between the notes | 1/8 dotted | 1/4 dotted | 600 ms |

## One Song, Three Tempos

*Firm Foundation (He Won't)* was released in 2021 on Sparrow Records, written by Cody Carnes, Chandler Moore and Austin Davis (CCLI 7188203). MultiTracks lists the original master in **Bb at 75 BPM in 6/8**, 6:18 long. Worship Together's song page lists the same song in Bb at **148 BPM**. PraiseCharts gives no number for its radio version, just 6/8 and "Med Fast."

We expected 148 to be a different arrangement or a typo. It's neither. A bar of 6/8 has six eighth notes, and a tempo can count any of three things: every eighth note, pairs of eighths (quarter notes), or the three-eighth beat you sway to (dotted quarters). MultiTracks' help center says Playback counts 6/8 "using the industry standard quarter note BPM," so its 75 is pairs of eighths. That makes the eighth-note rate 150 per minute, and 148 is that number within 1.3 percent. The sway is 50 per minute.

So the song is slow to sway to and quick underneath, which is probably why one site calls it "Medium" and another "Med Fast." The eighth notes are where the energy is, and that's the subdivision an electric part plays.

## Which Number Goes Into the Helix?

The Helix treats its stored tempo as quarter notes, so the number you store decides which note value lands where:

- **Store 75:** 1/8 = **400 ms**, the song's eighth note. 1/4 dotted = **1,200 ms**, one sway.
- **Store 148:** 1/8 = **203 ms**, a sixteenth. Your usual eighth-note setting suddenly sounds like slapback. To get the eighth back you'd use 1/4 (405 ms), and for the sway 1/2 dotted (1,216 ms).

Storing 148 also builds in drift. A 1/4 repeat at 148 is 405 ms against the recording's 400, about 5 ms late per repeat, so the fourth repeat is 22 ms behind the band. The felt-beat repeat is 16 ms late each time. Neither is a disaster on a short tail; under a long swell with feedback around 40 percent, the echoes smear against the kick. Store 75. If your team's software already says 150, that works too, with the right-hand column of the table.

The dotted eighth, the default worship delay, comes out at 600 ms at 75. That's one and a half eighth notes, so in 6/8 the repeats fall halfway between the notes of each group of three. It's the same trap the [Trust in God](/blog/trust-in-god-elevation-worship-guitar-tone-helix) and [Gratitude](/blog/gratitude-brandon-lake-guitar-tone-helix) builds cover, and the [6/8 delay table](/blog/delay-time-bpm-calculator) has the numbers for every tempo in this range.

Don't tap this song in. A Helix treats each tap as a quarter note, so tapping the sway gives you 50 BPM and every note value is off by half again. If tempo has to change mid-song, our guide to [Helix Tap Tempo Pitch](/blog/helix-tap-tempo-pitch-accurate-vs-transparent) explains why the repeats bend when it does and which setting stops it.

## A Song With No Verse

MultiTracks' song map for the original master runs Intro, Chorus 1, Chorus 2, Turnaround, Chorus 3, Chorus 4, Refrain, Chorus 1, Chorus 2, Refrain, Vamp, Interlude, Bridge, Tag, Chorus 1, Chorus 2, Refrain, Outro, Ending. Nineteen sections, and none of them is a verse. Eight are choruses and three are refrains. (Worship Together's chart offers an alternate verse; the recording doesn't use it.)

That changes how a snapshot plan works. In a verse-chorus song the snapshots follow the form: quiet verse, bigger chorus. Here the same melody comes around eight times, so a snapshot has to mark *which time through* it is. A ladder does that:

| Snapshot | Sections | Changes |
|---|---|---|
| 1. First pass | Intro, Chorus 1–2, Turnaround | Delay 1/4 dotted at 20%, reverb about 3 s, drive off, light picking or swells |
| 2. Build | Chorus 3–4, Refrains, second Chorus 1–2 | Delay 1/8 at 12%, reverb about 2.5 s, Minotaur on at low gain |
| 3. Bridge | Vamp, Interlude, Bridge, Tag | Delay 1/4 dotted at 25%, reverb about 4 s at 30%, drive off, volume swells |
| 4. Last pass | Final Chorus 1–2, Refrain, Outro | Delay 1/8, Minotaur gain up, reverb about 2.5 s |

Snapshot 1 and snapshot 4 play the same chorus. The difference between them is what the congregation hears as the song building, and it has to come from the guitar as much as the band. Set Tempo Select to per-preset so every snapshot shares the stored 75. Our [Sunday-morning snapshots guide](/blog/helix-worship-snapshots-sunday-morning) covers the one-preset-per-song layout this assumes.

## The Amp, the Drive and the Room

**Amp:** **US Deluxe Nrm** into a **1x12 US Deluxe**. Drive about 4.5, Bass 4, Mid 5.5, Treble 6, Presence 4, Ch Vol to taste. A Deluxe breaks up earlier than a Twin, so at this Drive setting a harder strum on the eighth-note pattern pushes it just past clean, the way a small combo starts to grit when the drummer moves to the ride. That edge is useful in a song that has to grow without new sections to grow into. Cab low cut near 90 Hz, high cut near 7 kHz.

**Comp and drive:** an **LA Studio Comp** first, Peak Reduction set so it only catches the loudest strums, Mix about 60 percent. A **Minotaur** after it: Gain about 2 for snapshot 2, about 4 for snapshot 4, Treble about 5, Output set so engaging it adds a little level. A Klon-style drive keeps the low strings from swelling as the gain goes up, which matters when the bass player is also climbing.

**Reverb:** at a felt beat of 1,200 ms, a 3-second decay covers two and a half sways. A **Glitz** or **Dynamic Hall** at that length, mix around 25 percent, pre-delay 30 ms, low cut 200 Hz, carries the first pass. Pull it to 2.5 seconds once the band fills in.

## Which Key Is Your Team in?

The original is in **Bb**. Most electric players reach it with **capo 3 and G shapes** or **capo 1 and A shapes**. Worship Together also lists A, D and G as recommended keys, and PraiseCharts offers A, Bb and G.

The other version teams often ask about is the medley with *Great Are You Lord* from Kari Jobe and Cody Carnes' *Live From Europe* (2024). MultiTracks lists it in **A**, at the same **75 BPM in 6/8**, 8:24 long, with two bridges and a fifth chorus in its map. Moving between the two versions is a one-fret capo change: capo 2 with G shapes, or open A shapes. The delay settings don't change at all.

On an **HX Stomp**, merge snapshots 1 and 3 (both use the 1/4 dotted delay and no drive) and swell with a volume pedal. On a pedalboard, any delay with a millisecond display takes 400 and 1,200 directly. On a **Quad Cortex** or another modeler with a stored tempo, the same rule applies: a modeler's BPM counts quarter notes, so store 75 and pick the note values from the left-hand column.

## What to Try First

Check which number your team's chart and click use before anything else. Set a delay to 1/8 with the mix high, store the chart's tempo, and play along with the recording. If the repeats fill in the eighth notes, you're set. If they run twice as fast as the strumming, the chart is counting eighth notes; halve the tempo, or move every note value up one step.
