*How Great Is Our God* is one of the easiest songs in the worship catalog to learn and one of the easiest to play flat. The guitar tone for it is not the hard part. The hard part is that the chart gives you the same chords in the verse, the chorus and the bridge, so the harmony never tells the room that anything has changed. The lift from section to section has to come from the band, and on electric guitar most of it comes from how the preset is laid out.

| Recording | Key | BPM | Length | Sections in the MultiTracks map | Dotted 1/8 | Quarter |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chris Tomlin, *Arriving* | Db | 76 | 4:31 | 11 | 592 ms | 789 ms |
| Chris Tomlin, World Edition (*The Essential Collection*) | Db | 78 | 5:47 | 17 | 577 ms | 769 ms |
| Anthem Worship, *Praise* | F | 71 | 3:19 | 8 | 634 ms | 845 ms |

Keys, tempos, lengths and section counts are from MultiTracks; all three are in 4/4. Chris Tomlin, Jesse Reeves and Ed Cash wrote the song (CCLI 4348399), and *Arriving* came out in September 2004. PraiseCharts also lists the original in Db at 76. WorshipTogether gives the original key as G and prints its chart there, so if your chart is in G and the track is in Db, both are right about their own page. The two sites also label the same 76 BPM differently: PraiseCharts calls it "Med Fast" and WorshipTogether calls it "Slow." Go by the number.

Db is a fret above C. A capo at the first fret with C shapes (C, G/B, Am7, Fmaj7, G) puts you in the recording's key and keeps open strings in the chords.

## One Progression, Eleven Sections

WorshipTogether's chart, in G, runs the verse as G, D/F#, Em7, Cmaj7, D. The chorus is the same five chords in the same order. The bridge uses D/F#, Em7, G/D, Cmaj7 and D before landing back on G. That G/D is the only chord in the song that belongs to one section, and it is an inversion of a chord you were already playing.

MultiTracks maps the *Arriving* recording like this: Intro, Verse 1, Chorus, Verse 2, Chorus, Bridge, Bridge, Chorus, Chorus, Chorus, Ending. WorshipTogether's chart ends the same way, with "REPEAT BRIDGE" and "REPEAT CHORUS 3X."

I expected the bridge to be where the song opens up, because that is what bridges usually do: a new chord, a key lift, somewhere for the band to go. The chart shows the same chord set walking down the bass (F#, E, D, C in the G chart) and nothing else. Then the map shows something harder. There is no instrumental section anywhere in it, and the song finishes with three choruses back to back. Same words, same melody, same chords, three times.

So the arrangement asks the electric guitar for five different levels on one progression, and three of them are for the same section. If your chorus sound is a single snapshot, the last ninety seconds of the song have nowhere to go.

## The Helix Preset

Set the tempo to 76 for the *Arriving* recording, or follow the click over [MIDI clock](/blog/helix-midi-clock-playback-click-worship). The delay is set as a note value so the snapshots can change it.

| Block | Settings | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Volume Pedal** | First in the chain | Swells for the intro and first verse |
| **Deluxe Comp** | Threshold −24 dB, Ratio 3:1, Mix 50% | Keeps picked two-note shapes even at the bottom of the ladder |
| **Minotaur** | Gain 3, Tone 5, Level 6 | The first step up; off until the bridge repeat |
| **Essex A30** | Drive 4, Bass 5, Treble 6, Cut 5, Ch Vol to taste | Clean when you play lightly, a little grain when you strum hard |
| **2x12 Blue Bell** | Low Cut 100 Hz, High Cut 7.5 kHz | Keeps the low strings out of the bass player's descending line in the bridge |
| **Adriatic Delay** | Time 1/8 dotted, Feedback 25%, Mix 18% | 592 ms at 76 BPM |
| **Plateaux** | Decay 2.5 s, Mix 22% | Fills the space under held chords without covering the next one |

The Essex A30 is the Helix model of a top-boost AC30. It has a Cut control where most amps have Presence, and Cut works backwards: higher is darker. If the tone is harsh in your in-ears, raise Cut before you touch Treble.

## Five Snapshots for Three Choruses

| Snapshot | Where | What changes | What to play |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1: Bed | Intro, Verse 1 | Minotaur off, Drive 4, delay 1/8 dotted at 18%, Plateaux 28% | Swells, one per chord |
| 2: Lift | First two choruses, Verse 2 | Minotaur off, Drive 4, delay 16%, Plateaux 22% | Picked two-note shapes on the middle strings |
| 3: Bridge | Both bridge passes, first of the three choruses | Minotaur on, Drive 4, delay 16%, Plateaux 20% | Strummed chords, lightly the first time through |
| 4: Up | Second of the three choruses | Minotaur on, Drive 5, delay 14%, Plateaux 18% | Full strums, add the low strings |
| 5: Top | Last chorus | Minotaur on, Drive 5, delay 1/4 at 14%, Plateaux 18%, output +1.5 dB | Same strum, an octave shape on top if there is a second guitar holding the chords |

Each step changes one or two things. From 2 to 3 the Minotaur comes on. From 3 to 4 the amp's Drive goes from 4 to 5. From 4 to 5 the output comes up 1.5 dB and the delay changes from a dotted eighth to a quarter note.

That last change matters more than the level. A dotted eighth puts each repeat between your strums, which is what makes a picked verse part feel like it is moving. Under full eighth-note strumming in the last chorus, those in-between repeats are noise. A quarter-note repeat lands on the beat, behind a strum you are already playing, so the delay thickens the part without adding rhythm to it.

The reverb goes down as the ladder goes up, from 28 percent to 18. That is on purpose. The quiet sections need the reverb to fill space, and the loud ones already have a full band doing that.

Verse 2 uses snapshot 2, not snapshot 1. The song has already had a chorus by then, and dropping all the way back to swells makes the second verse feel like a restart. Play the same picked shapes you used in the chorus, a little quieter.

For the ending, go back to snapshot 1 on the last chord and let it ring.

### Other versions

The World Edition's map has 17 sections and adds two instrumentals, two refrains and an outro. Use snapshot 3 for the first instrumental and snapshot 4 for the second, and treat the refrains like verses. The Anthem Worship arrangement has no closing choruses at all: it ends on a double bridge, so snapshots 1 through 3 cover it and the bridge repeat is the top of the song.

Teams often run this song into the hymn *How Great Thou Art*. If yours does, check the hymn's meter and tempo on your own chart before service and save a second preset for it; a delay synced for 76 BPM in 4/4 will not suit a hymn your team plays freely.

## Other Platforms

- **HX Stomp:** you get three snapshots. Use Bed, Bridge and Top, cover the Lift by bringing your guitar's volume knob up from the Bed snapshot, and cover the Up step by strumming harder into snapshot 3. Drop the Volume Pedal block and swell with the guitar's volume knob.
- **Quad Cortex:** an AC30-style capture set clean, a Klon-type drive in front, an analog-voiced delay and a plate. Build the same five scenes, and put the delay's note value under scene control for the last one.
- **Pedalboard:** three steps are realistic. Light drive off for verses and early choruses, on for the bridge, and a second boost or your guitar's volume knob for the last chorus. If your delay has a subdivision switch, flip it from dotted eighth to quarter before the final chorus, or turn the delay off there and let the reverb carry it.

## Before Rehearsal

Count the choruses in your team's arrangement and decide which one is the top. Everything else gets set by working down from there. If your worship leader likes to add a fourth chorus when the room is singing, hold snapshot 5 back for it and stay on 4 until you are sure it is the last one.
