Tom DeLonge's black Strat with the white racing stripes is for sale for the first time. To The Stars, DeLonge's company, opened pre-orders on November 27 for the **Limited Edition "I Feel So" Fender Stratocaster**, and Guitar World reported it the same morning. The run is 500 guitars at **$1,699.99** each.

The original was a one-off the Fender Custom Shop built in 2002. It showed up in Box Car Racer's "I Feel So" video and on that band's tour, and it never went into production. DeLonge's regular signature Strat has been back in Fender's catalog for a few years. This finish hasn't.

## What's Confirmed

All of this comes from the To The Stars product page:

- **Run:** 500 worldwide, sold only through To The Stars, one per customer and household
- **Price:** $1,699.99, not eligible for discounts or free shipping
- **Finish:** black with Olympic White racing stripes
- **Pickup:** one Seymour Duncan Invader humbucker
- **Neck:** "Early 2000s" spec with a Modern C profile, 9.5-inch radius slab rosewood board, 21 medium jumbo frets, 7mm white pearloid dots, vintage-style truss rod and a '69-style Strat logo
- **Hardware:** Fender Deluxe locking tuners and locking strap buttons
- **Neck plate:** stamped with the serial number over DeLonge's artwork
- **Included:** an embroidered Fender FE1225 gig bag and a signed certificate of authenticity
- **Shipping:** made to order, expected around late March 2027, subject to change

Three things to know before you pay. The sale is final and non-refundable. The photos show a pre-production prototype, and the page says all details can change. And Guitar World points out the arrow inlays from the original guitar aren't on this one. It has plain dots.

**Not on the listing:** body wood, bridge type, control layout, country of manufacture and weight. The regular Tom DeLonge Strat is a hardtail with a single volume knob, and this one will probably match, but the page doesn't say so. If the bridge or the knob count matters to you, ask To The Stars before ordering, because you can't send it back.

## One Pickup, and It's the Loud One

The Invader is the whole electrical story here. It's one of the highest-output passive humbuckers Seymour Duncan sells, with ceramic magnets and big hex-head pole pieces. It's thick in the lows and low mids, and it pushes an amp's front end hard.

That changes how you set everything after it. Our [overdrive with humbuckers guide](/blog/overdrive-with-humbuckers-settings) covers the general rule, and the Invader is the extreme case of it:

- **You need less gain than you think.** The pickup already supplies a lot of it. An amp setting that sounds right with a vintage-output humbucker turns to mush with an Invader.
- **You need less bass than you think.** The pickup is bass-heavy on its own. Add a Rectifier-style amp, which is also bass-heavy, and palm mutes lose their edges.
- **Pickup height is your tone knob.** If the guitar has no tone control, the screwdriver is the adjustment. Lowering the pickup a turn or two cleans up the lows and gives you more dynamic range. Our [pickup height post](/blog/pickup-height-magnet-pull-warble) explains what to listen for.

Carl's piece on [single-pickup guitars](/blog/single-pickup-guitar-logic) makes the bigger point: with one pickup you stop switching and start using the volume knob and your right hand. On a guitar like this, the volume knob is the clean channel.

## How to Set It Up on a Helix or HX Stomp

A caveat first. I couldn't find a primary source that names the amps on the Box Car Racer record. DeLonge's blink-182 sound from those years is usually tied to a Mesa Triple Rectifier and a Marshall JCM900, and one Reverb retrospective has him moving toward a Gibson ES-335 during Box Car Racer. So treat what follows as a starting point for "single Invader into a high-gain amp," not a documented rig.

Two amp choices on Helix, both on the 0–10 scale:

**Cali Rectifire** (Mesa Dual Rectifier, Modern mode) for the thick, scooped side:

- Drive 4.0
- Bass 3.0
- Mid 5.5
- Treble 5.5
- Presence 5.0

**Brit 2204** (Marshall JCM800) for the tighter, more midrange side. Helix doesn't have a JCM900, and our [JCM800 vs. JCM900 comparison](/blog/jcm800-vs-jcm900) explains how the two differ:

- Drive 5.5
- Bass 3.5
- Mid 6.5
- Treble 6.0
- Presence 5.0

Those Drive numbers will look low. Start there anyway and add gain only if chords stop sustaining. Octave riffs, which are most of what DeLonge plays, get clearer as the gain comes down, because two notes an octave apart smear quickly when the amp is saturated.

Then the usual high-gain housekeeping:

- **Low cut on the cab block at 90–100 Hz, high cut at 7–8 kHz.** If it still fizzes, our [fizzy high gain fix](/blog/fix-fizzy-high-gain) walks through the rest.
- **A noise gate first in the chain.** A pickup this hot brings noise up with it. Set the threshold just high enough to close between chords. The [gate settings guide](/blog/noise-gate-threshold-decay-settings-high-gain) has the method for doing that without cutting off sustain.
- **A second snapshot for cleans.** Drop the amp's Drive to around 2 and bring the channel volume up to match. Then roll the guitar's volume to 6 or 7. On a Helix Floor or LT you can also switch on the Guitar In pad in Global Settings, which exists for exactly this kind of pickup.

The [Helix amp model cheat sheet](/blog/helix-amp-model-cheat-sheet) lists what each of those model names is based on if you're on another platform and need the equivalent.

## Do You Need This One

For the tone, no. The standard Tom DeLonge Strat has the same pickup. So does any hardtail guitar you route an Invader into, and a used Invader costs about what a pedal does. If you own a Squier with a bridge humbucker, a pickup swap and the settings above get you most of the sound.

What the $1,699.99 buys is the stripe finish, the serialized neck plate, the signed certificate and a place in a 500-guitar run. That's a fair thing to want if Box Car Racer was your record. It's a collector's purchase with a four-month wait and no returns, so go in knowing that.
