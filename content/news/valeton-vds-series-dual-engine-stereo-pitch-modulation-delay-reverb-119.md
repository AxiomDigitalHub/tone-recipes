Valeton just announced the VDS Series, three stompboxes that each run **two effects engines in true stereo** (stereo in, stereo out) for **€119 apiece**. One does delay and reverb, one does modulation, and one is a dual pitch shifter. On all three, holding both footswitches while you power up flips the pedal between true bypass and buffered bypass. That's a small detail, and it's the one that tells you somebody at Valeton has actually built a pedalboard.

There's no US price yet. Thomann lists all three as "on request," and Gearnews expects delivery to take **at least two to three months**. So this is an announcement, not something you can buy this weekend.

## What's in the three boxes

**VDS-1D Delay & Reverb**
- Reverb engine: hall, plate, spring, or shimmer (octave-up). Controls are decay, FILTER (tone) and R-MIX (wet/dry).
- Holding the reverb footswitch **freezes the tail**.
- Delay engine: digital (STANDARD), BBD, or tape. Controls are TIME/DIV, DB (feedback) and D-MIX.
- Holding the right footswitch puts it into tap tempo.

**VDS-2D Dual Modulation**
- Engine 1: phaser, flanger or chorus. Engine 2: rotary, envelope filter or tremolo.
- Controls are speed, depth and two MORPH knobs. MORPH 1 sets engine 1's wet/dry mix in series (STEREO) mode, or the balance between the two engines in parallel mode. MORPH 2 is context-dependent: rotary mic position, filter cutoff or tremolo waveform.
- Each engine gets its own footswitch and, unusually, **its own tap tempo**.

**VDS-3D Dual Pitch Shifter**
- Two pitch engines set to different intervals, each with its own PITCH knob.
- A WET B sets the volume ratio between the two engines, and DRY sets the unprocessed level.
- Three glide modes on a mini switch, with RISE (glide-up speed) and FALL (return speed) controls.

## Why the pitch shifter matters if you run an eight-block modeler

I run an HX Stomp, and here's the thing I keep running into: **pitch is the most expensive thing you can put on a small modeler.** We broke down the actual numbers in [what polyphonic pitch blocks cost in DSP and whether they're worth it](/blog/polyphonic-pitch-blocks-dsp-cost-worth-it). The short version is that one good harmony or poly-shift block can take a share of the chip that would otherwise run your amp, cab and a couple of drives.

A two-voice pitch pedal with its own dry control does that job off the chip entirely. Gearnews says it sounds like a polyphonic shifter, but that's one listener's early impression, not a measured spec, so wait for demos before trusting the tracking. If it does track cleanly, here's how I'd wire it up:

- **Before the modeler, in front of the amp block**, if you're using it for octave-down riffs or a fake 12-string. Set DRY to unity and bring one engine up an octave at low A WET B. Pitch shifters track best on a clean, uncompressed signal, so it goes first.
- **In the modeler's send/return**, if you want harmonies or shimmer-style pads after the dirt. We cover the routing in [send/return loop tricks beyond shimmer](/blog/modeler-send-return-loop-tricks-beyond-shimmer). Keep in mind the Stomp's two sends and returns only become one stereo loop when you pair them, so running the pedal in stereo uses up both.
- **Glide modes** are the part a modeler block usually won't do from one switch. Set RISE slow and FALL fast and you get a whammy-style pitch scoop on press with a quick snap back on release. Set both slow and you get the slow-rise pad that ambient players build with an expression pedal.

## The delay/reverb box is the worship-board sleeper

The VDS-1D's shimmer reverb plus **hold-to-freeze** covers most of what an ambient worship player builds a pad patch for. That's the move we describe in [faking a synth pad on guitar with freeze and hold](/blog/synth-pad-guitar-no-keyboard-freeze-hold). If you're on a budget and don't own a big reverb pedal, our [shimmer without a BigSky](/blog/shimmer-reverb-without-bigsky) guide covers where to land decay and mix. Those starting points should carry over: keep R-MIX lower than feels right, because shimmer turns to mud fast in a full band.

The catch: the gear coverage doesn't say whether the delay and reverb can be set parallel or only run in series, and there's no mention of presets, MIDI or power draw. On a worship board where one song needs a dotted-eighth and the next needs a pad, no presets means bending down between songs. That's fine for a lot of players, but check it before you assume this pedal replaces a preset-driven ambient box.

## Stereo, done right this time

True stereo in *and* out across all three matters more than the price. Plenty of budget "stereo" pedals sum the input to mono, which kills anything stereo coming from upstream. If you run a wet/dry or two-amp rig, read [stereo signal chain architecture](/blog/stereo-signal-chain-architecture) before you buy. Then check your patch in mono at front of house, because a dual-engine pedal in parallel mode is exactly the kind of thing that sounds huge in stereo and phase-cancels in mono. Our [stereo width tricks that survive mono](/blog/stereo-width-tricks-that-survive-mono) guide is the check.

## Should you care

If you have a full Helix or a big board with a Strymon or Eventide on it, no. If you're on an HX Stomp, an HX Stomp XL or any eight-block box and you keep running out of DSP whenever you add a harmony or octave block, the VDS-3D might be the cheapest way to free up the chip. We don't have demos, a US price or a ship date yet, so don't preorder based on a parameter list. But this is the first budget dual-engine pitch pedal that's earned a spot on my watch list.
