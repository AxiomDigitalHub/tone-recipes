Line 6 released Helix Stadium Native on August 4, and alongside the paid plugin they did something they have never done in the company's history: they shipped a permanently free license. Not a 15-day trial — that exists separately — but an Intro tier with no expiry, no subscription, and no hardware requirement. Make an account, download it, keep it.

Then the coverage described it as "every amp and effect you can play at zero cost," which is not what it is, and the gap between those two descriptions is worth about 900 words.

## What Is Actually In The Box

Helix Stadium Native Intro contains five amp models with nine channels, five matching cabs, and 16 effects. Here is the complete amp list, because every source I read either summarized it as "five amps" or described it in genre terms without naming anything:

| Intro amp | Cab | Modeled on |
|---|---|---|
| US Luxe Black | 1x12 US Deluxe | Blackface Fender Deluxe Reverb |
| Essex TB30CC | 2x12 Silver Bell | Vox AC30 Top Boost |
| Brit 2203MV | 4x12 Brit V30 | Marshall JCM800 2203 |
| Revv 120 Red | 4x12 1960 T75 | Revv Generator 120, red channel |
| Ampeg SVT-50th Anniversary | Ampeg 810 SVT AV | Ampeg SVT |

Four guitar amps. One bass head. The named effects include Scream 808, Minotaur, Ratatouille, and Dark Dove Fuzz — a Tube Screamer, a Klon, a RAT, and a fuzz — plus modulation, delay, reverb, dynamics, an input gate, and a seven-band parametric EQ to fill out the 16.

The full license runs $399.99 new, or $99.99 if you already own Helix or Stadium hardware, and it opens up 27 Agoura amps at 55 channels, 111 HX amps, 46 cabs, and 274 effects. AAX, AU, VST3, Mac and Windows, 44.1k through 192k.

That is a real free plugin. It is not a crippled demo. But look at the amp table again and notice what is missing.

## Zero HX Legacy Models Is The Entire Story

The full license lists 111 HX amps. The Intro license lists zero. Every model in the free tier is an Agoura model — Line 6's new engine, the one that debuted on Stadium hardware and that I wrote about when [firmware 1.10 added eight amps to it back in March](/news/helix-stadium-firmware-1-10-new-amps).

This is not a technicality. It means the free plugin cannot open your Helix presets. Not "opens them with some models substituted" — the HX model set that Helix, HX Stomp, HX Effects, POD Go, and Helix Native have all shared for a decade is simply not present. Nothing that references a Litigator or a Placater or an Essex A30 has anywhere to land.

Which includes, to be direct about it, every Helix recipe on this site.

I want to be careful not to frame that as a failure. Line 6 is doing something defensible here: seeding a new modeling engine by giving away a slice of it, rather than giving away an old engine they are trying to move people off of. From a platform strategy view it is the correct move. It is just the opposite of what "free Line 6 plugin" makes most people assume, and nobody covering the launch said it out loud.

So the useful question is not "can I run my presets." It is no. The useful question is: given four guitar amps and a Tube Screamer, how much of what we actually publish can you rebuild?

More than I expected.

## The Translation Table

I pulled the HX amp models our Helix content calls for and counted them. The distribution is lopsided in a way that works out well here.

**Essex A30 is the most-used amp model on this entire site by a factor of two.** It appears in more of our Helix write-ups than the next two models combined — it is the spine of nearly every worship recipe we publish, and it shows up constantly outside that lane too. Intro ships **Essex TB30CC**, an AC30 Top Boost, which is the same amp.

That single mapping is why this plugin is worth downloading even though none of our presets load.

Here is the rest of it, ordered by how often we reach for the source model:

| What our recipes call for | Closest Intro amp | Honesty rating |
|---|---|---|
| Essex A30 | Essex TB30CC | Direct hit — same amp, new engine |
| US Double Nrm | US Luxe Black | Close. Deluxe, not Twin — breaks up far earlier |
| Matchstick | Essex TB30CC | Workable. Chimey cousin, less compressed |
| Litigator | US Luxe Black + Minotaur | Rebuild, not a swap. See below |
| US Deluxe Nrm | US Luxe Black | Direct hit |
| Placater Clean | Brit 2203MV, gain low | Rough. Wrong flavor of clean |
| Essex A15 | Essex TB30CC, volume down | Close enough |
| Cali Rectifire | Revv 120 Red | Workable, tighter and more modern |
| Brit J45 | Brit 2203MV, master down | Wrong era — more gain, more mids |
| Revv Gen Purple | Revv 120 Red | Close. Red is tighter and more aggressive |
| Brit Plexi Nrm | Brit 2203MV, master down | Same as J45 — it's a JCM800 pretending |

Two entries need explanation.

**Litigator** is the one that does not translate, and it is the one our blues and ambient content leans on hardest. It is a Dumble-style amp — the compression and the midrange bloom are the point. Intro has no Dumble. The closest thing is US Luxe Black with the Minotaur in front of it, gain around 9 o'clock, output up, which gets you the Klon-into-blackface stack that people have been using as a Dumble substitute for thirty years. It is a different sound. It is a good sound. It is not the Litigator, and if a recipe of ours specifies Litigator with drive at 4.0 you should treat it as a starting direction, not a setting.

**US Double Nrm → US Luxe Black** is the substitution that will trip people up quietly. A Twin stays clean at volumes where a Deluxe is already breaking up. If you build with the Deluxe at the Twin's settings you will get hair you did not ask for. Back the amp Drive down roughly a knob position from whatever the recipe says and push the Channel Volume up to compensate. Our [amp model cheat sheet](/blog/helix-amp-model-cheat-sheet) covers the Twin-versus-Deluxe distinction in more detail if you want the reasoning.

## What This Actually Unlocks, By Reader

**If you play worship and don't own a modeler.** This is the best free thing that has happened to you in a while. The AC30 is the model our [Bethel](/blog/bethel-music-guitar-tone-helix) and [Elevation](/blog/elevation-worship-guitar-tone-helix) recipes are built around, and Intro has it natively along with delay and reverb. You cannot import our presets, but you can follow the settings by hand and land somewhere real. If you have been reading [HX Stomp versus Helix LT](/blog/hx-stomp-vs-helix-lt-worship) trying to decide what to buy, download this first and spend two weeks with it before you spend anything.

**If you already own Helix hardware.** Intro is not for you and will mostly be confusing. It has fewer amps than your unit and won't open your presets. If the Agoura engine interests you, the $99.99 upgrade tier is the honest path — that is a genuinely aggressive price for 138 amps and 274 effects, and it is the same price whether you own a Stadium or a 2016 Helix Floor.

**If you want to hear what Agoura sounds like before committing.** This is the real function of the free tier, and it works. Four amps across blackface, AC30, JCM800, and modern high gain is a well-chosen spread — you can form an opinion about the engine's feel from those alone. Whether Agoura is meaningfully better than HX is a question I am not going to answer from a spec sheet, and now neither of us has to.

## The Proxy Detail Worth Flagging

Intro loads Proxy clones — up to two per preset — and it loads cab IRs. Good.

But you cannot *make* a Proxy clone in the plugin. Clone creation happens on Helix Stadium hardware. So for a free-tier user with no hardware, "Proxy support" means you can play clones other people captured, not capture your own. That is still useful, and it is more than most free plugins offer. It is just a receive-only pipe, and the announcement does not phrase it that way.

The contrast with what is happening on the open-format side is hard to miss. [TONE3000 and Steve Atkinson's NAM Architecture 2](/news/tone3000-nam-architecture-2-a2-open-source-modeling) put out an engine anyone can capture with, on any hardware, in a format that travels — and [HeadRush shipped native support for it two days ago](/news/headrush-5-1-native-nam-tone3000-integration). Proxy is a good-sounding format that lives in Line 6's house. NAM is a format that lives in yours. Both things can be true at once, and if you are choosing a platform in 2026 that distinction is worth more than an amp count.

## What I'd Do

Download it. It costs nothing and Line 6 has committed to no expiry.

Then open the Essex TB30CC, pull up whichever of our AC30-based recipes you were going to build anyway, and dial it by hand instead of loading a preset. You will not get an identical result — different engine, different amp, different cab — and that is fine. What you will find out is whether Agoura's AC30 responds to your pick attack the way HX's does, which is the only question the free tier can actually answer and the one worth asking before anybody spends $399.

Then back up the two settings you liked. Version control the thing before you decide you like it. Otherwise in a month you will remember that it sounded good and have no idea why.
