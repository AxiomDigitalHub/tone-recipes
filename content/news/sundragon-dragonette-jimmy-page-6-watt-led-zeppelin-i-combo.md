The original 50 Sundragon Limited Edition combos were signed by Jimmy Page and hand-wired one at a time in New York. Most of them get treated like documents, not tools. So I read the announcement for the new **Sundragon Dragonette** with real interest. It's the first Sundragon built to get played at a volume a normal person can live with.

## What Sundragon Announced

The **Dragonette** is a **6-watt combo with a single 10-inch speaker**, which Sundragon describes as carefully chosen. Gearnews reported it on November 9. The circuit has two parents:

- **Preamp:** the same one as the **Nymph**, the 1-watt Sundragon that came out last year.
- **Power section:** a scaled-down version of the power section in the original Sundragon. That amp was Page's recreation of the modified late-'50s Supro he used on *Led Zeppelin I*.

Page credits "Mitch and Perry" with the work. That's Mitch Colby, who hand-wired the originals, and producer Perry Margouleff, the same team behind the first Sundragon in 2019. Page calls the Dragonette old meets new: the voice of a vintage amp in a smaller, more modern format.

Sundragon's own site says the amp covers clean tones through overdrive, "from mild to deep," and that it's coming this fall. Neither Sundragon's site nor the Gearnews report gives a tube complement or a control layout. Gearnews lists the price at **$9,000 USD**, ordered direct from Sundragon. Nothing says the run is limited, and there were no demo videos when it was announced.

## The Price, From a Guy Who's Priced These

Nine thousand dollars for a 6-watt 1x10 is not a value proposition, and Sundragon isn't pretending it is. Some context: the 50-piece Limited Edition launched at a reported $12,500 in 2019, and the production Standard Edition followed at about $3,875. The Dragonette costs more than twice the Standard Edition for a fraction of the wattage. You're paying for the name, the hand-wiring, and the claim that this circuit is as close to the *Led Zeppelin I* room as you can buy.

Is it a good buy? If you collect Page artifacts, yes. Signed, small-run Page gear has a long record of holding its value. If you need an amp, no. The tone underneath is a small, early-breaking American combo, and that's one of the best-documented sounds in guitar history.

## How to Cop the Led Zeppelin I Sound on a Modeler

The Dragonette's whole idea is that the magic of a small Supro is power-section breakup at a sane volume. A modeler gives you that at any volume. Here's my starting point on Helix, HX Stomp, or Helix Stadium:

1. **Amp: Soup Pro.** That's Line 6's Supro-inspired model, and it's the right family. Our list of [underrated Helix amp models](/blog/10-helix-amp-models-underrated) ranks it first because it sounds thin at the defaults. Run **Drive around 6–7, Bass 4, Mid 6, Treble 5–6, and push the Master to about 7**, so the power-amp simulation does the compressing the way a cranked 6-watter would. The full lineup, and where Soup Pro sits next to the tweed models, is in our [Helix amp model cheat sheet](/blog/helix-amp-model-cheat-sheet).
2. **Cab: go small.** This matters more than any knob. Load a **1x10 or 1x12 Jensen-style IR** with a dynamic mic slightly off the cap. A 4x12 V30 cab will turn the whole thing into a Marshall impression. Page's original Supro was converted from 2x10 to a single 12, and the Dragonette uses a 10. Try both and keep the one that coughs a little when you dig in.
3. **Guitar: start with a Telecaster bridge pickup.** Page cut most of the first record with his '59 "Dragon" Tele, not the Les Paul people picture. On a humbucker guitar, roll the volume back to about 7 to get closer to that bite.
4. **Fuzz, for the dirtier parts of the record: a Tone Bender in front.** Page's early fuzz was Tone Bender–based, and our [overdrive vs. distortion vs. fuzz guide](/blog/overdrive-vs-distortion-vs-fuzz) explains why it's brighter and nastier than a Fuzz Face. Keep the fuzz level modest and let the Soup Pro add the rest.
5. **Room, not plate.** Use a short room reverb at low mix. Most of the size on that record came from the studio and the mic placement, not from a reverb tank.

On Quad Cortex, the same rules apply: pick a small Class A or Supro-style capture and pair it with a small-speaker IR. Pairing it with a big cab is the one mistake that will ruin this tone on any platform.

## Should You Care

If you're going to own one Page amp in your life and you have the money, the Dragonette may be the one you'd actually play. Six watts is a real living-room and studio level, and that makes it the first Sundragon most owners will actually crank.

If you want the sound of *Led Zeppelin I*, you already have the tools. A small Supro model, a small cab, a Telecaster, and a Tone Bender for the dirty parts. Most of the rest is in how Page attacked the strings, and no amp sells that.
