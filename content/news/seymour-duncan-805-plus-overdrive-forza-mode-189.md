Seymour Duncan announced the 805+ Overdrive on November 17. It's the 805 circuit, the company's green, mid-forward take on a Tube Screamer, with a second voice added. A top-mounted toggle chooses between them. Switch down gives you the original 805. Switch up gives you Plus mode, whose voicing comes from Seymour Duncan's Forza overdrive.

Street price is $189. It's in stock at Seymour Duncan now.

## The Spec Sheet

- **Controls:** Drive, Level, and an active three-band EQ (Bass, Midrange, Treble), plus the 805/Plus mode toggle
- **805 mode:** the original mid-forward "green" overdrive
- **Plus mode:** a full-frequency overdrive with more gain and sustain, voiced from the Forza
- **Clean boost:** about 8dB of gain with Drive all the way down
- **Power:** 9V battery or 9V–18V DC
- **Switching:** true bypass, top-mounted jacks

## Why Two Voices Is the Real Story

Almost every overdrive on a pedalboard sits on one side of a single question: do you want the mids pushed forward, or do you want the guitar to stay itself, only louder and hairier?

A Tube Screamer-family drive picks mids. It cuts low end before the clipping stage and leans on the upper midrange, which is why a TS tightens a cranked amp and helps a lead cut through a band mix. The cost is that chords lose weight and a neck pickup can start to sound nasal. The original 805 was always in that family. Its active EQ (a real Bass/Mid/Treble stack rather than a single tone knob) just gave you more room to steer the hump.

The Forza went the other way. It's open and even, with more low and high end left in. That's the kind of drive you want when your amp already sounds right and you only need it pushed harder.

Most players solve this by owning two pedals. The 805+ puts both on one footprint with one shared EQ. The tradeoff is that you're choosing a voice, not stacking two. One toggle, one footswitch: you can't run 805 into Plus the way you'd stack a TS into a transparent drive.

## Where Each Mode Earns Its Keep

**805 mode** is the one for an amp that's already breaking up. Keep Drive low, Level high, and use it as the TS-style tightener in front of a Marshall-flavored channel. The [Tube Screamer before a high-gain amp](/blog/tube-screamer-before-high-gain-amp) guide covers why this works. The low-cut before clipping keeps palm mutes from going woolly. The active Midrange is the control to watch. Turning it down from noon gets you closer to a TS9 with a smoother top. Turning it up gets you the 808-style honk that solos ride on.

**Plus mode** is the one for a clean or edge-of-breakup amp: a Deluxe Reverb, an AC-style chime platform, or the clean amp block in a worship patch. Because it doesn't throw away low end, it holds up better under full chords and humbuckers. If you've read our [TS vs. Klon vs. Blues Driver](/blog/tube-screamer-vs-klon-vs-blues-driver) comparison, Plus sits closer to the Blues Driver and transparent-drive end of that spectrum than to the green box.

**Drive all the way down** in either mode gives you that roughly 8dB clean boost. That's enough to push a tube amp's front end for a solo without adding the pedal's own clipping. The [clean boost vs. overdrive vs. volume pedal](/blog/clean-boost-vs-overdrive-vs-volume-pedal-solo-boost) guide walks through when that's the better move.

One more practical note: 9V to 18V. At 18V you get more headroom before the pedal's own clipping, so the drive sounds less compressed and the clean boost sounds cleaner. If you use Plus mode as an always-on "amp-but-more" pedal, try both.

## Getting Both Voices on a Helix or HX Stomp

You don't need the pedal to chase either sound in a modeler, because both voices already have close stand-ins in the Helix drive list:

- **805 mode → Scream 808.** Set Gain low (around 2–3), Level high, and Tone near noon. Then use a Parametric EQ block after it to rebuild what the 805's active EQ does, since the 808 model has a single Tone knob. A gentle mid bump around 700–800Hz gets you the "green" push. Our [Tube Screamer settings guide](/blog/tube-screamer-settings-guide) has starting points for each amp family.
- **Plus mode → Teemah! or Horizon Drive.** Both are full-range drives that keep the amp's lows in place. Teemah! has its own bass and treble cut controls, which work like a simplified version of Plus mode's EQ.
- **Snapshot trick:** Put the Scream 808 and the full-range drive on the same path. Make a snapshot with only the 808 active, and another with only the full-range drive active. Now you have the 805+'s toggle on a footswitch, with no gap between voices.

On Quad Cortex, the TS808 model plays the 805 role. Then pick a low-compression transparent drive for the Plus role, and use the same scene-based switching.

## The Bigger Picture

This is the second Seymour Duncan story on Fader & Knob in a week. The [Frankenstein EVH stripe pickups](/news/seymour-duncan-frankenstein-evh-stripe-finishes-classic-vs-plus) also used a "classic vs. Plus" split. The 805+ follows the same pattern of keeping the original voice and adding a second one beside it.

At $189 with an active EQ, 18V headroom, and two real voices, the 805+ is a strong one-pedal answer to the mid-forward vs. full-range question. If you already own a TS and a transparent drive, you've solved this problem already. If you're starting a board, or you're trying to free up a slot, it's worth a look.
