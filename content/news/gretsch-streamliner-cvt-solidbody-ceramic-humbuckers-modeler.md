Say "Gretsch" and everyone pictures the same thing: a big orange hollowbody, Bigsby wobble, Brian Setzer twang, that jangly Filter'Tron chime. Country, rockabilly, the Cure. Nobody pictures palm-muted chugs.

So the new **Gretsch Streamliner CVT** is a bit of a troll, and I mean that as the highest compliment. It's a **solid mahogany body**, a **bolt-on neck**, and **two high-output ceramic humbuckers**, and Gretsch is aiming it dead-center at rock and metal players for **$278**. It is the least Gretsch-sounding Gretsch they've ever built. I love a company that's willing to argue with its own brand.

## What It Actually Is

Released **July 23**. Here's the spec sheet, minus the marketing:

- **Body:** solid mahogany, double-cut, beveled contours and a rounded heel for upper-fret access
- **Neck:** bolt-on mahogany, Gretsch's "Fast 'C'" profile, 12"-radius laurel board, 22 medium-jumbo frets
- **Pickups:** dual **HotWire humbuckers** — high-output, **ceramic** magnets, vacuum wax-potted
- **Controls:** master volume, master tone, three-way toggle. That's it.
- **Bridge:** compensated wraparound, sealed die-cast chrome tuners
- **Finishes:** Jet Black, Sea Foam Green, Aged White, Olive Drab
- **$278 / £249 / €289.** No lefty, which is the one genuine bummer.

Read that pickup line again, because it's the whole story. **Ceramic. High-output. Wax-potted.** Those three words tell you this thing was voiced to be slammed into gain, not to jangle. A wraparound bridge and a bolt-on neck keep the cost down and, honestly, keep the thing sensible — this is a working guitar and a fantastic modding platform, not a museum piece.

## Why This Matters If You Play Direct

Here's the part the press release skips, and it's the part that actually changes how this guitar behaves through your rig: **ceramic high-output humbuckers hit the front end of your amp block completely differently than the pickups you probably A/B your presets with.**

Most of us build and audition modeler presets on whatever's in reach — a Strat, a Tele, an alnico-loaded Les Paul. Those are lower-output and more open. A **ceramic** humbucker is hotter, more compressed, and tighter in the low end before it even reaches your input. Drop this guitar in front of a preset you love and three things happen at once:

1. **Your amp block gains up.** The hotter signal pushes the virtual preamp harder, so a patch that was "edge of breakup" on your Tele is suddenly in full crunch. Same block, more dirt, because the guitar is doing the pushing.
2. **The low end tightens — then flubs.** Ceramic magnets are punchy and fast, which is *great* for palm mutes. But cranked into a high-gain model, that punch stacks with the amp's own low end and the chugs can go to oatmeal fast.
3. **The top end gets brittle.** This is the ceramic tax. That extra output and treble bite that makes the pickup cut in a band mix can read as ice-pick and fizz through a modeler with the presence and treble where you'd normally set them.

None of that is a flaw in the guitar. It's just a different tool, and you dial it differently.

## Where To Set The Knobs

I ran this same logic building high-gain patches for hotter pickups, and it comes down to four moves:

1. **Pull the amp block's input gain (or drive) back a notch.** You're feeding it a hotter signal, so you need *less* of the amp's own gain to land in the same place. Chasing more gain on a ceramic humbucker is how you end up with mush. Not sure which block is which amp? The [Helix Amp Model Cheat Sheet](/blog/helix-amp-model-cheat-sheet) maps every model to the real amp so you're not guessing.
2. **Tighten the front end with a Tube Screamer, not a RAT.** For a hot humbucker into high gain, a TS-style pedal with the drive low and level up is the classic low-end tamer — it firms up the palm mutes before they hit the preamp. That [TS-into-a-Marshall stacking move](/blog/stacking-ts-and-klon-into-marshall) is exactly the trick, and it matters *more* with ceramic pickups than with the single-coils that guide was written around.
3. **Roll treble and presence down before you touch anything else.** The ceramic ice-pick lives up there. Start with treble and presence a couple notches lower than your Strat preset and bring them back up only if it goes dull.
4. **Use the neck pickup's warmth as your clean channel.** A ceramic bridge humbucker is a one-trick chug machine — a good trick. The neck pickup is where you'll find the rounded, thicker cleans, so build your clean patch around it, not the bridge.

Do that and the CVT turns into a genuinely tight, aggressive rhythm guitar into any modeler — the *opposite* of everything the Gretsch logo on the headstock is telling your brain to expect.

## Should You Buy It

If you want an affordable, honest solidbody built for gain and you don't care that it says Gretsch on a shape that doesn't look like a Gretsch, $278 for solid mahogany and two hot humbuckers is a legitimately good deal. It's a great first "real" electric, a great backup, and a great mod platform if you ever want to drop alnico pickups in and civilize it.

Just go in knowing what you're buying: this is not the guitar that makes the twang. It's the guitar that chugs, and into a modeler it drives hot and bright — so build the patch *for* the pickup instead of dropping it into a preset voiced for something softer and wondering why it bites. If you've only ever heard the difference described, [I've made the case for why the guitar in front of the modeler matters as much as the block](/blog/modeler-vs-tube-amp-shootout) more than once. This is a $278 way to feel it.
