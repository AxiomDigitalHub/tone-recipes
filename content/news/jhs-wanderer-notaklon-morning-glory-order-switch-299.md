JHS released **the Wanderer** today. It's a dual overdrive that puts two existing JHS circuits side by side: the **NOTAKLÖN**, JHS's Klon-style design, and the **Morning Glory V4**, its Bluesbreaker-style one. It's **$299**, listed at Sweetwater, with Thomann stock "soon," per [Gearnews](https://www.gearnews.com/jhs-wanderer-the-ultimate-dual-drive/).

At first glance this looks like "two pedals you might already own, in one box." The **ORDER** toggle is what makes it more than that.

## What's on It

**NOTAKLÖN side (left):** Volume, Gain, Treble. JHS quotes up to 16 dB of clean boost. A **Shamrock Mod** switch adds +4 dB of gain, more midrange, and an extra hard-clipping stage.

**Morning Glory V4 side (right):** Volume, Drive, Tone, a **Gain** toggle, and a **Bright Cut** switch.

**Shared:**
- An **ORDER** toggle sets which side hits first.
- **Each side has its own input and output jacks**, all top-mounted, so you can split the two circuits and put something between them.
- **Red Remote jacks** let you switch both gain toggles with your feet.
- Silent soft-touch switching with **buffered bypass**.

None of the coverage I found lists power requirements. Check the manual before you plan your power supply around it.

## Why the Order Switch Matters More Than Either Circuit

These two circuits do different jobs. A Klon-style pedal is mostly a **clean-boost-plus-a-little-hair** circuit with a mid push, and it shines when it's feeding something. A Bluesbreaker-style pedal is a **low-gain, flat-ish, amp-like drive**, the "sounds like the amp got a little louder" kind. Stack them, and whichever one comes first becomes the input conditioning for the other:

- **Klon → Bluesbreaker:** the Klon side hits the Morning Glory harder and with more mids, so the second pedal does the clipping and the Klon shapes it. You get a thicker, more compressed lead tone that keeps the Bluesbreaker's open top end.
- **Bluesbreaker → Klon:** the Morning Glory provides the grit, and the Klon acts like a mid-forward volume boost after it. The result is louder and more "out front," with less added compression. It's a solo boost on top of a rhythm tone.

People argue about which order is correct. There is no correct order. They're two different results, and the right one depends on your amp. That's what I like about JHS putting the switch on top: it makes you try both.

## Settle It on Your Modeler Before You Buy

You can run the experiment tonight. On Helix, the **Minotaur** is the Klon model. For the Bluesbreaker side, use your platform's lowest-gain, flattest "transparent" drive. Different platforms model different pedals here, so pick by behavior, not by name. You want a drive that adds a little grit without a big mid hump.

1. **Pick one amp and keep it on the edge of breakup.** Set it so a hard pick attack just barely grits. Everything below depends on the amp sitting right there.
2. **Build drive A → drive B, then duplicate the preset and swap the order.** Change nothing else.
3. **Level-match the two presets** with the [preset level-matching method](/blog/level-match-modeler-presets). The stacking order changes output level, and the louder preset wins every blind comparison. Level-match or the test proves nothing.
4. **Play the same phrase through both at the volume you gig at.** Listen to the note decay and to how much the guitar's volume knob cleans things up, not to the first second of the attack.

If you mostly prefer one order, you've learned something useful: you don't need the switch, and two separate pedals (or two blocks) will do. If you went back and forth depending on the song, the Wanderer's ORDER toggle is solving a problem you actually have. Our [Klon settings guide](/blog/klon-centaur-settings-guide) and [stacking a TS and a Klon into a Marshall](/blog/stacking-ts-and-klon-into-marshall) cover the gain-staging logic in more detail. [TS vs. Klon vs. Blues Driver](/blog/tube-screamer-vs-klon-vs-blues-driver) is the right place to start if you've never pinned down what the Klon is doing in your chain.

## The Separate Jacks Are the Underrated Spec

Four jacks instead of two means you can put **something between the two drives**: a compressor, a wah, or the input of your modeler. If you run a modeler with an FX loop or a send/return block, you can feed the NOTAKLÖN into the modeler's input, send out to the Morning Glory, and return after it. That puts modeled pre-drive effects (a compressor, a filter) between two analog drives. It's also the [four-cable method](/blog/4-wire-method-explained) on a small scale. Watch your levels at each jack. A Klon-style pedal with 16 dB of boost will clip a modeler's input if the input pad is set for a passive guitar.

## The Shamrock Switch Changes Your Gain Structure

The Shamrock Mod adds hard clipping on top of the extra gain and mids. In practice it moves the NOTAKLÖN side from "boost that drives the next thing" toward "a drive in its own right." When it's engaged, the order question can flip. A Shamrock'd Klon first may already be clipping hard enough that the Morning Glory behind it mostly adds volume and color. If an order you liked starts sounding congested, try switching the Shamrock off before blaming the order.

## Where I Land

At $299 it's not a budget purchase, and if you already own a Klon-style pedal and a Bluesbreaker-style pedal you can get both orders with a patch cable. The Wanderer makes sense if you switch orders between songs, want both on one footprint, or want the Red Remote to change gain modes mid-song without bending down. Run the modeler test first. It'll tell you in an evening whether the order switch is worth paying for.
