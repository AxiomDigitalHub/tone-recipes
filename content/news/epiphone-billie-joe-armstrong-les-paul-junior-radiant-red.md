Yesterday I wrote about Marshall's $3,999 baby-blue Dookie Plexi and spent 1,500 words arguing you could cop the tone on a modeler for free. Today Epiphone hands me the *other* half of that rig — the guitar — for **$545**. So I get to keep arguing, except now the math is even better and I get to talk about pickups, which is my favorite fight.

Full disclosure again, because it'd be dishonest to hide it: *Dookie* is why I play guitar. A single-P-90 Les Paul Junior is basically my personal platonic ideal of an instrument. I run Lollar P-90s in my Jazzmaster for a reason. So read the skepticism knowing the enthusiasm is real too.

## What It Actually Is

A **Billie Joe Armstrong signature Les Paul Junior** in a new **Radiant Red** finish. Slab **mahogany body**, set **mahogany neck** with a chunky '50s profile, Indian laurel board, 22 frets, 24.75" scale. One **Epiphone P-90 PRO dogear** in the bridge. A **wraparound Lightning Bar bridge** mounted straight to the body. Master volume, master tone, CTS pots, Graph Tech nut. It ships in a black hardshell case with — I'm not making this up — a **faux leopard-print furry interior**.

**$545 / £488 / €569.** Available now, limited run. That's the Epiphone; the Gibson USA version of Billie Joe's Junior exists too and costs roughly three times this.

That's the whole spec sheet. And the shortness of it *is* the point — there is nothing to explain because there is nothing to switch. No coil taps, no five-way, no neck pickup to forget you left selected. You plug in, you turn the one knob up, you play. I've written a whole nerdy love letter to exactly this design decision in [Why a One-Pickup Guitar Makes You a Better Player](/blog/single-pickup-guitar-logic), and a slab-mahogany P-90 Junior is the thesis statement.

## Why a P-90 Matters More Than the Signature

Here's where the modeler players need to lean in, because this is the part that actually changes how the thing sounds through your rig — and it's the part the press release skips.

A P-90 is a **single-coil**, but it is not a Strat single-coil. It's fatter, hotter, and thicker in the low-mids — a wider, hotter coil that reads more like the angry middle child between a Strat pickup and a humbucker. **That extra output and midrange is why a P-90 into an amp *feels* like it's already halfway to breaking up.** It hits the front end of your amp block harder than the Strat or Tele you probably A/B your presets with, so the exact same Plexi model will bite, compress, and gain up noticeably more with this guitar in front of it.

That's a feature, not a warning — it's *the* punk-rhythm sound — but it means you don't dial a P-90 like a Strat. If your modeler presets were built around a single-coil neck-position clean, this guitar will shove them into breakup you didn't ask for. I broke down what P-90 output does to a gain stage — and how to keep it from turning to mush — in [P-90s Into Overdrive: Taming the Fat Single-Coil](/blog/p90-pickups-overdrive). Worth two minutes before you plug this into a preset you love and wonder why it suddenly sounds meaner.

## Building the Whole Dookie Rig In Your Modeler

So now you've got both halves. Yesterday was [the amp](/news/marshall-1959bja-billie-joe-armstrong-dookie-plexi); today's the guitar. Here's the complete signal chain, on any modeler, at PA volume, for the cost of stuff you already own:

1. **Guitar: P-90 bridge, volume on 10.** One pickup, wide open. That's the Junior's entire user manual.
2. **A boost/dirt in front of the amp block.** For the *Dookie* snarl I reach for a **RAT** every time — the [RAT settings guide](/blog/rat-pedal-settings-guide) has a punk starting point. But here's the P-90 adjustment: because the pickup is already hot, **back the pedal's gain down a notch** from where you'd set it for a Strat. You're topping off a tank that's already half full. Let the pickup do the work and the pedal do the tightening.
3. **Amp: a bright Plexi model, pushed.** On Helix that's the **Brit Plexi Nrm/Brt**; every platform has one. Not sure which block is the Plexi on your unit? The [Helix Amp Model Cheat Sheet](/blog/helix-amp-model-cheat-sheet) maps every block to the real amp. Drive around 7, presence up, and let that P-90 midrange bloom into the breakup.
4. **Watch your low end.** The one place a hot P-90 into a cranked Plexi gets you is flub. If the palm mutes turn to oatmeal, a Tube Screamer tightens the front end better than a RAT — [stacking a TS into a Marshall](/blog/stacking-ts-and-klon-into-marshall) is the clean-up move.
5. **Cab: a 1960A-style 4x12, SM57 slightly off-center.** Bright, not ice-picky.

Run that direct to a PA and you've got the *Basket Case* sound at any volume — the one thing the real $3,999 non-master Plexi physically cannot do quietly. This is my actual live rig on a four-minute soundcheck, minus the leopard case.

## Should You Buy It

If you want a punk guitar that will teach you more about your right hand than any pickup swap ever will, a single-P-90 Junior is one of the great honest instruments, and $545 for a slab-mahogany one with a real bone-simple wraparound bridge is a genuinely fair price. The Radiant Red is loud in the good way. Buy it and turn it up.

If you're a collector who wants Billie Joe's name and can't stomach that it says Epiphone on the headstock instead of Gibson, the USA version is there for triple the money — and honestly, for the *tone*, into a modeler, I couldn't tell you which one you're hearing. The P-90 doesn't know what's printed on the headstock. Neither does your amp block.

Either way: the guitar that made a ten-year-old on a $99 Squier want to play was never four grand, and it was never even $545. It was one pickup, turned up, into something breaking up. Now you can just buy the guitar that says so on the truss rod cover.
