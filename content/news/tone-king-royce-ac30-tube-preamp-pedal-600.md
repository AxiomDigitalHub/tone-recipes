An AC30 is a beautiful amplifier and a miserable thing to own. It's heavy, it runs hot, and it only sounds like itself when it's loud enough to bother the neighbors. In twenty-five years behind a counter I watched plenty of them come in on trade from players whose backs gave out before their love for the sound did. Tone King's new **Royce** is aimed squarely at those players. It puts the preamp on the floor and lets you send it straight to the board.

Guitar Pedal X covered the Royce on November 16, following a preview at Guitar Summit earlier this autumn.

## What it is

The Royce is a **two-channel, AC30-style tube preamp pedal with a DI**. The spec sheet:

- **Tubes:** three 12AX7s running at high voltage, not a starved-plate 9V circuit
- **Channels:** two, each with Treble, Middle, Bass, Volume, Cut, a Top Boost switch, a three-position cab selector, and its own attenuation control. Tone King's IRONMAN attenuator appears twice, once per channel.
- **Power amp:** a "zero-watt" power amp simulation with a phase inverter. HF compression, low-pass, and high-pass filters can be edited per channel in Tone King's SYNERGY software.
- **Effects:** a digital stereo tremolo (Depth, Speed) and a spring reverb (Level, Dwell), each assignable per channel
- **Cab sim:** low-latency IR loader with 20 OwnHammer IRs, bypassable
- **Outputs:** dual balanced stereo XLR with ground lift, a 1/4" main out, a TRS headphone out, and a stereo series effects loop
- **Control:** 5-pin MIDI with up to 128 presets that recall channel, bypass, IR, loop, and reverb state. USB-C for the editor.
- **Size and power:** 183 × 145 × 70 mm, 9–12V DC at 800mA. Plan your power supply around that 800mA. Most isolated outputs won't cover it.
- **Price:** $600 / £600. It's in stock in the US now, with European stock expected shortly.

The coverage doesn't say which AC30 generation the circuit follows, and Tone King hasn't said either. "AC30-style" with a Top Boost switch on both channels tells you where they're aiming. This is the Top Boost sound, the bright, scooped jangle of the Shadows and the Beatles that later became U2's, not the darker Normal channel.

## Who should buy one

If you play a modeler, the Royce isn't a replacement. It's a specialist. It makes sense in three cases:

1. **You want real tubes and a direct rig.** Plenty of players on in-ear monitors miss how a preamp tube compresses under the pick. The Royce gives you that response and still sends a stereo, cab-simmed signal to the board.
2. **You already run a pedalboard into the house.** With the loop, MIDI, and XLR outs, the Royce can be the amp at the center of a pedalboard rig. Your drives go in front, your time effects go in the loop.
3. **AC30 is your sound and nothing else is.** If you spend 90% of your night on one amp, $600 for that amp in pedal form is fair money next to an AC30 combo and a load box.

If you jump between AC30, Plexi, and a Fender clean in the same set, a modeler still makes more sense. You can get the Royce's job done on the Helix you already own.

## The Helix version: Essex A30

Helix's **Essex A30** is its Vox AC30 Top Boost model. Here's a starting point that gets close to the Royce's sweet spot. It's the same family of settings we use across our AC30 recipes.

- **Amp: Essex A30.** Drive 5.0, Bass 4.5, Mid 6.0, Treble 6.5, Cut 3.5, Presence 5.0, Ch Vol to taste, Master 7.0
- **Cab: 2x12 Blue Bell** (Celestion Alnico Blue), dynamic mic around 1–2 inches off the cap. Our [Alnico Blue vs Greenback piece](/blog/alnico-blue-vs-greenback-ac30-worship) explains why the Blue is the right call for chime.
- **After the amp:** an Optical Trem and a '63 Spring reverb, the same order the Royce uses

The **Cut** control is where most people go wrong. On an AC30 it works backward: turning it up takes treble away. If the Essex sounds glassy and harsh, try more Cut before you touch Treble. Our [AC30 Cut knob explainer](/blog/vox-ac30-cut-knob-explained) covers it in detail, and the [AC30 settings guide](/blog/vox-ac30-settings-guide) covers the rest of the panel.

To hear the Top Boost sound in context, three of our recipes are built on the Essex A30. [The Edge's "Where the Streets Have No Name"](/recipe/edge-where-the-streets-have-no-name) shows the chime with dotted-eighth delay. [Brian May's "Bohemian Rhapsody"](/recipe/brian-may-bohemian-rhapsody) shows what happens when you push it with a treble booster. [Way Maker](/recipe/moore-way-maker) shows the clean worship version. Every setting in those recipes is a starting point for dialing in the Royce too. Its Treble, Middle, Bass, and Cut follow the same layout as the amp.

## My take

People have been building AC30 preamps in a box since the Vox Valvetronix days. The Royce is the first I've seen that gets real tubes, a proper DI, MIDI recall, and stereo modulation into one pedal at a price that doesn't need a second mortgage. Whether three 12AX7s and a simulated power section get you the sag of a cranked pair of EL84s is a question for the first independent reviews, and none are out yet. If EL84 breakup is what you're chasing, read our [EL84 tube character piece](/blog/el84-tube-character-ac30-boutique) before you order. That's where an AC30's personality really lives.
