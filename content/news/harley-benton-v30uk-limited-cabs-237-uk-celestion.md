Thomann dropped three limited-run **Harley Benton V30UK** cabinets on **October 8**, all loaded with **UK-made Celestion Vintage 30s**:

| Model | Config | Power / impedance | Weight | Price |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| G112Plus LTD V30UK | 1x12 | 60 W @ 8 Ω | 10.6 kg | **$237 / €249** |
| G212Plus LTD V30UK | 2x12, angled baffle | 120 W @ 4 Ω mono, 60 W @ 8 Ω stereo | 19.7 kg | **$379 / €399** |
| G212VPlus Vertical LTD V30UK | vertical 2x12 | **120 W @ 16 Ω** | — | **$379 / €399** |

Construction is the usual Harley Benton Plus recipe: 18 mm Falcata core, meranti back panels, plywood front, black Tolex, metal corners, removable grille cloth, rubber feet. The horizontal 2x12 gets an optional three-piece rear baffle so you can run it open, closed, or half-and-half. The vertical gets a slanted upper front panel and a partial rear opening.

This is not a new idea for Harley Benton. They already sell the **G112Plus Greenback** (UK-made Celestion G12M-25) and the **G112Plus Creamback** (UK-made G12M-65). The formula is the same every time: Thomann's own cabinet, somebody else's real speaker, a price that makes you check it twice. The V30 is just the flavor everyone was waiting for, and this time it is a limited run.

So let me do the part nobody in the coverage is doing, which is the arithmetic.

## The Cabinet Costs Seventy Dollars

A bare UK-made Celestion Vintage 30 is **$160 directly from Mesa/Boogie's parts store** — and Mesa specifically stocks the UK-made version, which matters in a second. US retail on a standard V30 runs about **$169**. In the UK you are looking at **£110–£125**.

Subtract that from $237 and the box, the Tolex, the corners, the grille, the jack plate and the shipping are costing you somewhere around **$70**.

I have built cabs. $70 does not build that cab. You cannot buy 18 mm sheet goods, Tolex, hardware and a baffle for $70 and have anything left over for your time. So the honest read on this announcement is: **Harley Benton is selling you a UK V30 at roughly parts cost and giving you the cabinet.** That is a genuinely good deal, and I say that as someone who spends a lot of words telling people not to buy things.

Which is why I want to be really clear about who should and should not be clicking buy, because I think a bunch of modeler players are about to.

## Your Modeler Already Has Five of These

If you own a Helix, HX Stomp, Helix LT, Helix Native or POD Go, you do not have "a V30 cab." You have **five** of them, and two of them are a format match for what Harley Benton just announced:

| Helix cab | Real cabinet | Speaker |
|---|---|---|
| **1x12 Recto** | Mesa/Boogie Rectifier 1x12 | **Celestion V30** |
| **2x12 Stealth** | Friedman Runt 2x12 | **Celestion V30** |
| 4x12 Brit V30 | Marshall 1960AX | Celestion V30 |
| 4x12 Recto | Mesa Rectifier Standard 4x12 | Celestion V30 |
| 4x12 XXL V30 | Bogner Uberkab 4x12 | Celestion V30 |

The full decoder for every stock cabinet, including what the fictionalized names actually map to, is in our [Helix cab models decoded](/blog/helix-cab-models-decoded) guide. If you want a 1x12 UK V30 sound and you own any Helix-family device, **1x12 Recto** is the block, and it costs zero dollars.

So the question is not "do I want a V30." You have had a V30 the entire time. The question is what a physical cab gives you that the block does not, and that answer is narrower and weirder than most people expect.

## What You Actually Buy With a Real Cab

Not frequency response. That is the part people get wrong. A good V30 IR is a measurement of a real V30 in a real cabinet, and across the range where a guitar lives, a decent IR and the real thing are closer than any forum thread will admit.

What you buy is three things:

**1. No mic, and therefore no mic decision.** Every IR in your modeler is a speaker *plus a microphone at one position*. That mic is baked in permanently. When you sit in front of a real cab, you are hearing the whole cone across its full dispersion pattern, not an SM57 two inches off the dust cap. That is a real difference and it is the biggest one. It is also the thing a V30 IR cannot give you by definition, no matter how good the capture was. We went through this in [cab IRs vs a real cab in an untreated room](/blog/cab-irs-vs-real-cab-bedroom-room-treatment), and the honest finding there holds: the real cab wins in the room and loses the second a mic goes in front of it.

**2. Air moving at your legs.** Which is a feel thing, not a tone thing, and I am not going to pretend it is not real. It changes how hard you play. It also does not reach the audience.

**3. The rear baffle, if you buy the horizontal 2x12.** That three-piece panel is the most underrated spec in this announcement. Open-back and closed-back are not an EQ difference — the low-frequency behavior changes because the cabinet loading changes, and [open-back vs closed-back is a real acoustic difference, not a tone-knob one](/blog/open-back-vs-closed-back-cab-tone). A removable panel means you own both cabs. Your modeler, meanwhile, has them as two separate IRs you have to pick between, and you cannot blend them without phase problems.

And here is what you give up, which the press release will never tell you: **your tone stops being the same every night.** I do live sound three nights a week. The band that walks in with a real cab sounds different in my room than it did in their practice space, every single time, and we spend soundcheck on it. The band that walks in direct sounds the same in both. I soundcheck in four minutes because of this. That is not a small trade.

## The UK vs China Fight You Already Lost

The reason "V30UK" is in the product name is that Celestion builds Vintage 30s in both the UK and China, and guitarists have been arguing about the difference for roughly twenty years.

The forum consensus, roughly: UK ones are darker, smoother, hold composure at volume, take longer to break in. Chinese ones are brighter, more extended on top, break up sooner, and get called fizzy or nasal. The measurement threads are less dramatic than the prose threads — the published frequency comparisons show a real difference, but it is a couple of dB in a narrow band, and plenty of people who have both in the same cab report they would not pick it blind. Mesa reportedly specifies UK-made for their stock V30s, which is at least a data point that someone with a budget thinks it matters.

I genuinely do not know who is right, and I am not going to pretend to from a laptop in Philadelphia.

But here is the part that is actually useful to you, and it is the reason I wanted to write this:

**If you play through a modeler, you never got to make this choice.** Every V30 IR in your device was captured from one specific speaker, on one specific day, from one specific production run. Line 6 does not publish which. Neither does anyone else. The "Brit V30" in your Helix came from a particular 1960AX with four particular speakers in it, and whoever loaded that cabinet made the UK/China call for you, permanently, in about 2014.

So when someone on Reddit tells you your modeler's V30 sounds fizzy compared to their real one — that is not a digital-versus-analog argument. **That is possibly a UK-versus-China argument wearing a costume.** Which is kind of hilarious, and which is also unfalsifiable from where you are sitting, so the correct amount of time to spend on it is zero.

If you buy this cab, you will know what you have. That is a legitimate thing to want. It is just not a tone upgrade you can predict.

## Two Spec Traps That Will Cost You a Speaker

This is the part I actually care about, because both of these are in the announcement and neither is in the coverage.

**Trap one: 60 watts means 60 watts.** The 1x12 is rated **60 W at 8 Ω**, because a Vintage 30 is a 60-watt speaker and there is one of them in there. Most people buying a cab for a modeler are driving it with a class-D power amp — and the popular ones put out 200, 350, even 700 watts into 8 Ω. A 60-watt speaker fed by a 350-watt power amp is a speaker you are going to replace, and it will not fail politely. It will get progressively worse over a few gigs while you blame your preset.

If you go this route, watch your output stage, and understand that a modeler's amp block clipping and a power amp clipping are two completely different failures that sound similar through a speaker — [preamp vs power amp distortion and how to actually tell them apart](/blog/preamp-vs-power-amp-distortion-how-to-hear) is the distinction that saves you here.

**Trap two: the vertical 2x12 is 16 ohms only.** Read that row in the table again. The horizontal 2x12 gives you 4 Ω mono or 8 Ω stereo. The **vertical gives you one option: 120 W at 16 Ω.** A lot of affordable solid-state power amps are specified for 4 and 8 Ω and will deliver roughly *half* their rated power into 16 Ω, sometimes less. That is not dangerous, it is just disappointing, and you will be sitting there at full master wondering why it will not keep up with the drummer. If you already own the power amp, check its 16 Ω rating before you order the vertical.

## If You Buy It, Three Things Change in Your Preset

Assume you did it. You have the 1x12, a power amp, and an HX Stomp. Here is what has to change, and people skip all three.

**Turn the cab block off.** Not "turn it down." Off. A real speaker is already the speaker; running an IR into it stacks two cabinets and you get the comb-filtered mush that makes people conclude modelers sound bad. This is more involved than flipping one switch, because the rest of your preset was voiced around that block being there — the full rebuild is in [disabling the cab IR and revoicing the preset](/blog/disable-cab-ir-for-voiced-frfr-preset-rebuild).

**Delete your High Cut, then rebuild it from scratch if you still need it.** This is the one I want to put in bold. That High Cut you have at 6.5 kHz was not fixing your amp. **It was fixing your microphone.** Close-miked IRs carry a top-end spike that a real speaker in a real room does not produce, because a real speaker's high-frequency output beams straight forward and falls off hard off-axis, and your ears are not two inches off the dust cap. Keep the old cut and you will have a dull cab and no idea why. Start at no cut at all and only add one if the room tells you to.

**Re-dial your amp block's Master, and then re-dial your gain.** Your Master setting was compensating for a speaker that could not distort. Now it can. And on Helix the amp block runs a **0–10 scale, not 0–1** — if you have been typing decimals, that is a separate and much larger problem. Our [Helix amp model cheat sheet](/blog/helix-amp-model-cheat-sheet) has the starting gain for every model if you are rebuilding from zero.

## Who Should Buy This

**Buy it** if you already play through a real amp or a power amp into a cab, and you want a UK V30 1x12 for less than a bare UK V30 plus an empty box. That is a straightforward win and the limited run means it will not be there in March. If you are in the EU it is even easier, because you are not paying transatlantic freight on 10.6 kg of plywood.

**Buy the horizontal 2x12 instead of the vertical** unless you have specifically confirmed your power amp is happy into 16 Ω. The removable three-piece baffle is worth more than the slanted top panel.

**Do not buy it** if you currently go direct and the plan is "a real cab will fix my tone." It will fix the one thing an IR structurally cannot — the mic — and it will hand you back a power amp to buy, 23 pounds to carry, a tone that changes room to room, and a soundcheck that takes twenty minutes instead of four. I run a Jazzmaster into an HX Stomp with no amp on stage and I am not changing that for a 2 dB argument about cone paper. If the goal is sounding better in the room, the honest comparison is [FRFR versus a real guitar cab for modeler players](/blog/frfr-vs-guitar-cab-for-modelers), and there is a [sub-$500 FRFR roundup](/blog/sub-500-frfr-roundup-stage-right-behringer-alto) that will get you further for the same money.

**Do not buy it to settle the UK/China thing.** You will own one UK V30 and zero Chinese V30s, which is not an experiment, it is a purchase.

What I would actually want to see is somebody with the G112Plus LTD V30UK and the regular Creamback version putting an SM57 on both at identical positions and publishing the sweeps against the Helix **1x12 Recto** block. My guess is the two real cabs are further apart from each other than either is from the model, and that the biggest single variable in the whole test ends up being where the mic went. I do not have the cab, so that is a hypothesis and not a finding — but it is cheap to test and nobody has.
