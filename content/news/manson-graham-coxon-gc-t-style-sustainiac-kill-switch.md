Manson Guitar Works, the UK builder that Muse's Matt Bellamy owns, unveiled two signature T-styles with Blur guitarist Graham Coxon on November 20. Guitar World broke the news. There are **10 of each model**, so they'll be gone before most of us finish reading the spec sheet. The idea behind them is worth stealing anyway: a Tele body with a hot bridge pickup, an infinite-sustain neck position, and a kill switch under your picking hand.

## The Two Models

The two Guitar World image captions name the models as the **GC-1 "Cilla Black"** and the **GC-3 "Bethnal Green."** The article text muddles the names in one spot, so check Manson's own [Graham Coxon page](https://www.mansonguitarworks.com/collections/graham-coxon) before you hit buy on one. Here's what the launch coverage confirms:

- **Body:** two-piece swamp ash on both
- **Bridge pickup:** a custom-wound humbucker on both
- **Neck position:** a **Sustainiac** with its own switching
- **Necks:** birdseye maple on the GC-1, roasted figured maple on the GC-3. Guitar World calls them "speedy"
- **Kill switch:** flush-mounted on the GC-1's body. The coverage doesn't mention one on the GC-3
- **Run:** 10 of each, made in the UK

**Not published yet:** price, scale length, fret count, fretboard wood, hardware, pickup winding specs and which Sustainiac model is fitted. If you're actually in the running for one of twenty guitars, you already know to ask Manson directly.

## How It Came About

According to Bellamy, it started with a band he and Coxon play in for fun, The Jaded Hearts Club Band, which covers '60s songs. Coxon saw him playing a Tele-like Manson and asked to try it. Bellamy gave him one, Coxon went to the workshop, and a signature model followed. Coxon describes the result as a stripped-back hot rod built on a classic simple design. He says the bridge pickup sounds superb and the neck Sustainiac is now something he can't do without.

## What These Three Features Actually Do to Your Tone

Most of us aren't getting one of twenty. Each of the three big features maps onto something you can do tonight, though.

### 1. A humbucker in a Tele body

A bridge humbucker on a swamp ash T-style gives you the Tele's snap and attack with more output and less hum than a single-coil. In practice that means it hits a dirt pedal harder, so **turn the gain down** from wherever you'd set it for a Tele single-coil. Our [TS808, Klon and RAT with humbuckers guide](/blog/ts808-klon-rat-humbuckers) has the numbers. The short version is that a RAT that sounds right on a single-coil Tele at Distortion 1 o'clock will probably want 11 o'clock with a humbucker.

If you want to hear what a Tele does when it slams from clean to crushed, look at our [Creep recipe](/recipe/greenwood-creep-clean-to-crunch). Greenwood's Telecaster Plus runs into a clean Fender for the verses, and a Shredmaster at full gain takes over for the chorus. The amp never changes. A hot bridge pickup makes that contrast even bigger. The [Shredmaster vs. RAT post](/blog/marshall-shredmaster-vs-rat-creep-tone) covers which dirt pedal gets you closer.

### 2. The Sustainiac

A Sustainiac is a driver in the pickup slot that keeps the string vibrating, so a note holds or blooms into harmonic feedback at any volume. A modeler can't move your strings. Here are the closest substitutes, from most to least convincing:

- **An EBow.** It's the same principle in your hand instead of in the guitar. Our [EBow technique guide](/blog/ebow-guitar-technique-infinite-sustain-swells) covers the swells.
- **Compressor into high gain, at volume.** Real feedback needs the speaker moving air near the guitar. Going direct, a compressor ahead of a high-gain amp block stretches the sustain but won't bloom into a harmonic the way a sustainer does.
- **Freeze or hold.** It's a different sound but a similar job. Freezing a chord and playing over it is the "sustain without a sustainer" trick. Our [freeze-and-hold pad guide](/blog/synth-pad-guitar-no-keyboard-freeze-hold) sets it up.

### 3. The kill switch

A kill switch cuts the output completely while you hold it. Tap it in rhythm and you get the stutter effect. Coxon's is flush-mounted on the body, which matters because it sits under your picking hand.

On a modeler, the easy version is a **Volume block on a footswitch**. Assign the switch to the block's level with min 0 and max 100, and set it to **momentary** if your unit supports that. HX Stomp and full Helix both do. It works for a dramatic cut before a chorus. It won't work for fast sixteenth-note stutters, because your foot is slower than your hand. If you actually want that sound, a $15 momentary switch wired across your output jack is the cheapest mod you'll ever do. Get a tech to do it if you've never soldered.

## The Bottom Line

Twenty guitars, no price yet, gone soon. What's worth taking from it is the layout: a Tele with a hot bridge humbucker, one infinite-sustain voice, and a way to kill the signal instantly. You can get the first with any humbucker-loaded T-style, the second with an EBow or a compressor and a loud amp, and the third with one footswitch assignment.
