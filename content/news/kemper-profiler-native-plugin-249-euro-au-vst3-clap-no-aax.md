Kemper has finally done the thing people have asked about for more than a decade. Profiler Native is the Profiler as software: a plugin and a standalone app, based on the Profiler MK2, available now from Kemper's own site. Gearnews reported it on November 24, and Bonedo, Guitar.com and several other outlets carry the same details.

Kemper's own product page had no spec text we could read at the time of writing, so everything below comes from that press coverage. Where the outlets don't say something, I'll tell you.

## What's Confirmed

- **Formats:** AU, VST3 and CLAP, plus standalone
- **No AAX.** Kemper says Pro Tools support is planned for a later release
- **Profiles:** every generation, from the original profiles through Liquid Profiles to Profiling 2.0
- **Effects:** more than 80, covering overdrive, fuzz, modulation, pitch, delay and reverb
- **Price:** €249, with a €199 introductory price
- **Hardware owners:** up to €100 (or dollars) off after logging in to a Kemper account, with the amount depending on which unit you own
- **Trial:** 10 days, free
- **No profiling.** Making profiles still requires Kemper hardware
- **No NAM** support

One price note. Guitar.com quotes £208, which doesn't line up neatly with the euro figures. Check Kemper's shop for your own currency before you assume anything.

## The Format Question Comes First

Ten days is a short trial, so sort this out before you start the clock.

If you record in Pro Tools, the plugin won't load today. Your only way in is the standalone app, which is fine for practice and no use for mixing. I'd wait for AAX.

CLAP is the newest of the three formats and plenty of DAWs still don't host it. Gearnews names Ableton Live and Pro Tools as two that don't. That matters less than it sounds, because VST3 covers nearly every DAW on Windows and Mac, and AU covers Logic and GarageBand. Confirm which one your DAW uses and you're done.

## What It Changes About Recording

A Profiler owner has always had two ways to record. Print the profile on the way in and live with it, or record a dry DI as well and reamp it through the hardware later. The second method works, but it happens in real time and it ties up the unit.

A plugin removes that step. Record the dry guitar, put Profiler Native on the track, and the profile stays open until the mix is finished. Change the rig, the gain or the cab at the mix stage without touching a cable. If you've never worked from a DI, our pieces on [reamping a clean DI](/blog/reamp-clean-di-through-cab-sim-pedal) and [what reamp comparisons can and can't tell you](/blog/reamp-comparisons-what-they-cant-test) cover the method.

Two settings decide whether the plugin responds like the hardware does.

**Input level.** A profile reacts to how hard you hit it, same as the amp it came from. The hardware has a known instrument input. Your audio interface doesn't, and its gain knob sits in front of the plugin. Set that gain so your hardest strumming peaks well short of clipping, somewhere around -12 to -6 dBFS, then leave it alone. If a familiar rig sounds thinner or hotter than you remember, look at that knob before you blame the software. None of the coverage says whether Profiler Native has an input calibration control, so that's the first thing I'd look for in the manual.

**Buffer size.** Playing through any amp plugin adds your interface's round trip to the signal. At 64 or 128 samples most players are comfortable. At 512 you'll feel it. Our [recording latency budget](/blog/guitar-recording-latency-budget-buffer-size-monitoring) has the numbers. Track at a low buffer and raise it for mixing.

## What Nobody Has Said Yet

Four things I'd want answered before paying:

1. **Rig transfer.** Rig Exchange is named as a source of profiles. No outlet says whether Rig Manager talks to the plugin, or whether a Performance built in the plugin moves to a Stage or Player and back. If your plan is to build at home and load the same rigs on the gig unit, that's the whole purchase.
2. **Licensing.** How many computers, and whether it needs a dongle or just an account.
3. **System requirements.** Operating systems and Apple Silicon support aren't listed in the coverage.
4. **The discount tiers.** "Up to €100, depending on your rig" covers a lot of ground. Log in and see your actual price.

## Who Should Buy It

If you own a Profiler and record at home, this is the easy case. The discount brings the launch price down to roughly €99 at best, your rigs already exist, and you get the reamp workflow without the real-time pass.

If you don't own one, the calculation is different. You'd be paying €199 to €249 for a player that can't make its own captures, in a market where NAM is free and Line 6 has [Helix Stadium Native](/news/line6-helix-stadium-native-intro-free-plugin). What Kemper is selling against that is the library: fifteen years of commercial and free profiles, now including [Profiling 2.0](/news/kemper-os-14-stable-profiling-2-live) rigs. Whether that library is worth the money depends on whether the amps you want are in it. Our comparison of [Kemper profiles and Helix models](/blog/kemper-profiles-vs-helix-models) explains what a profile gives you that a model doesn't, and the reverse.

The trial costs nothing. Use the ten days on the two or three amps you actually record with, at the buffer size you actually track at, and decide from that.
