I've had more than one customer walk into the shop asking how to make a Les Paul sound like Greeny. My honest answer has always been the same: you'd have to pull the neck pickup and flip the magnet. Most people don't want to do that to a guitar they paid real money for. **OctaKing Labs** now says you can do it with a pedal instead.

## What OctaKing Announced

The **Burstphonic PAF** is OctaKing's first pedal. Guitar World covered the launch on November 6. OctaKing was co-founded by pickup builder Alexey Dovnar, and the run is capped at **500 units worldwide**.

The pedal is fully analog and has two voicings:

- **Greeny mode** models the phase cancellation and frequency curve of the famous accident. Peter Green's 1959 Les Paul had its neck pickup magnet reversed, so with both pickups on, the middle position goes out of phase. That's the hollow, vocal sound on Green's early Fleetwood Mac records, and it's the guitar Gary Moore owned for decades and played on *Blues for Greeny*.
- **'59 mode** follows the dynamic envelope of a late-'50s PAF.

A second switch voices the pedal for **a Strat or a Les Paul**. Inside, the circuit splits the signal, shifts the phase on one leg, and recombines them to a mono output. The controls are Level, Bass, Sparkle, and P.A.F., and there are hidden internal calibration trims.

**Jack Moore**, Gary's son, gave feedback and final approval during development. OctaKing's product page says he also gave the team access to Gary's "Stripe" 1959 Les Paul Standard, and that the pedal was frequency-matched to it 1:1. Guitar World's coverage also mentions measurements from a late-'90s Gary Moore signature model. OctaKing calls the circuit "patent-pending" on its own site.

## The Price

The numbers don't line up across sources. When I checked on November 10, OctaKing's shop listed the pedal at **$564.99, marked down to $404.99**. Guitar World's review gives **$420 / £315 / €360**. Whichever number you get, it's boutique money for a limited run, and OctaKing's page showed it as available now with UPS express shipping.

Is it worth it? Limited, artist-blessed pedals with a famous name on them tend to hold their value better than the average boutique drive. If what you want is the sound and not the box, read on.

## How to Get Close on a Modeler

No Helix or Quad Cortex model flips a pickup magnet, so this is an approximation, not a clone. What phase cancellation does to a two-pickup sound is well understood: it strips out the low end and low mids that the two pickups share and leaves a thin, nasal, mid-forward voice. You can sketch that with EQ.

1. **Guitar: middle position, neck volume a bit lower.** On a Les Paul, set the bridge at 10 and the neck around **7–8**. Greeny's out-of-phase sound changes a lot with the volume balance, and this gets you into the right range before any processing.
2. **Parametric EQ, first in the chain.** Use a **high-pass around 180–220 Hz** and cut **4–6 dB at about 400–500 Hz** with a wide Q. Then add a small bump, **2–3 dB around 1.2–1.8 kHz**. That's the hollow honk. If the result sounds like a cocked wah, narrow the bump.
3. **Amp: low-gain Marshall, cleaner than you think.** Brit Plexi or Brit 2204 on Helix with **Drive around 3–4**. Green's sound was mostly clean-ish and dynamic, and Moore's Greeny playing on *Blues for Greeny* is far less saturated than his '80s rock tones. Our [best Helix amp models for blues](/blog/best-helix-amp-models-blues) list shows where these sit, and the [Helix amp model cheat sheet](/blog/helix-amp-model-cheat-sheet) maps each one to its real amp.
4. **Drive goes after the EQ, if at all.** Humbuckers already push a drive harder, as we explain in [why overdrive sounds different with humbuckers](/blog/overdrive-with-humbuckers-settings). Keep any boost low-gain so the phase-cut voice stays thin and doesn't get compressed back into a normal neck-plus-bridge sound.

If you want to hear how much a phase relationship can thin a tone, our piece on [phase cancellation in dual-amp modeler presets](/blog/phase-cancellation-dual-amp-modeler-presets) covers the same physics, just on the output side.

## Should You Care

If you're a Peter Green or Gary Moore player and you won't modify a vintage Les Paul, this is the first off-the-shelf box built specifically for that sound with the Moore family involved. That's a fair reason to buy one of the 500.

If you're curious and not a collector, try the EQ sketch first. It won't fool anyone who's played the real guitar. It'll tell you in ten minutes whether that hollow middle position is a sound you'd actually use, before you spend $400 to find out.
