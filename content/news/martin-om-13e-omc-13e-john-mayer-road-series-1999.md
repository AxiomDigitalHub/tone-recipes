I've been waiting a long time for a John Mayer Martin that doesn't cost what my first car did. Today Martin announced two of them. The **OM-13E John Mayer** and the **OMC-13E John Mayer** are the first Mayer signatures in the Road Series, and both list at **$1,999.99**.

## What Martin Announced

Martin put out the announcement on November 10. Both guitars share one spec sheet:

- **Top:** solid Engelmann spruce with an aging toner
- **Back and sides:** solid black walnut
- **Bracing:** scalloped spruce X-bracing
- **Neck:** Performing Artist profile, standard taper, 1 11/16" nut, 25.4" scale
- **Fingerboard and bridge:** ebony, with custom faux mother-of-pearl inlays that include Mayer's signature
- **Appointments:** gloss body, antique white binding, a faux abalone rosette modeled on the OMJM's, nickel open-gear tuners, and a pre-printed Mayer signature on the interior label
- **Electronics:** Martin E1 with a built-in tuner
- **Case:** softshell

There's one real difference between the two. The **OM-13E** is a non-cutaway Orchestra Model with herringbone top trim. The **OMC-13E** has a 14-fret cutaway and multi-stripe trim. Both are sold through Martin dealers and martinguitar.com. The release doesn't list a separate ship date.

## Where This Sits Next to the OMJM

The guitar most people know is the **OMJM**, the standard-line Mayer OM that's still in the catalog. Retailers list it around $4,199. The Mayer partnership started in 2003 with the limited OM-28 John Mayer. So the new Road models aren't replacing anything. They're a way in at about half the price.

The material swap is where that money goes. The OMJM's back and sides are East Indian rosewood. The Road models use **black walnut**. Walnut usually sits between mahogany and rosewood. It has more mid-range focus than rosewood and less of rosewood's scooped sparkle. On an OM body, that's a good fit for fingerpicking and for a guitar that has to sit under a vocal. I haven't played one yet, so I'm describing the woods and not this guitar.

## The Tone Part: Making the E1 Sound Like the Guitar

Most people who buy one of these will plug it in more than they mic it, whether that's at church, at an open mic, or into an interface at 11 PM. The E1 is a piezo-based system, and a piezo heard straight through a PA is where a $2,000 acoustic starts to sound like a $200 one. Here's my starting point.

**1. Fix the input first.** A piezo wants a high-impedance load. If you go into a Helix or HX Stomp, set the input impedance to **1M** or higher. Don't leave it on Auto, where a block in front of it can drag the load down. Our [piezo DI and input impedance guide](/blog/piezo-di-for-acoustic-guitar-input-impedance) explains why this one setting changes the whole top end.

**2. Cut before you add body.** Piezo quack lives in the upper mids. Put a parametric EQ first in the chain and pull **2–3 dB around 2.5–3.5 kHz** with a moderate Q. Then roll the low end off at about **80–100 Hz**. An OM won't miss that low end, and the room's feedback will. We compare it with the IR approach in [acoustic IR vs. subtractive EQ](/blog/acoustic-ir-vs-subtractive-eq-piezo-real). For a walnut guitar, subtractive EQ gets you further than people expect.

**3. Add an acoustic IR, or not.** If you have a body IR slot, load one at a 40–60% mix rather than 100%. That restores some of the air the piezo can't hear without making the guitar sound like someone else's. The [worship acoustic DI walkthrough](/blog/acoustic-guitar-through-modeler-worship-di) has the full signal chain and the feedback workflow, and it applies at any gig.

**4. Compress lightly.** Mayer's acoustic sound is dynamic. It's fingers and thumb, not a strummed wall. Use a slow-attack compressor at a 2:1 ratio with just 2–3 dB of gain reduction. Anything more flattens the very thing the OM shape is good at.

If you came to this guitar from Mayer's electric work, the same restraint is what makes his clean Strat sound work. Our [John Mayer clean tone settings](/blog/john-mayer-clean-tone-settings) and the [Gravity recipe](/recipe/mayer-gravity-super-clean-dynamic) are mostly about leaving space, and that carries straight over to an acoustic.

## Should You Care

If you've wanted a Mayer OM and the OMJM's price kept you out, this is the one to try. Walnut is a material choice, not a cut corner. The Performing Artist neck and the OM body are what make the OMJM feel the way it does, and both are here.

If you mostly play plugged in, budget a little time for EQ, or a decent DI if your rig doesn't have an impedance setting. The E1 will get you to the gig, and the four steps above get you the rest of the way.
