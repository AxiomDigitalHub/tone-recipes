## The Blind Faith Telecaster Is a Weird, Wonderful Hybrid

This is a recreation of the guitar Clapton played in 1969 with Steve Winwood and Ginger Baker: a Telecaster Custom body wearing a replica 1956 Stratocaster neck. Fender is building 130 of them with period-correct details, modern electronics, a vintage "Woof Tone" wiring kit, and an "EPC" serial prefix.

A Tele body with a Strat neck is not a novelty — it's the reason that guitar sounds the way it does. You get the Telecaster's bridge geometry and the ash-and-steel snap of a Tele bridge pickup, played through the softer 7.25"-radius, chunkier-profile feel of a mid-50s Strat neck. The neck changes how you *play* it, and how you play it changes what comes out. Clapton's 1969 tone is fatter and rounder than the Tele stereotype, and part of that is that he was fretting it like a Strat.

The 1969 Blind Faith sound is the transition point in his playing — after the Bluesbreakers Les Paul years, before the Blackie Strat era. Thick, vocal, slightly compressed, a lot of neck pickup, and driven by a cranked amp rather than a pedal. That last bit matters most, and it's the part people get wrong.

**Getting there on a modeler.** The mistake is reaching for a drive pedal. This tone is amp breakup — a loud amp on the edge, not a clean amp with dirt in front of it.

Start with a **Marshall-family model at low gain rather than a high-gain block**. On a Helix, that's Brit Plexi Nrm or Brit J45 Nrm — not the bright channels. Drive around 5–6 on the 0–10 scale, and the master pushed to 7+ so you're getting power-amp behavior instead of preamp fizz. Bass 4, Mids 6.5, Treble 5, Presence 3.5. Roll the treble down further than feels right at first; a bridge Tele into a bright amp model is a knife, and the 1969 tone is not a knife.

Then play the **neck pickup**, and use your guitar's volume control as the gain stage. Clapton's whole vocabulary in this era is built on backing the volume off to 6 for chords and rolling up for leads. That interaction is the tone, and it's free — [our pickup position guide](/blog/pickup-position-guide) covers what each position is actually contributing.

If you want the same neighborhood with a real reference, [the BB King blues lead tone recipe](/blog/bb-king-classic-blues-lead-tone) is built on the same principle — vocal midrange, amp-driven, no pedal — and our [best Helix amp models for blues](/blog/best-helix-amp-models-blues) has the shortlist.

## The Silver Sky in Incline Blue

70 of them. Rosewood board, a "Crossroads Sun" inlay at the 12th fret, a Crossroads Guitar Festival 2026 neck plate, and Incline Blue paint. $3,750. Mayer will play one onstage at the festival.

Let me be straight about what's on offer: this is a standard-spec Silver Sky in a limited finish with commemorative appointments, at a premium over a regular rosewood Silver Sky. You are buying the run number and the cause, not a different guitar. That's a completely legitimate thing to buy — Crossroads Centre is real and the money goes there — but nobody should convince themselves the Incline Blue sounds different.

What the Silver Sky actually *is*, tonally, is a Strat with a very particular set of choices: 635JM single-coils voiced brighter and cleaner than most vintage-output Strat pickups, a flatter-than-vintage 7.25" board with a rounder profile, and a trem setup that stays in tune. It's clean-forward. It rewards headroom. We put it head-to-head with a standard Strat [here](/blog/prs-silver-sky-vs-fender-strat-mayer-tone), and the short version is that the Silver Sky is less hairy and more articulate — which is exactly what Mayer's playing needs and possibly not what yours does.

**The Mayer clean tone on a modeler** is a Fender blackface model, not a Marshall. Deluxe Reverb or Twin family, treble around 6, bass 4, and — this is the one people skip — a **compressor before the amp, set gently**. Ratio low, attack medium so the pick attack survives, maybe 3 dB of gain reduction. The glassy sustain in "Gravity" is compression doing quiet work, not the amp. Then a touch of spring reverb, and the neck-plus-middle position (position 4). Full walkthrough in [our John Mayer clean tone settings](/blog/john-mayer-clean-tone-settings).

The thing that took me a whole summer to figure out at 16 is that the Mayer tone is *not a dirty tone with the gain down*. It's a genuinely clean amp with dynamics work in front of it. If you build it as a low-gain overdrive you will chase it forever and never land it — and if you're not sure what your compressor is doing to your attack, [that's a whole diagnosis of its own](/blog/compressor-killing-your-tone).

## What I'd Do

If you have $3,750 or $4,999 and you want to give money to an addiction treatment center while getting a nice guitar, this is a good way to do it. I'm not going to talk anyone out of that.

If you have $0 and you want the tone: the Blind Faith sound is a neck pickup, a cranked low-gain British amp, and your volume knob. The Mayer sound is a blackface model, a gentle compressor, and position 4. Neither of those costs anything, and both of them are more about your right hand than about which guitar is under it.

The guitars are limited to 130 and 70 respectively and went on sale August 26. The Martin 000-13E at $1,899 runs through January 27, 2027, and is the only piece here a working musician might realistically buy to actually play.
