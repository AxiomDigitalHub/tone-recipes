Orange has brought the Tiny Terror back for its 20th birthday. Gearnews reported the Tiny Terror 20th Anniversary on November 26: a limited run of 1,000 heads worldwide, finished in British Racing Green, each with a certificate of authenticity hand-signed by Orange founder Cliff Cooper.

The Tiny Terror came out in 2006 and started the lunchbox-amp category. A lot of small heads followed it. Very few of them stayed as simple.

## What's Confirmed

- **Format:** single-channel tube head
- **Power:** 15 watts or 7 watts, chosen on the same switch that handles standby
- **Controls:** Volume, Tone, Gain
- **Speaker outputs:** 8 ohm and 16 ohm
- **Effects loop:** none
- **Finish:** British Racing Green
- **Run:** 1,000 units worldwide, with a certificate signed by Cliff Cooper
- **Price:** $699 at Sweetwater. Thomann lists it as coming soon

Gearnews describes the Tiny Terror design as two 12AX7 preamp tubes and a pair of EL84 power tubes. It states that about the design in general and not as a line on this model's spec sheet, and Orange's own product page wasn't loading when this was written, so confirm the tube complement there before you order a spare set. No ship date or UK and European pricing has been published in the coverage so far.

Nothing in the announcement points to a circuit change. What you're paying for over a used original is the color, the number, and the signature.

## Why Three Knobs Is the Whole Amp

There's no bass, middle or treble here. Gain sets how hard the preamp is driven, Volume sets how hard the power section is driven, and Tone trims the top. Everything you'd normally do with an EQ, you do with the balance between the first two.

That makes the amp a good teacher. Turn Gain up and Volume down and you hear preamp distortion: tighter, fizzier, more compressed. Turn Gain down and Volume up and the EL84s do the work: looser, louder, more touch-sensitive. If the difference between those two is still fuzzy to you, [preamp vs power amp distortion](/blog/preamp-vs-power-amp-distortion-how-to-hear) walks through how to hear it, and [EL84 tube character](/blog/el84-tube-character-ac30-boutique) covers what that power section adds.

Starting points on a clock face. These are standard settings for this style of amp, not measurements of the anniversary unit, so adjust by ear and by pickup:

| Sound | Gain | Volume | Tone | Mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Clean with some hair | 9 o'clock | 1 to 2 o'clock | 1 o'clock | 15 W |
| Classic rock crunch | Noon | Noon | Noon | 15 W |
| Thick lead, lower volume | 2 to 3 o'clock | 10 to 11 o'clock | 11 o'clock | 7 W |
| Power-tube breakup at home | 10 o'clock | 2 to 3 o'clock | Noon | 7 W |

The 7-watt setting is the feature I'd pay attention to. Halving the wattage does not halve the loudness; it takes off a few decibels. What it does is move the point where the power tubes start to give, so the last row of that table becomes usable in a room with other people in it. It's still a loud amp at 7 watts with the Volume past noon. Plan on a cab with a single 12 and a closed door.

## The Missing Loop

No effects loop means delay and reverb go in front of the amp or after a microphone. Into the clean and edge-of-breakup settings, that's fine and sounds like every record made before loops were common. Into the high-gain settings, repeats and reverb tails get distorted along with the guitar and turn to mush.

If you play with a lot of ambience on top of a driven sound, this is the wrong head, and the anniversary version doesn't change that.

## Getting There on a Helix or HX Stomp

Helix has no Tiny Terror model. The Orange-derived amps in the list are Mandarin 80, based on the OR80, and Mandarin Rocker, based on the Rockerverb. Both are bigger amps with different power sections, so treat them as the right family voicing and not as a match.

A starting point with Mandarin 80, on the 0–10 scale Helix uses:

- **Drive:** 4.5 to 5.5 for crunch, 7.0 for the lead setting
- **Bass:** 4.0. A small EL84 head has less low end than an OR80, so pull this back first
- **Mid:** 6.0
- **Treble:** 5.5, then use it the way you'd use the Tone knob and leave Presence at 5.0
- **Master:** 6.0 to 7.0 so the power amp model is contributing
- **Sag:** 6.0 to 7.0 for some of the small-amp give
- **Cab:** a 1x12 or 2x12 with a Greenback-style or Vintage 30-style speaker, low cut around 90 Hz, high cut around 6.5 kHz

Then build the patch the way the real amp forces you to: one amp, no snapshot tricks, and ride your guitar's volume knob for clean. On a modeler you get the thing the hardware can't give you, which is delay and reverb placed after the amp block.

If you want the reasoning behind why a small, simple amp reacts the way this one does, [class A vs class AB](/blog/class-a-vs-class-ab-amp) is the background reading.

## Who Should Buy One

If you want a Tiny Terror to play, used originals have been around for 20 years and the circuit, by every account so far, is the same idea. If you want this one, it's because of the green, the 1,000-unit run and Cliff Cooper's signature, and at $699 that's a fair premium for an anniversary piece from the company that invented the category. Just know what it is: one channel, three knobs, no loop, and loud.
