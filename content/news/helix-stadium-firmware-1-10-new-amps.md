**Correction, November 23, 2026.** The original version of this post described a "firmware 1.10" with eight new amp models, faster Proxy cloning and a redesigned cab block. That was wrong. There was no 1.10. The release was Helix Stadium 1.3, and its contents are below, taken from Line 6's March 24 press release. The URL still carries the old version number so existing links keep working.

Line 6 released **Helix Stadium 1.3** on **March 24, 2026**, as a free update for Helix Stadium XL Floor and Helix Stadium Floor. Line 6 calls it the **Proxy Update**, because it "rolls out phase one of our Proxy cloning engine," per the [press release as carried by MusicPlayers](https://musicplayers.com/2026/03/line-6-launches-helix-stadium-1-3-firmware-update/).

## What 1.3 adds

| Area | What changed |
|---|---|
| Proxy cloning | Phase one: clone an amp and cab, an amp, a preamp, or a distortion pedal |
| Amps | Five new Agoura channels from two amps, 55 channels in total |
| Showcase | Preset and snapshot automation, MIDI Note Flags, MIDI recall of Playlists and Songs |
| Settings | New global settings for Song load behavior and Looper Flag autostop |

## Proxy, phase one

Proxy is Line 6's capture system, and 1.3 is the first firmware in which Stadium owners can make their own clones. Four source types are supported: **Amp+Cab, Amp, Preamp, and Distortion pedal**.

The part that sets it apart from the other capture systems is where the work happens. Line 6 describes Proxy as a cloud-based cloning engine. The Stadium records the training signal and sends it to Line 6's servers, which build the clone. Line 6's stated reasons are accuracy (more detail than onboard processing would typically allow) and upgradability (the engine can improve without waiting for a firmware release).

That has two practical consequences. You need the unit online to make a clone. And the result depends on a service Line 6 runs, which is a different arrangement from a Quad Cortex or Kemper that captures entirely on the device.

The press release does not state how long a clone takes or how many clones a preset can hold, so we are not going to guess at either.

## The two new amps

- **Matchstick 30** (3 channels: Channel 1, Channel 2, Jumped), based on the Matchless DC30.
- **Mandarin Rock 30** (2 channels: Normal, Overdrive), based on the Orange Rockerverb 100 MKIII.

For worship players the Matchstick is the one to notice. The Matchless DC30 is one of the standard modern worship amps, and the legacy HX Matchstick models are among the most used in our Helix recipes. This is the same amp rebuilt on the Agoura engine, with a Jumped channel the HX version does not have. Settings from an HX Matchstick preset are a starting point on the Agoura model, not a copy: the engine is different, so dial it again by ear and match levels before comparing.

The Mandarin Rock 30 covers the thick British high-gain ground between a Plexi and a modern metal amp.

## Showcase

Showcase is the Stadium's playback and automation system. In 1.3 it gains true preset and snapshot automation, so a song timeline can change your tones for you. It also gains MIDI Note Flags for triggering external synths and samplers, and MIDI recall of Playlists and Songs. If you run tracks and a click on Sunday, automation along the song timeline is the feature in this release that changes your workflow the most.

## How to update

Install it on the unit over Wi-Fi, or over USB with Line 6 Central from [line6.com/software](https://line6.com/software/). Back up first.
