Dunlop has put the first Green Rhino back into production. The Way Huge Smalls Green Rhino Overdrive, announced on November 24, recreates the 1995 Green Rhino Overdrive II in the compact Smalls enclosure. Premier Guitar carried the press release and Gearnews followed a day later.

It's a small release, and a welcome one. The mid-forward overdrive is the pedal most of us lean on without thinking about it, and this is one of the well-loved versions of that idea, back in its plainest form.

## What's Confirmed

- **Controls:** Volume, Tone and Drive
- **Circuit:** LF353 op-amp with feedback clipping, plus MPSA18 transistors
- **Build:** through-hole construction, true bypass
- **Enclosure:** Smalls format with top-mounted jacks
- **Power:** 9 volts, battery or external supply
- **Price:** $139.99 street. Gearnews lists €149 and £125 at Thomann

The announcement doesn't give a ship date. US and European retailers already have listings, so check stock where you buy.

## What Three Knobs Means Here

Later Green Rhinos grew extra controls for shaping the low end and the mids. This one goes the other way, back to the 1995 layout, and that changes how you use it.

Feedback clipping around an op-amp is the same basic approach as a Tube Screamer: the distortion happens inside the gain stage, the lows get trimmed before clipping, and the mids come forward. Dunlop describes this pedal's voice as mid-focused, which fits. So the practical question is the one you'd ask of any pedal in that family. Do you want that midrange push, or are you going to fight it?

With only a Tone knob, you can't tune the low end to the amp. You get the voicing the designer chose. On a bright, scooped amp that's usually a gift, because the pedal fills in exactly what the amp is missing. On an amp that already has a lot of mids it can stack up and turn nasal, and Tone alone won't fix that. If that's your rig, a pedal with real EQ, like the [Seymour Duncan 805+](/news/seymour-duncan-805-plus-overdrive-forza-mode-189) from last week, is the more flexible choice.

The announcement compares this pedal only to the 1995 original and doesn't publish a frequency plot, so how its mid hump differs from a TS808's is something to listen for in the demos and not something I can give you a number for.

## Where to Set It

The three jobs this kind of pedal does well, with starting points on a clock face:

| Job | Drive | Tone | Volume |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clean boost into an amp that's already breaking up | 8 to 9 o'clock | Noon | 2 to 3 o'clock |
| Edge-of-breakup on a clean amp | 11 to 1 o'clock | 11 o'clock | Unity, then a touch more |
| Tightening a high-gain amp | Minimum | Noon to 1 o'clock | 2 o'clock or higher |

These are the standard starting points for the pedal type, not measurements of this unit, so move them by ear. The reasoning behind each is in our [Tube Screamer settings guide](/blog/tube-screamer-settings-guide), and the third row gets its own piece in [Tube Screamer before a high-gain amp](/blog/tube-screamer-before-high-gain-amp). If you play humbuckers, pull Drive back further than you think, as covered in [overdrive with humbuckers](/blog/overdrive-with-humbuckers-settings).

## Getting the Same Job Done on a Helix or HX Stomp

There's no Green Rhino model in Helix. The closest block is Scream 808, which shares the feedback-clipping approach and the three-control layout, and it will do all three jobs above.

On the 0–10 scale Helix uses:

- **Boost:** Gain 1.0 to 2.0, Tone 5.0, Level 7.0 to 8.0
- **Edge-of-breakup:** Gain 4.5 to 5.5, Tone 4.5, Level around 5.0, then match bypassed volume
- **Tightening high gain:** Gain 0 to 1.0, Tone 5.0 to 6.0, Level 7.0 or higher

It won't be an exact match for this pedal's voicing. What it gives you is the same behavior in the chain: less low end going into the amp, more mids, earlier breakup. If you want to hear how that family compares with the alternatives before deciding whether the hardware is worth a slot on your board, [Tube Screamer vs Klon vs Blues Driver](/blog/tube-screamer-vs-klon-vs-blues-driver) lays out the differences.

## Who It's For

If you already own a Tube Screamer you like, this is a different flavor of something you have, and $139.99 is a lot for a flavor. If you don't, or if the original Green Rhino is the one you've been watching used listings for, this is the simple version at a fair price, small enough to fit a crowded board and with the jacks on top where they belong.
