On **November 10**, Seymour Duncan put its Custom Shop **Frankenstein** humbuckers in stripes. Each of the three Eddie Van Halen-approved models now comes in four finishes taken from Eddie's early guitars:

- **Red, Black & White**, the Frankenstrat most people picture
- **Black & White**, which Guitar World ties to the guitar's earlier form
- **Grey & Black**
- **Bumble Bee** (yellow and black)

Premier Guitar and Guitar World both report a **$199 street price per pickup**, available now from the Custom Shop. The Custom Shop's own listing shows the Frankenstein Custom from $189 before finish options, so expect the exact figure to depend on which color you tick. In Australia, Australian Musician lists an RRP of $499 with stock arriving in early December.

For 25 years I've watched customers buy pickups for the cover and regret it for the sound. The paint is the only new thing here. Seymour Duncan hasn't announced any change to the windings. What the paint does is make three very different pickups look alike, and that's a good reason to read the spec sheet before you pick a color.

## Three Pickups, One Paint Job

The collection goes back to the early 2000s. The Custom Shop reproduced the pickup in Eddie's Frankenstein guitar, then built two variations, and Eddie approved all three. From Seymour Duncan's Custom Shop pages:

| Model | What it's built to be | DC resistance | Magnet | Spacing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Frankenstein Custom** | A reproduction of the pickup in the Frankenstein guitar | Not published | Alnico II | Trembucker |
| **Frankenstein Classic** | The P.A.F.-style tone of the first six Van Halen albums | 8.2k | Alnico II | Standard or Trembucker |
| **Frankenstein Plus** | A hot-rodded version Eddie developed and approved in 2013 | 17.32k | Alnico II | Trembucker only |

All three are wax-potted, offered with single- or four-conductor cable, and made in Santa Barbara.

Look at the resistance column. The Plus reads more than double the Classic. DC resistance isn't output; [it measures wire, not voltage](/blog/pickup-dc-resistance-what-it-actually-measures). But these are two pickups from the same shop, with the same magnet, for the same guitarist. When one reads twice the other, there's a lot more wire on it. Seymour Duncan calls the Plus "extra-hot," and it is. Through a cranked amp, the Classic and the Plus will not feel like the same pickup.

The spacing column matters too. Trembucker spacing is the wider pole layout for Floyd-style bridges. If you're putting a Frankenstein in a Gibson-spaced guitar, the Classic is the only one with a standard-spaced option. With a Plus, the outer strings won't sit over the poles.

## Which One Is the Record You're Chasing

- **For *Van Halen* through *1984*:** the **Classic**. Seymour Duncan built it around the first six albums, and at 8.2k with Alnico II it's in vintage PAF territory. Low enough to clean up when you roll the volume back, which Eddie did constantly.
- **For the guitar itself:** the **Custom**. It's the reproduction of the pickup that was in the Frankenstein. Seymour Duncan doesn't publish its resistance, so I won't guess at one.
- **For more gain at lower amp settings:** the **Plus**. Seymour Duncan says it raises output while staying clear at lower volume. That's a 2013 pickup. It isn't the 1978 sound, and it doesn't claim to be.

## Setting a Modeler for Each

Our [Eruption brown sound recipe](/recipe/evh-eruption-brown-sound) on Helix is built on **Brit Plexi Brt** with Drive at **7** and Sag set high. The high Sag stands in for the variac trick. That recipe assumes a vintage-output humbucker, which makes it the Classic's recipe.

**With the Classic,** load the recipe as written. If you're playing along with the debut album, tune down a half-step first. My [StroboStomp EVH piece](/news/peterson-strobostomp-evh-13-van-halen-i-sweeteners-eb-sharp-179) covers how far sharp each track actually sits.

**With the Plus,** the same preset will be too hot. Twice the wire hits the Plexi block harder, so the note breaks up earlier and the low end gets loose under a heavy pick. Don't reach for EQ first:

1. **Bring Drive down** from 7 to about 5 and set it by ear against the record. The Plus supplies the gain the Drive knob used to.
2. **Check your input impedance.** A pickup with that much wire on it is more sensitive to load. On Helix, set **Guitar In-Z** to Auto or 1M before deciding the pickup is dark. Our [DC resistance explainer](/blog/pickup-dc-resistance-what-it-actually-measures) explains why a fixed low impedance dulls a hot pickup more than a vintage one.
3. **If you switch guitars at a gig,** save the Plus settings as a separate snapshot or preset instead of reworking one patch. [Leveling two guitars on one rig](/blog/one-modeler-two-guitars-different-output-levels) walks through the order to do it in.

The [Panama recipe](/recipe/evh-panama-brown-sound) is the *1984*-era tone. With the Classic or the Custom it works as written. With the Plus, make the same Drive cut.

All three use Alnico II, so the magnet won't decide this for you. If you want to know what Alnico II does in a hot humbucker compared with Alnico V, [the magnet guide](/blog/pickup-magnet-types-alnico-2-3-5-ceramic-string-pull) covers it. The rest of our Van Halen recipes are on the [Eddie Van Halen artist page](/artist/eddie-van-halen).

## My Take

I've seen this happen in the shop many times. Somebody wants "the Eddie pickup," sees the stripes and picks the hottest one because hot sounds like Eddie. Then their brown sound turns into mud, because the records they love were made on something closer to a PAF. The stripes are well done and $199 is fair for a Custom Shop pickup. Pick the model for the record you want, then pick the color.
