Boss announced the **Katana-25** on **October 29**. It's a compact home combo: a **25-watt** reactive analog power section built on Boss's Tube Logic approach, a **custom 8-inch speaker in an open-back cabinet**, **six amp characters** (Boss says they cover pop, blues, country, classic rock and high-gain metal, and one is tuned for acoustic-electric guitars), **60+ Boss effects with up to five running at once**, **20 factory presets plus 10 empty slots**, a tap-tempo button, a built-in tuner, onboard **Bluetooth**, and a **Power Control switch** that drops it to **half a watt**. It's **$199.99** and in stores now.

Guitar World called it the Katana that's "always been missing," and I agree. The Mini is a toy you love and the 50 is a real amp you have to make room for. This one fits next to the couch.

## The Katana-25 Is a Katana-50 at Its Middle Setting

The number everybody leads with is 25 watts. I don't think it's the important one.

The Katana-50's Power Control has three positions: **50W, 25W, and 0.5W**. I covered this in the [Katana hidden-settings guide](/blog/boss-katana-hidden-settings). At home, a 50 almost never needs the top position. Fifty watts is more than a living room asks for, so the 25W and 0.5W settings get all the use.

The Katana-25 just drops the position nobody was using at home. It has two settings, **full (25W)** and **half-watt**, and those are the two that matter in a house with a kid asleep upstairs.

So the power section isn't the real change. The speaker is.

## The Spec That Changes Your Settings: 8 Inches, Open Back

A Katana-50 pushes a 12-inch speaker. The 25 pushes an **8-inch in an open-back box**. Those two facts do more to your tone than the wattage does, and they push in the same direction:

- **A smaller cone moves less air at the bottom.** You'll lose the low end you're used to hearing under a palm mute.
- **An open back lets the rear wave out of the cabinet**, where some of it cancels the front wave in the lows. You get a wider, airier sound that fills the room, but it's lighter in the bass and looser on chugs. The [open-back vs. closed-back breakdown](/blog/open-back-vs-closed-back-cab-tone) explains why.

Here's what that means at the knobs:

**Don't chase the low end with the Bass knob.** Your first instinct on a small open-back amp will be to crank Bass to make up for what's missing. Don't. The speaker can't reproduce it, so all you buy is flub and a cabinet that sounds like it's struggling. Leave Bass around noon. If you want weight, find it in the low mids.

**On the high-gain character, cut Bass a bit below noon and back the gain off from where you'd set it on the 50.** An open-back 8-inch gets loose fast with high gain. Tighter low end and a bit less saturation will sound heavier in the room than "more of everything." It's the same logic as [why a tube amp sounds different at bedroom volume](/blog/tube-amp-sounds-different-bedroom-volume): small speakers at low volume punish exactly the frequencies you're tempted to add.

**On the clean and crunch characters, the open back is a gift.** Open-back cabs are what jangly cleans and edge-of-breakup sounds want. If you play cleans and light drive, this is probably the most flattering cabinet in the Katana line for you.

If you've got settings from our [Katana tube-amp settings](/blog/best-katana-settings-tube-amp) or [every-genre guide](/blog/boss-katana-settings-guide-every-genre) dialed for a 50, treat them as a starting point. Pull Bass and Gain back a notch, keep Treble where it is, and listen again.

## Five Effects at Once Covers a Practice Rig

Five simultaneous effects is more than a practice amp needs, and I mean that as praise. My HX Stomp chain at 9 PM is a compressor, one drive, a delay and a reverb. Four blocks. On the Katana-25 that leaves one slot free for a modulation or a boost.

Two features matter more than the effect count:

**The tap-tempo button is on the front panel.** If you've ever practiced a dotted-eighth part against a recording with the delay time off by 30 milliseconds, you know this beats any effect on the list.

**Tone Studio's Session feature plays YouTube content and can switch your memories at set points in the song.** That's an automated preset change for a song you're learning, in a $199 amp. For anyone running a [20-minute practice session](/blog/20-minute-practice-session) around a set list, that's the feature I'd actually use: verse tone, chorus tone, no tap-dancing while you're still working out the chord changes.

## What Boss Hasn't Said Yet

I read every launch write-up I could find, and none of them list the **jacks**. No confirmed headphone output, aux in, or USB audio. On a home amp that matters. The USB recording trick in the [hidden-settings guide](/blog/boss-katana-hidden-settings) depends on USB audio, and silent practice needs a headphone out.

The amp is described as having full Tone Studio and Tone Exchange support, so it probably has a phones jack. Still, "probably" is not a spec. Before you buy, check the rear-panel photo or ask the dealer two things: **is there a headphone/rec out, and does USB carry audio?**

Same caution on presets. Boss says Tone Exchange works with it. Whether patches built for the 50's 12-inch combo sound right through this 8-inch box is a different question. Expect to redo the EQ (see above).

## Who Should Buy It

**You've outgrown the Katana Mini but you don't have room for a 50.** This is your amp. In my [Katana Mini vs. amPlug comparison](/blog/katana-mini-vs-vox-amplug-4-budget-headphone) I said the Mini is confidently a small amp, with three hardware channels, no app, and no presets. The 25 is still a small amp, but it adds presets, Tone Studio, and a real power section.

**You mostly play cleans and light drive at home.** The open-back 8-inch suits you. Buy it.

**You mostly play high gain.** It'll work if you follow the EQ advice above, but a closed-back 12 will always chug better. Spend the extra on a 50.

**You already have an HX Stomp and headphones.** You don't need this, and I say that as someone who wants one anyway. The Stomp is the more flexible rig. The Katana-25 is for the nights you want to play guitar in a room without plugging in headphones, and some weeks those are the only nights I actually play.
