On **September 5**, BluGuitar confirmed that the **AMPX** is finally going into production and will launch at **Guitar Summit 2026** in Mannheim, September 25–27.

The numbers, as published: **first production batch of about 50 units**, a **second batch of 250**, retail **$1,999.99**. Distribution runs through a "Waitlist Checker" on a new BluGuitar site — you enter an email, you get assigned a zone, and the zone tells you where you stand in the queue. Germany ships first, then the US and everywhere else. Thomas Blug says he has been gigging the thing for two years straight, and the company remains self-funded, which is the stated reason it took as long as it took.

It took four years. I watched this amp get announced at the summer NAMM show in **2022** and then not exist. I have been in this business long enough to have a filing cabinet full of amps that got a booth and a press release and never got a serial number, so let me be plain about my position: a confirmed production batch with a launch venue and a queue mechanism is a materially different announcement than a prototype on a table. This one looks real. Fifty units is also not a lot of amplifiers.

## What It Actually Is

Here are the specs off BluGuitar's own product page, which is the only spec sheet I trust on a product this delayed:

- **100-watt Nanotube X power amp** — a miniature valve driving the output stage
- **Neural Analog Amp design**, with circuit parameters editable at the component level and saved to presets
- Ships with the tones from the **AMP1 Mercury and Iridium editions**
- **Analog effects:** compressor, boost, overdrive, fuzz, tremolo
- **Digital effects bank:** chorus, phaser, flanger, univibe, delay, reverb — pre or post, mono or stereo, on 8-core processing
- **Dynamic IR stereo speaker emulation**, up to five dynamic IRs at once
- Two speaker outputs, line out, headphone out, effects loop, MIDI In/Out, two expression pedal inputs
- Universal power, 100–240V
- **Approximately 5 lbs**, 426 × 198.9 × 92.6 mm
- **BlugPRINTs** library for additional amp voicings, effects, and cabinets

One housekeeping note, because four years is long enough for a spec sheet to drift: earlier press on this amp cited a **16-core** processor and **6.5 lbs**. The current official page says **8-core** and **about 5 lbs**. I am going with the manufacturer's page. If you are pre-ordering off a number you read in 2022, check it again.

The other thing worth stating carefully is the "100% analog" claim, because it is doing marketing work. The **amp** is analog — input to output, real circuit, real nanotube power section. The **analog effects** are analog. The digital part is control, recall, and a separate bank of digital modulation and time effects that are, unavoidably, digital when you switch them on. That is a good design. It is not the same sentence as "100% analog," and you should know which half you are buying.

## The Part Nobody Says Out Loud: This Is Not a Modeler

Most of you reading this own a Helix or a Quad Cortex, and the pitch you are about to hear a hundred times between now and Mannheim is that the AMPX replaces a room of amps in a 5-pound box. So let me appraise that honestly.

The AMPX is **one amplifier** with **four channel voicings** — Clean, Vintage, Classic, Modern, inherited from the AMP1 — that you can edit deeply and recall instantly. It is not a library of a hundred modeled amps. If your reason for owning a modeler is that you need a Vox on one song and a Twin on the next and a Recto after that, the AMPX does not solve that problem and is not trying to.

What it does solve is the problem a modeler is genuinely worst at: **a real analog power section pushing a real speaker**. If you have been running a Helix into a power amp to get your cab moving air — and we have written the whole argument for [preamp out into power amp in versus the effects loop](/blog/preamp-out-power-amp-in-vs-effects-loop-jacks), and covered [variable-wattage power amps](/blog/variable-power-amps-rivera-fryette) — the AMPX collapses that two-box rig into one box that weighs five pounds. Two speaker outputs. That is the actual value proposition, and it is a real one.

But run the math before you get excited. You already own the direct rig. The line out with Dynamic IR is a nice feature, and it is a feature your modeler already has, [with or without the cab block engaged](/blog/disable-cab-ir-for-voiced-frfr-preset-rebuild). What $2,000 buys you is the preamp and the power section, not the convenience. If you have never been bothered by [running FRFR](/blog/frfr-vs-guitar-cab-for-modelers), the AMPX is solving a problem you do not have.

The "dynamic IR" language is worth one sentence too, since IR terminology has gotten loose. A conventional impulse response is a static snapshot — one speaker, one mic, one level. A dynamic IR changes its response with level and load, which is meant to capture the way a speaker's behavior shifts as you drive it. Whether five of them at once is five times better than one is a question I will answer after I hear it in Mannheim and not before. We covered the general landscape of [IR loader pedals and direct rigs](/blog/ir-loader-pedals-direct-rig-two-notes-boss-ir2-mooer) if you want the baseline.

## How to Get the Blug Voicing on the Modeler You Already Own

This is the useful part. The AMPX ships with the AMP1 Mercury and Iridium tones, and BluGuitar publishes what those four channels are trying to do. Three of the four have a close relative already sitting in your Helix, free.

**Clean** — BluGuitar describes it as massive headroom off the 100-watt power section with treble reserve, built as a pedal platform. Start with **Placater Clean**, the clean side of the hot-rodded British family. If it reads too dark for you, **Matchstick** gets you the treble reserve at the cost of the British low end.

**Vintage** — "tight, mid-emphasized, woody, creamy midrange distortion, holds together under low tunings." That is **Brit Plexi Jump** with the drive backed off, or **Line 6 2204 Mod** if you want the tighter hot-rodded 2204 behavior. I have argued before that 2204 Mod is one of the [most underused blocks in the box](/blog/10-helix-amp-models-underrated) and this is exactly the gap it fills.

**Classic** — this is the interesting one, because BluGuitar says the channel's Tone control sweeps it from "hot-rodded British high gain" to "German wall of sound." Those are two different Helix blocks. **Placater Dirty** is the British end. **German Mahadeva** is the German end. There is no single model that sweeps between them, so pick your end and commit.

**Modern** — "extremely high gain, massive low cut, crystal-clear string separation." **German Mahadeva** or **Cali Rectifire**, and the operative words there are *massive low cut*.

Which brings me to the one setting that actually matters:

**That Tone knob on the AMP1 is not a tone control in the way you think.** Reading BluGuitar's own descriptions, what it is moving is the **low cut and the midrange center** — that is how you get from "British" to "German" on one knob, and it is how the Modern channel keeps string separation at that much gain. On a Helix, you replicate that with the amp block's **Low Cut parameter**, not the Bass knob. Set Bass to taste for body, then bring **Low Cut up from 80 Hz toward 120–150 Hz** on the high-gain voicings. That single move is most of the difference between a modeled high-gain amp that sounds like mush on a low B and one that sounds like the AMPX demos.

Rest of the starting point, on the Helix 0–10 amp scale, for the Classic/Placater Dirty side:

- **Drive 6–7.** The AMP1 family has gain on tap but it is a tight gain, not a saturated one.
- **Bass 4.5**, with **Low Cut at 110 Hz** doing the real work.
- **Mids 6.** These channels are mid-forward on purpose. That is the "woody" in BluGuitar's own copy.
- **Treble 5.5, Presence 5.** Presence is your power-section brightness and it is the closer analog to what a nanotube output stage contributes.
- **Master 7 or higher.** Same rule as always — the master on a modeled amp is your power-tube behavior, and leaving it at 5 is why your patch sounds like a preamp. Level-match afterward, [the way we lay out here](/blog/level-match-modeler-presets).

Full block-by-block reference is in the [Helix amp model cheat sheet](/blog/helix-amp-model-cheat-sheet).

What you will not get is the feel of a real power section loading into a real speaker. I say that in every one of these and I will keep saying it — we ran the [modeler versus tube amp comparison](/blog/modeler-vs-tube-amp-shootout) and the gap is narrower every year and it is still there, and it lives almost entirely in [power amp distortion rather than preamp distortion](/blog/preamp-vs-power-amp-distortion-how-to-hear). The AMPX's whole argument is that it is on the correct side of that gap in a box you can carry with one hand.

## The Appraiser's Read

At $1,999.99 with 50 units in the first batch, this is not a purchase decision for most people yet. It is a waitlist decision.

If you are a working player who already carries a modeler *and* a power amp to every gig, get in the queue. That is the rig the AMPX actually replaces, it replaces it at five pounds, and there is no other product doing precisely that with a real valve in the output stage.

If you own a Helix and play direct into the PA, sit this one out. You are not buying an amp, you are buying a power section you will not plug a cab into, and $2,000 is a lot for a preamp voicing you can approximate with Placater Dirty and a low cut this afternoon.

And if you are pre-ordering on the strength of a demo video: it has been four years, the spec sheet has already moved once, and the first batch is 50 units. Wait for Mannheim. The amp will still be there on the 28th, and so will the queue.
