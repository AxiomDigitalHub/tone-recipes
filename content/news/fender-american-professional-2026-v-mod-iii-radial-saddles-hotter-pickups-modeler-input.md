Fender announced the next generation of its **American Professional** series on **October 13**. Every model is built in Corona, California, and every one ships with a deluxe molded case.

First thing: it isn't called the American Professional III. Fender dropped the number, so this is just "American Professional" again, the same name the 2017 line had. That'll annoy anyone who buys and sells used guitars for a living. In ten years, someone will list a 2026 model as a 2017 and someone else will pay the wrong price. **Check the pickup covers and the saddles before you buy a used one.** I'll explain why below.

Guitar World points out that this pricing undercuts where the American Professional II had climbed to. I've watched Fender's US prices go one direction for most of my career, so a flat or lower price on a flagship is worth noting. Left-handed Strat, Tele, P and J models arrive in **spring 2027**.

## The 70-Year Gripe

Fender's Justin Norvell told Guitar World that the new Strat saddle fixes a gripe more than 70 years old. He's right about the age. On the bent-steel saddles Leo used on the '54 Strat, the height screws stick up past the top of the saddle once you lower the action. Put your picking hand on the bridge to palm-mute and you can feel them. Players have filed them down, swapped them for shorter screws, or bought aftermarket saddles for decades. My '62 has two that have been shortened by a previous owner, and it was a common job at the shop.

The new **ClassicGear Radial** bent-steel saddles keep the screw ends below the top surface. It's a small fix that's overdue.

The other hardware changes:

- **Telecaster and HotShot Tele:** a top-load/string-through bridge with **compensated brass bullet saddles**. Three-saddle Teles have always forced you to choose which string in each pair plays in tune, and compensated saddles are the standard fix. Brass also brightens the attack slightly compared with steel, which suits a Tele bridge pickup.
- **Jazzmaster and Jaguar:** a **ClassicGear J-Bridge** with a **Panorama tremolo**. Offset bridges are probably the most commonly replaced part on any Fender, and Norvell says Fender was trying to bring the feel of its Custom Shop offset bridge into production. One correction to some early coverage: according to Fender's own release, the redesigned **HiMass Modern** bridge goes on the **basses**, not the offsets.
- **Necks:** a **Deep "C"** on every six-string, rolled fingerboard edges throughout, and a newly sculpted heel on the Teles.

## The Pickups Are the Part That Changes Your Settings

Every guitar gets **V-Mod III** pickups, voiced per model. Two details from Fender's Steven Casado, via Guitar World, matter if you play through a modeler:

1. **More turns of wire.** The new pickups have a few more turns than the V-Mod II for extra output and what Fender calls a more modern tone.
2. **A new magnet stagger** on the Strat, Tele and Jazzmaster pickups. The pole heights now follow the fretboard radius more closely, and Fender says the result holds up better under heavy distortion and feels more balanced across chords.

Fender hasn't published DC resistance figures, so I won't guess at numbers. "More turns" points one way, though: **more output, a bit more midrange push, and a resonant peak that's slightly lower and less sparkly** than the V-Mod II. Our [DC resistance explainer](/blog/pickup-dc-resistance-what-it-actually-measures) covers why resistance alone won't tell you how much hotter a pickup sounds. On a modeler, hotter output shows up in three places.

**1. Your input pad and gain staging.** A modeler's input sees the pickup directly, with no amp's tolerance between them. If you set up a Helix or HX Stomp preset around a V-Mod II Strat, or any vintage-output Strat, the same preset will hit the amp block harder with these pickups. Clean presets that sat right at the edge will start to break up, and crunch presets will get compressed. Before you blame the preset, read [when you actually need the input pad](/blog/modeler-input-pad-when-you-need-it) and check your [input impedance setting](/blog/modeler-input-impedance-setting-what-to-set-it-to). Then [level-match](/blog/level-match-modeler-presets) your snapshots again, because a hotter guitar changes how far apart they are in volume.

**2. Amp-block drive, especially on clean Fender models.** The first thing an owner will notice through a Deluxe Reverb or Twin model is that the clean preset isn't as clean. Pull the **Drive** (or Volume, on non-master-volume models) back about half a number on Helix's 0–10 scale, then adjust by ear. Don't touch the Master first. On the blackface models, the gain you hear comes from the preamp, and that's where the extra pickup output lands.

**3. The stagger matters most under gain.** With the old vintage stagger on a 9.5" board, the B string often sits low and the G string jumps out, and gain makes the imbalance obvious. If Fender's radius-matched stagger works as described, these Strats should sound more even through a high-gain model than older Strats do. That's the claim worth checking in a store. Play a big open chord through a high-gain preset and listen for whether the G still pokes out. Pickup height still matters more than any stagger, so set it before you judge anything. Our [pickup height and magnet pull guide](/blog/pickup-height-magnet-pull-warble) has the measurements.

## Push-Push Tone on Every Guitar

Every six-string now has a **push-push tone control** that adds another pickup voice. Fender's product pages describe two of them:

- **Stratocaster:** adds the neck pickup in switch positions 1 and 2. You get bridge+neck and bridge+middle+neck, the two combinations a stock Strat can't do.
- **Telecaster:** puts the pickups **in series** in position 2. The result is louder and fatter than the stock parallel middle position, closer to a humbucker.

Fender didn't spell out the HSS, HotShot and offset functions in the materials I've seen. The Jazzmaster and Jaguar also keep the **Rhythm Circuit** and add a **tap on the bridge pickup**. I'll confirm those once the manuals are out.

**For modeler players, the Tele series setting needs care.** It's noticeably louder than position 2 in parallel, so treat it as a different guitar for gain staging. If you switch to it mid-song on a clean preset, the amp block gets hit with a level jump the same size as the one I described above, on top of the hotter pickups. Either build a snapshot for it or leave room in the amp's Drive. Bridge+neck on a Strat sounds closer to a Tele middle position, so it's worth trying on any [country Tele recipe](/blog/country-telecaster-tone-settings) if you play a Strat. For background on what each pickup combination does, see our [pickup position guide](/blog/pickup-position-guide).

## The HotShot Tele and the HSS

The **HotShot Telecaster** puts a V-Mod III Strat pickup in the middle position with 5-way switching. That gives you Strat in-between sounds with a Tele bridge pickup. It's the guitar I'd hand a player who covers a whole worship or wedding set on one instrument.

The **Stratocaster HSS** gets a redesigned bridge humbucker. According to Guitar World, the two coils use different pole pitches and wire gauges to balance the tone. Fender hasn't said what the push-push does on the new HSS. The American Professional II used it for a coil tap, and I'd expect something similar, but I haven't confirmed it. If you're deciding between HSS and SSS for a modern rig, our [single-coil vs. humbucker piece](/blog/single-coil-vs-humbucker-worship-ambient-tone) applies directly.

## Should You Trade In Your American Pro II?

Not for the pickups alone. A II with properly set pickup heights and a modeler set up for it will sound good for the rest of its life. The reasons to change are the hardware: the saddles, the brass Tele bridge, and especially the offset bridge if you've been planning to swap a Jazzmaster bridge anyway. A new Jazzmaster at $1,899.99 with the bridge already sorted compares well with a used II plus an aftermarket bridge and a tech's time.

If you're buying used in a few years, look at the saddles. Radial saddles with no screw tips showing mean it's the 2026 model, whatever the listing says.
