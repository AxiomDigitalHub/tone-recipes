# Sunday Setlist — November 8: two songs in 6/8, one rule for both

**For Sunday November 8, 2026** · draft written 2026-11-03

You got the setlist Tuesday. Here's what to do with it by Sunday. Five picks for November 8: four songs that have been in the top 20 for years, plus one that's sitting at #4 on this week's combined CCLI / PraiseCharts / Lifeway list. Two of the five are in 6/8, which is where most Sunday delay settings go wrong, so both of those notes use the same fix. The other three run from 71 to 98 BPM, which puts your dotted eighth anywhere from 459 to 634 ms, so plan on one tap-tempo per song this week. Same rig as always: an AC30-style amp (Essex A30 on Helix), a Klon-style boost that stays on, one delay, one plate.

## 1. Worthy Of It All — CeCe Winans

This week's riser: #4 on Renewing Worship's combined top 20 as of November 2. The Believe For It recording is in Eb at 71 BPM, so the dotted eighth is 634 ms. Keep it to 2 repeats with the mix around 18%, because at that length a third repeat lands on top of the next lyric. Play the verses on snapshot 5 (Clean Ambi) with volume swells, then move to snapshot 2 (Drive) when the band comes in. Save snapshot 6 (Ambi Drive) for the 'You deserve the glory' bridge, where the swell matters more than the gain.

## 2. How Great Is Our God — Chris Tomlin

A perennial staple, still #8 this week. The original Arriving recording is in Db at 76 BPM, so the dotted eighth is 592 ms. If your band plays the newer live version in C at 77, the dotted eighth is 584 ms, close enough that you can leave the preset alone. This is a strummed song, so the boost does the work: Klon-style at unity with the gain around 10 o'clock on snapshot 1 (Clean) for the verses, then snapshot 2 (Drive) for every chorus. Don't use the Lead snapshot here, because the hook belongs to the vocal.

- [Klon settings: clean boost to full drive](https://faderandknob.com/blog/klon-centaur-settings-guide)
- [Sunday-morning snapshot layout](https://faderandknob.com/blog/helix-worship-snapshots-sunday-morning)

## 3. This Is Amazing Grace — Phil Wickham

The fastest song in the set: Bb at 98 BPM on The Ascension, so the dotted eighth is 459 ms. Pull the plate decay down to about 2 s, because a long tail at 98 turns the driving eighths into a wash. The signature line in the intro and turnarounds is a lead part, so put it on snapshot 4 (Lead). Use snapshot 2 (Drive) under the verses and snapshot 3 (Drive+) for the 'worthy is the Lamb' bridge.

- [Phil Wickham guitar tone on Helix](https://faderandknob.com/blog/phil-wickham-guitar-tone-helix)

## 4. Great Are You Lord — All Sons & Daughters

6/8 song #1: key of A. MultiTracks lists it at 72 BPM and Integrity Music at 144, which is the eighth-note count. A dotted eighth (625 ms) fights the triple grouping, so use a straight eighth instead: 30000÷72 = 417 ms, mix at 12%. This is a Telecaster-into-AC30 song, so stay on snapshot 1 (Clean) for nearly the whole thing, keep the boost on, and let the amp's bloom carry the bridge. If 417 sounds off against your drummer, tap it in from the hi-hat. Don't trust either chart number.

- [Full recipe: Stu G's Great Are You Lord tone](https://faderandknob.com/recipe/garrard-great-are-you-lord)
- [Single coil vs. humbucker for worship](https://faderandknob.com/blog/single-coil-vs-humbucker-worship-ambient-tone)

## 5. Graves Into Gardens — Elevation Worship / Brandon Lake

6/8 song #2: key of B at 70 BPM per the MultiTracks original master. Use the same rule as Great Are You Lord: skip the dotted eighth and use 30000÷70 = 429 ms, close enough to 417 that one tap in rehearsal covers both songs. Play the verses on snapshot 5 (Clean Ambi), then snapshot 2 (Drive) for the chorus. Snapshot 3 (Drive+) is for the 'You turn graves into gardens' build, and that's the only place this set gets loud.
