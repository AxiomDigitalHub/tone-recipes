## 2. Positioning (restated for reuse)

**Category (internal):** the recipe layer of guitar tone. Competitors own *what to buy* (reviews) and *why it works* (theory). Fader & Knob owns **exactly how to set it up**. "Recipe layer," "coordinates not vibes," and every other phrase in this paragraph is INTERNAL shorthand. It never appears in first-touch copy (see the escalator test below).

**StoryBrand frame:** the hero is the guitarist who can hear the sound they want but cannot get it out of their own gear. F&K is the guide with a plan: find your song or sound, get the exact settings, adjust two knobs for your room. The villain is vague advice ("use your ears", "it's all in the fingers", folklore settings that were never right).

### The escalator test (rule for all first-touch copy)

Picture the customer riding an escalator past our window. Two seconds. They play guitar, they're smart, and they have never heard of Fader & Knob, "tone recipes," presets, or snapshots. First-touch copy (homepage hero, ads, social hooks, email subject lines, gift guides, directory listings) must pass all three:

1. **It names a feeling they already have**, in their own words, not a feature we ship.
2. **Zero native vocabulary.** No "tone recipe," "recipe," "preset," "snapshot," "signal chain," "IR," "platform-translated," or plan names. If a word requires having visited the site, it fails.
3. **It promises the outcome, not the mechanism.** They buy sounding like the record; how we do it comes later.

The vocabulary ladder: **first touch** = plain language only → **landing page** = introduce one native term and define it in the same sentence ("a tone recipe: the exact knob settings for a famous sound") → **product** = full native language. Insider vocabulary is earned, never assumed.

### One-liners that pass the test

- **Universal:** You bought the gear. It still doesn't sound like the record. We write down the exact settings that fix that.
- **Shortest form (hero / ad):** The exact settings for the sound you're chasing.
- **Gear owners (amps/pedals):** Every knob on that amp has a right answer. We tested them and wrote them down.
- **Modeler owners** ("modeler" is allowed here; this segment self-identifies by it): Stop scrolling 900 presets. Get the exact sound, dialed in, for your unit.
- **Worship guitarists:** The songs come Tuesday. Sound like the album by Sunday.
- **Gift buyers (don't play guitar):** They have the guitar. Give them the sound.
- **Post-gift (new gear owners):** That thing you just unwrapped has a hundred sounds inside. Here's where they are.
- **The experiment audience (founders/press, not customers):** An AI runs this business in public: research, writing, audits, and all. Every correction is published.

## 3. Message pillars

Four pillars. Every piece of outbound communication leads with exactly one.

**P1: Coordinates, not vibes.** Exact positions, verified values, tables not adjectives. Proof: corpus-validated .hlx presets, the public corrections counter, the accuracy gate. Use for: settings guides, comparisons, social proof of rigor.

**P2: Every platform, one recipe.** The same tone translated across Helix, Katana, Quad Cortex, TONEX, Fractal, Kemper, and a plain pedalboard. Proof: platform tabs on every recipe, downloadable presets in native formats. Use for: buying-guide traffic ("whatever you buy, we cover it"), platform-switch moments, firmware news.

**P3: Honestly AI, corrections in public.** Openly AI-operated: recipes are AI-researched and AI-generated, checked by automated audit pipelines, with every correction published. The human role is operator and accountability, not per-tone verification, and we never claim otherwise. Never fake community, never fake testing claims. Proof: /experiment log, day counter, 23+ public corrections, labeled personas, the weekly audit pipeline. Use for: LinkedIn/founder channel, Show HN, AI-directory listings, press. Never lead with this on gear content; players care about the tone, not the org chart.

**P4: From purchase to sound.** The gap between buying gear and sounding right is the product. Proof: recipe pages that start where the manual ends; post-purchase guides ("just got a Katana, now what"). Use for: seasonal gifting campaigns, buying guides, onboarding emails. This is the pillar that turns seasonal gear-buying moments into subscribers (see `SEASONAL_CAMPAIGN_CALENDAR.md`).

## 5. Voice (operational summary; full rules in COMMODITY_WRITING_CHECKLIST.md and the persona bibles)

Non-negotiables for every channel, human or automated:

1. Numbers over adjectives; positions over ranges. Any tone word ("warm") gets a qualifier (frequency, mechanism, or comparison). This is the #1 audit failure (299 hits).
2. Verdict first. No diplomatic non-answers; give the rule, then when to break it.
3. No throat-clearing, no "Conclusion" restating the post, no banned filler (delve, leverage, seamless, unlock, "when it comes to", "whether you're a beginner or a pro").
4. Personas are labeled AI. No fabricated testing, community, or numbers. Disclosed AI beats fake human, every time.
5. Accuracy outranks style. A wrong gain value is the one unrecoverable trust break.

Channel accents: blog = persona voices per the bibles; newsletter = fk-staff, plain and useful, one recipe one insight one link; LinkedIn = Daniel first person, plainspoken, checkable claims only; community (Reddit/TGP) = Nathan Cross rules, 90% help 10% mention, human-posted only; news = fast, sourced, no drama.
