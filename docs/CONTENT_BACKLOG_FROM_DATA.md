# Content Backlog — Built From What Actually Ranks

**Created:** 2026-06-14
**Source:** GA4 page report, Mar 16 – Jun 13 2026 (`Reports_snapshot (2).csv`).

## The pattern in the data
Sorted by **active users** (external discovery, not internal clicks), the winners are unambiguous:

| Page | Active users | Type |
|---|---|---|
| Best FRFR Speakers for Guitar Modelers in 2026 | 91 | Buying guide |
| Fender Deluxe Reverb Settings | 63 | Amp settings |
| Cab IR Library Roundup (Ownhammer vs York vs ML) | 27 | Comparison |
| The Edge's Delay Settings Decoded | 20 | Settings / how-to |
| Helix Floor vs LT vs Stomp vs Stadium | 13 | Comparison |
| Metallica Rhythm Tone Settings | 12 | Artist settings |
| Marshall Silver Jubilee vs JCM800 | 11 | Comparison |
| Silent Recording w/ Tube Amp (Captor X) | 11 | Technical how-to |
| Marshall ST20H vs SC20C | 11 | Comparison |
| Delay Types Compared | 10 | Comparison |
| Andy Timmons Lead Tone | 10 | Artist settings |

**Three formats win Google:** (1) **buying guides** ("best X for Y in 2026"), (2) **head-to-head comparisons** ("X vs Y"), (3) **settings/how-to** ("[gear] settings", "how to [outcome]").

**What underperforms in search:** the pure artist "tone recipe" pages. SRV's Pride & Joy shows 79 *views* but only 13 *active users* — most of that is internal browsing, not search entry. Most artist recipes pull 1–3 users. They're valuable as the internal-link web and as AI-citation surfaces, but they are **not** the Google front door. Stop treating recipe volume as the growth metric.

## Implication for the roadmap
Shift the new-content mix from "more artist recipes" toward the three winning formats, **anchored to gear you can rank for**. Keep the Non-Commodity Gate (AI_SEARCH_PLAYBOOK §6): every post needs first-hand settings/testing, not a SERP summary.

### High-priority backlog (comparison + buying-guide intent)
These mirror proven winners and target buyer/decision keywords. Verify none already exist before writing; if a near-match exists, **update + redate it** instead (playbook §6).

1. **"Best FRFR Speakers"** is your hit — build the cluster around it: best budget FRFR under $500, best powered vs passive FRFR, FRFR vs traditional cab follow-ups. (You have some; interlink them tightly into the winner.)
2. **Amp "settings" guides** for every canonical amp not yet covered — the Deluxe Reverb page proves the format. Twin Reverb, Bassman, AC15, Plexi variants, Mesa stuff.
3. **Modeler head-to-heads** — Helix vs QC vs Kemper ranks; extend: QC vs Tonex, Helix vs HX Stomp buying logic, FM9 vs QC, "best modeler under $X in 2026" (you have one — expand/refresh).
4. **IR / cab comparisons** — the IR roundup (27 users) shows appetite; add per-brand deep dives and "best free IRs" buyer content.
5. **Technical how-to with buying angle** — the Captor X silent-recording piece (11 users) is a template: reamping, load boxes, DI comparisons.

### Keep but reframe
- **Artist recipes** → keep producing, but title and structure them around *settings* and *gear* ("[Artist] [Song] settings + the gear that gets there") so they have a shot at the settings-intent traffic that actually converts, and link them up into the relevant pillar + comparison posts.

## Internal-linking action (cheap, high-leverage)
Your recipe pages get internal traffic; your gear/comparison pages get Google. Push authority from the winners to the rest:
- Every comparison/settings post should link to 2–3 relevant artist recipes ("hear it in action").
- Every artist recipe should link up to its amp-settings pillar and any relevant comparison.
- The 8 guide pillars should each link to their top-performing leaf posts (and vice versa).

## Re-measure
Re-pull GA4 in 4 weeks. Success = the gear/comparison/settings cluster growing its share of Google organic, and 2–3 *new* posts entering the top-20-by-active-users list.
