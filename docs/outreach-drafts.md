## B. Community answers (Reddit / TheGearPage / Gearspace / Helix FB groups)

### B1 — "How do I get the ambient / Bethel-style worship tone on Helix?"
> The core of that pad-forward Bethel/Jesus-Culture sound is less about gain and more about layering space on a clean amp. Start with a clean AC30 (Essex A30 TB), keep the drive low. Then three things make it: (1) an octave-up shimmer — a POG-style pitch block at ~15% mix, or a reverb with a shimmer mode; (2) two delays — a darker analog one (~300–400ms) under a dotted-eighth digital one; (3) a big reverb (Searchlights or Glitz), mix higher than feels right in headphones (~30–40%) because this *is* the pad. The real trick is volume swells — use the volume block / auto-swell so chords fade in with no pick attack; that's what turns the guitar into a synth-like bed. Dotted-eighth time = (60000 ÷ BPM) × 0.75, so ~375ms at 120.
>
> I wrote up the full block-by-block version with snapshots here if it helps: faderandknob.com/blog/bethel-music-guitar-tone-helix

### B2 — "Trying to nail the Lincoln Brewster lead tone on Helix — any tips?"
> Brewster's the outlier — he's Strat-into-Marshall, not the usual Gretsch-into-Vox worship thing, so the recipe's different. Use a Plexi Variac (Brit Plexi family), but here's the counterintuitive part: keep Drive *below half*, mids and treble up, bass low. The gain isn't from the amp. The lead lift comes from a **mid-boost, not more distortion** — stack a low-gain Klon-style drive (Minotaur) always-on, then a Tube Screamer (Scream 808) just for leads with the level high and gain near zero, plus a little EQ bump around 1kHz. Single-coils are non-negotiable; humbuckers turn it muddy. Delay stays controlled (dotted-eighth, modest mix) — it's articulate, not ambient.
>
> Full settings + snapshot layout here if you want the exact numbers: faderandknob.com/blog/lincoln-brewster-tone-helix

### B3 — "Best Helix settings for the Hillsong / Nigel Hendroff sound?"
> The Hillsong foundation is a chimey guitar into a *semi-clean* low-wattage Vox (Hendroff's cornerstone is an AC15 — Essex A15 TB on the Helix, AC30 close behind). Key: keep the amp on the edge of breakup and let a pedal push it, not the amp's gain. His two always-on blocks are the secret — a low-gain boost (his is a Jackson Audio Prism; a Minotaur stands in) and a light compressor, on across every snapshot. Then a dotted-eighth tape delay for the rhythmic picking and a big shimmer reverb. Dynamics do the rest — the volume knob and pick attack take you from whisper-clean verse to full chorus. (Side note: the old "Klon always-on" thing people repeat is a myth — his always-on is the Prism boost.)
>
> Block-by-block walkthrough with the snapshot setup: faderandknob.com/blog/hillsong-guitar-tone-helix

### B4 — Short / low-effort answer (when a long post would be overkill)
> Dotted-eighth delay is the whole thing — time = (60000 ÷ BPM) × 0.75 (≈375ms at 120), feedback ~30%, mix ~20%, tempo-synced. Pair it with a transparent boost into a Vox-style amp on edge-of-breakup and a plate/hall reverb and you're 90% there. I keep exact worship presets (Hillsong/Bethel/Brewster etc.) at faderandknob.com if you want a starting point.
