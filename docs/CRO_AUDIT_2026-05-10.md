## Headline

The site has one severe CRO problem and one suspicious signal that needs investigation.

**Severe:** 24.44% of sessions experienced dead clicks (11 of 45). For context, healthy sites run 2-5%. You're 5 to 10x over. **Rage clicks are 0% and JavaScript errors are 0%, so this is not "the site is broken." This is "things look clickable but aren't."**

**Suspicious:** Four consecutive UK Edge desktop users hit `/recipe/knopfler-sultans-of-swing-clean` within a six-minute window on May 8 morning (06:50 to 06:56), all bounced in 12 to 20 seconds, all with one dead click. That pattern means a specific share or citation drove a small burst of traffic to that page and every one of them bounced because something on the page didn't work as expected.

## Where the dead clicks happen

I pulled all 11 dead-click sessions. The distribution:

**4 sessions on `/recipe/knopfler-sultans-of-swing-clean`** (UK, Edge PC, May 8 06:50 to 06:56, 12 to 20 second sessions, 1 click each, 1 page each, all single-page bounces). Same browser, same country, six minutes apart. This is a single share or citation source, almost certainly Bing or a Bing-cited surface, and the page is failing every one of those visitors.

**3 sessions on `/browse` with platform/era filters** (`?era=1990&platform=helix`, `?platform=helix&sort=son...`, etc.). Users are using your filter and sort UI; the dead clicks suggest some filter combinations either don't update results, return empty states, or the sort dropdown doesn't re-fire when re-clicked.

**2 sessions on blog posts** (`/blog/best-frfr-speakers-for-modelers`, `/blog/fender-deluxe-reverb-settings`). These were long sessions (42:57 and 34:19 respectively); the dead click was incidental, not the main story.

**1 session on `/recipe/edge-where-the-streets-have-no-name`** (UK, Edge PC, 1:33 duration, 3 clicks, 1 page).

**1 session: harder to read from the entry/exit truncation.**

The dominant pattern is `/recipe/knopfler-sultans-of-swing-clean` plus `/browse` with filters. Together those are 7 of 11 dead-click sessions, or 64% of the problem.

## Hypotheses for the dead clicks

I did not watch every recording end to end, but based on the page structures and user behavior pattern, these are the candidates ranked by likelihood:

### Hypothesis 1 (highest confidence): Signal-chain block icons look clickable but only show tooltips

The recipe page renders the signal chain as a row of block icons (Volume Pedal, Comp, Drive, Amp, Cab-Mic, Verb, EQ). Hovering shows a tooltip with the model name. Clicking on a block does what? If the answer is "nothing, or it shows a tooltip popup that appears on hover anyway," that's exactly the dead-click profile: cursor over a visually rich element that looks interactive, click, no navigation or expansion happens.

This explains why four users from the same source all dead-clicked: they all clicked the same kind of element (the signal chain block) the same way.

**Test:** open `/recipe/knopfler-sultans-of-swing-clean` and click each block icon. Does anything happen? If no, that's almost certainly your problem. The blocks should at minimum scroll the page to the corresponding "Per-block settings" section and highlight that block's panel, or expand inline. Cursor should be `pointer` only if something actually happens.

### Hypothesis 2: The `/browse` sort dropdown re-clicks don't fire

`Artist A-Z` got 5 clicks (25% of all browse-page clicks) and `Song A-Z` got 3 (15%). On a 21-pageview sample, that's 8 sort clicks across 12 distinct elements. Some of that volume is plausible "the user actually wanted to sort." Some of it is users clicking the same sort option twice expecting something to happen the second time, which fits the dead-click signature on the filter sessions.

**Test:** load `/browse`, click Artist A-Z, observe the order change. Click Artist A-Z a second time. Does it toggle to Z-A? If not, the second click is a dead click. Good UX is to either toggle, do nothing visibly with no cursor signal, or at minimum not register as clickable on the second press.

### Hypothesis 3: Free-tier preset download buttons silently no-op

The recipe page has `Download .hlx ↓` and `Settings PDF ↓` buttons. If a free-tier user clicks Download .hlx and the auth gate kicks in but doesn't surface a clear "sign in to download" modal, that registers as a dead click. The CTA looks like a button, behaves like a button on click, but produces no visible result.

**Test:** sign out, load any recipe, click Download .hlx. Does anything happen? Modal? Redirect to signup? Toast? Or silence?

### Hypothesis 4 (lower confidence): Mobile-only tap target issue

The 39-click EdgeMobile session from Bing referrer hit a dead click somewhere across 37 pages. On mobile, tap targets under 44 by 44 pixels are a recurring cause of dead clicks, especially on dense filter/sort UI.

**Test:** Lighthouse on `/browse` from mobile, look at "Tap targets are sized appropriately."

## What's working

Worth saying explicitly because it changes how aggressively to attack the dead clicks:

- **0% rage clicks** means users aren't frustrated to the point of spam-clicking. Whatever's broken, it's broken quietly.
- **0 JavaScript errors.** No exceptions firing in the console; the app is technically healthy.
- **2.31 pages per session and 51% scroll depth** mean the people who don't bounce are actually exploring.
- **Smart event "Download" fired in 4 sessions.** The download path works for someone, just not consistently.
- **One 34-minute, 37-page mobile session** demonstrates the site can hold deep attention even on a phone.

## The funnel is not set up

In Clarity's Funnels widget, there are zero configured funnels. This is the highest-leverage 30-minute task on this list. With Stripe live mode coming, you need to be able to see:

1. **Recipe page → Download .hlx → Signup → Checkout → Success** (the conversion path you actually care about)
2. **Pricing page → Signup → Checkout → Success**
3. **Blog post → Recipe page → Download** (the SEO entry to product path)
4. **Browse → Recipe page → Download**

Without these funnels you can't measure whether any of the changes below actually moved revenue.

One in three users abandon `/browse` between 10% and 15% scroll depth. That's right after the first row of recipe cards renders. They see one row, the page above the fold loaded with whatever record (in the screenshot it was Black Sabbath Paranoid), and they leave.

This is consistent with the dead-click theory: if the first thing they try to interact with (the album art, the "See the chain" link, or the platform filter) doesn't behave as expected, they bounce.

The fix is upstream of the scroll number. Once the dead clicks are resolved, this number should improve on its own.

## Prioritized fix list

Ordered by impact divided by effort.

**1. Audit clickable elements on `/recipe/[slug]` and `/browse`.** Open Chrome DevTools, hover every element, find every `cursor: pointer` and verify it does something visible on click. Pay special attention to: signal-chain block icons, filter chips, sort dropdown options, album art images, platform tab pills, the "See the chain →" link, the Download buttons. Effort: 1 to 2 hours. Impact: should cut dead-click rate from 24% to under 8% based on the cluster pattern.

**2. Fix the Knopfler Sultans of Swing recipe specifically.** That page is getting traffic from a cluster source (Bing or a citation) and bouncing every visitor. Whatever's broken there is probably broken on every recipe page; that page is just the canary. Effort: covered by item 1, but verify on Knopfler explicitly. Impact: high, because you have an active traffic source pointed at it.

**3. Configure four Clarity funnels.** Recipe → Download → Signup → Checkout, Pricing → Checkout, Blog → Recipe → Download, Browse → Recipe → Download. Effort: 30 minutes. Impact: you finally can measure whether any of this works.

**4. Add an authentication-aware Download button state.** If a logged-out user clicks Download .hlx, show a one-step modal ("Sign in with Google to download. Free downloads remaining: 10 of 10.") rather than silently doing nothing. This is also a sign-up driver, not just a dead-click fix. Effort: 2 to 3 hours including the modal. Impact: reduces dead clicks AND increases sign-ups.

**5. Make the signal-chain blocks legitimately interactive.** Click on a block scrolls/highlights its detailed settings section below. This is what users are clearly trying to do. Effort: half a day. Impact: turns dead clicks into a legitimate engagement signal.

**6. LCP improvement.** 3.3s LCP is hurting Core Web Vitals and likely hurting SEO ranking on mobile. Look at what loads above the fold on `/browse` and `/recipe/[slug]`. Likely culprits: album art images not lazy-deferred correctly, font swap delay, large signal-chain SVG. Effort: 1 day with Lighthouse in hand. Impact: SEO + bounce rate.

**7. Mobile tap-target audit on `/browse` filter chips.** Run Lighthouse mobile, fix anything under 44x44. Effort: 1-2 hours. Impact: helps the 18% of traffic on mobile, especially the EdgeMobile-from-Bing segment.
